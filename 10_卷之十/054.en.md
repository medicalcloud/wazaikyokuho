# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 054 観音散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Guanyin san (Guanyin Powder)

It treats children who have contracted wind-cold externally and damaged the spleen and stomach internally, with vomiting counterflow, vomiting and diarrhoea, inability to nurse or eat, and, over time, gradual emaciation and weakness. In general, when the spleen is deficient there is diarrhoea; when the stomach is deficient there is vomiting; when both spleen and stomach are deficient, vomiting and diarrhoea do not stop. This medicine greatly warms and nourishes the spleen and stomach and makes eating relishable. (The formula of Quanxie guanyin san appears later.)

renshen (ginseng; 1 liang)　fuling (poria; 1½ *qian*)　shenqu (medicated leaven; stir-fried; 2 *qian*)　shilian rou (lotus fruit flesh; stir-fried, plumule removed; 1 *fen*)　mianqi (cotton astragalus)　baizhi (angelica dahurica)　muxiang (costus root; blast-fried)　bai biandou (hyacinth bean; skins removed, roasted to a scorched yellow, fire toxin removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried; 1 *qian* each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 1 *qian* with a small *zhan* of water, one jujube and three agastache leaves, decoct down to four-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm, adjusted for the child's size.

## Notes

Guanyin san (Chunyou-era additions): for vomiting, diarrhoea, poor feeding and wasting when wind-cold is contracted externally and the spleen and stomach damaged internally in children. It explains: spleen deficiency gives diarrhoea, stomach deficiency gives vomiting, and deficiency of both gives persistent vomiting and diarrhoea.
- Ginseng, poria, astragalus, hyacinth bean, lotus fruit and licorice supplement the spleen and stomach; medicated leaven aids digestion; costus, angelica dahurica and agastache are added. Quanxie guanyin san appears later.
