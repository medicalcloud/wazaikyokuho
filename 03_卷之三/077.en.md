# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 077 順気術香散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shunqi zhuxiang san (Qi-Smoothing Atractylodes Fragrance Powder)

It treats qi failing to rise and descend, with vomiting counterflow and nausea, stuffiness and oppression of the chest and diaphragm, distension and fullness of the flanks and ribs; and damage by wine and food with belching and acid swallowing, stabbing pain of the heart and spleen, irregular bowels, a yellow face and wasted flesh and no desire for food. It also treats women's stabbing blood-qi pain and all cold qi.

dingxiangpi (clove bark; not exposed to fire)　suoshaner (amomum)　liangjiang (galangal; rhizome head removed, stir-fried)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, roasted with ginger juice)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked in rice-washing water)　jiegeng (kikyo; platycodon; rhizome head removed)　huixiang (fennel; stir-fried; 3 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, three slices of ginger and two jujubes, decoct down to eight-tenths and take it fairly hot, at any time; or stir it with a little salt into boiling water. Taken regularly it loosens the middle, smooths qi, harmonizes the stomach and improves eating.

## Notes

Shunqi zhuxiang san (Wu Zhige's additions): for vomiting, chest stuffiness and flank distension from disordered rising and descending of qi, for acid reflux, stabbing heart and spleen pain, irregular bowels, sallow complexion and poor appetite after damage by wine and food, for women's blood-qi pain and all cold qi.
- Pingwei san (Heiisan) (black atractylodes, magnolia bark, tangerine peel, licorice) plus clove bark, amomum, galangal, cinnamon, dried ginger and fennel to warm, and platycodon to raise qi.
- The "zhu" (atractylodes) in the name may be related to the variant names "Muxiang san (Mokkosan)" or "Maoxiang san" found in other editions (cf. "Shuxiang san" in juan 9, no. 051), but this has not been verified.
