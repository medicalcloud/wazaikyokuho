# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 098 人参丁香散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Renshen dingxiang san (Ninjinchokosan; Ginseng and Clove Powder)

It treats incessant vomiting in adults and children so that gruel, drinks and medicinal decoctions cannot be kept down. All vomiting illness arises because the sanjiao is disordered, the spleen and stomach are weak, cold and heat lose harmony, pathogen and healthy qi interfere with each other, clear and turbid are not separated, yin and yang are thrown into confusion, and lodged phlegm and retained fluid cannot be transported and transformed: there is stuffiness and fullness of the chest and diaphragm, vomiting counterflow and nausea, distending pain of the abdomen and flanks, shortness of breath with choking oppression, coughing and vomiting of phlegm and water, belching of sourness and acid swallowing, no desire for food, and gradual wasting. It also treats women's morning sickness in pregnancy, with vexation and confusion in the heart, dizziness and heaviness of the head and eyes, aversion to the smell of food, vomiting with vexation and oppression, tossing and turning without rest, limp, weak limbs unable to support oneself, and much lying down and little getting up. It also treats emaciation from chronic illness with extreme deficiency of the spleen and stomach, fullness of the middle with vomiting and complete inability to take food.

bai shaoyao (shakuyaku; white peony root; half a jin)　danggui (toki; Chinese angelica; rhizome head removed)　dingxiang (clove)　dingpi (clove bark)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　peng'ezhu (zedoary)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; 2 liang each)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed)　xiangfu (cyperus; stir-fried)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; stir-fried)　shanyao (Chinese yam; 4 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 5 *qian* with one *zhan* of water and three slices of fresh ginger, decoct together down to seven-tenths and take it warm on an empty stomach before meals. A child of two may take half a *qian* with half a *zhan* of water and one slice of ginger decocted down to four-tenths or less, taken warm; adjust further according to age. Taken regularly it harmonizes the spleen and stomach and improves eating.

## Notes

Renshen dingxiang san (bureau additions): for incessant vomiting in adults and children, morning sickness, and vomiting with inability to eat from extreme spleen-stomach deficiency in chronic illness; it explains vomiting as due to disorder of the sanjiao, spleen-stomach weakness and retained phlegm-fluid retention.
- Ginseng, atractylodes, poria, Chinese yam and licorice supplement the spleen; clove, clove bark, cinnamon and dried ginger warm; white peony and angelica harmonize the blood; cyperus and zedoary move qi.
