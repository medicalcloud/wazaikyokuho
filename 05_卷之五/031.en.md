# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 031 青娥丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qing'e wan (Young Maiden Pill)

It can treat all lumbar pain: deficient kidney qi taken advantage of by wind and cold, or blood and qi contending, with pain in the lower back as if broken, difficulty rising and sitting, inability to bend forward and back freely or to turn over; or excessive toil injuring the kidney channel; or living in low damp places so that earth qi injures the lower back; or injury from falls; or wind-cold lodging and contending; or qi stagnating without dispersing—all these cause lumbar pain; or a feeling as if a heavy object hung in the lower back, with difficulty rising and sitting. (Another formula appears later.)

hutao (walnuts; skins and membranes removed; 20)　suan (garlic; simmered into a paste; 4 liang)　poguzhi (psoralea; soaked in wine, stir-fried; 8 liang)　duzhong (eucommia; bark removed, soaked in ginger juice, stir-fried; 16 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills with the garlic paste. Take thirty pills each time on an empty stomach with warm wine; women take them with weak vinegar decoction. Taken regularly it strengthens the sinews and bones, quickens the blood, blackens the beard and moustache and improves the complexion.

## Notes

Qing'e wan (Baoqing-era additions): the representative formula for lumbar pain from kidney-qi deficiency combined with wind-cold, blood-qi contention, overwork, damp living, falls, wind-cold or qi stagnation (pain as if broken, difficulty rising, heaviness).
- Eucommia, psoralea and walnut supplement the kidneys and strengthen the lower back; made into pills with garlic paste. Another formula of the same name appears later.
