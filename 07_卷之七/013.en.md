# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之七 013 流気飲 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Liuqi yin (Qi-Flowing Beverage)

It is suitable for insufficiency of the liver channel with wind-heat received internally attacking the eyes upward, dim, dark eyes with unclear vision, constant sight of black flowers, much tearing in the wind, fear of the sun and aversion to light, accumulated discharge with red swelling, gritty roughness making the eyes hard to open, or the formation of obstructions and nebulae, inverted and curled eyelashes, dizzy eyes with red festering; women's blood-wind eyes; and seasonal sudden red, swollen eyes with purple-black lids; in short, any eye disease.

dahuang (rhubarb; blast-fried)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　juhua (chrysanthemum flower; twigs removed)　niubangzi (arctium seed; stir-fried)　xixin (asarum; shoots removed)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; shoots removed)　shan[zhi] (gardenia; skin removed)　bai jili (tribulus; stir-fried, spines removed)　huangqin (scutellaria; rhizome head removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　xuanshen (scrophularia; rhizome head removed)　manjingzi (vitex fruit; white skin removed)　jingjie (schizonepeta; stalks removed)　muzei (scouring rush; roots and nodes removed; 1 liang each)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked overnight in rice-washing water, stir-fried and drained; 2 liang)　cao jueming (cassia seed; 1½ liang)

Pound and sift the above into a powder. Take 2½ *qian* each time at bedtime mixed in cold wine. If an infant has the disease, simply have the wet nurse take it.

## Notes

Liuqi yin (from here the layer of Wu Zhige's additions): for dim vision, floaters, tearing, photophobia, discharge, red swelling, difficulty opening the eyes, obstructions, inverted lashes and festering lids from an insufficient liver channel with wind-heat; for women's blood-wind eyes; for epidemic sudden red, swollen eyes; and for any eye disease. For infants, the wet nurse takes it.
- Cassia seed, chrysanthemum, vitex, scouring rush and tribulus brighten the eyes; schizonepeta, saposhnikovia, asarum, lovage and arctium disperse wind; rhubarb, scutellaria, gardenia and scrophularia clear and purge heat; black atractylodes and licorice are added.
- "Shan (skin removed)" lacks a character in the base text; following the Executive Yuan edition it is read as shanzhi (gardenia) and [zhi] is supplied (see the variant notes).
