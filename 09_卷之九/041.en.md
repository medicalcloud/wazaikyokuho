# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 041 琥珀沢蘭煎 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Hupo zelan jian (Amber and Lycopus Syrup)

It treats the thirty-eight kinds of blood-qi disorders in women, the eight winds and five impediments, the seven conglomerations and eight aggregations, stabbing pain of the heart and abdomen, paralysis from wind-strike, aching hands and feet, bound static blood in the breasts, a stirring foetus in pregnancy, a dead foetus that will not come out, retained placenta, failed blood gathering at the heart, dizziness and blurred vision, blood pouring into the limbs, puffy swelling of the whole body, long-standing pain of the *chong* and *ren*, and cessation of births with no heir; it is taken morning and evening. It is also used where, after childbearing, the menses are irregular, with red and white vaginal discharge, nausea and vomiting counterflow, and an emaciated, weary body. When pregnant and in the last month, one dose a day makes the foetus slippery and delivery easy.

zi baji (purple morinda; core removed, stir-fried with glutinous rice)　huixiang (fennel; stir-fried)　mudanpi (moutan bark; core removed)　liujinu cao (artemisia anomala; twigs removed)　wuweizi (schisandra; stalks removed)　baizhi (angelica dahurica)　wujiapi (acanthopanax root bark; core removed)　jinchai shihu (golden-hairpin dendrobium; roots removed, chopped, soaked in wine, stir-fried)　zelan ye (lycopus leaf; stalks removed)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　chi shaoyao (red peony root)　sheng gan dihuang (dried raw rehmannia; washed, rhizome head removed)　chuan danggui (Sichuan angelica; soaked in wine overnight)　renshen (ginseng; rhizome head removed)　bai shaoyao (white peony root)　shu gan dihuang (prepared rehmannia; washed free of earth)　aiye (mugwort leaf; stir-fried with vinegar, kneaded into cakes with glutinous rice paste, dried over a fire and powdered)　fuzi (aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　baizhu (white atractylodes; 1 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of a pellet with refined honey. Take one pill each time ground into warm wine. For a leaking foetus with stabbing pain, take it with boiled glutinous rice water. For alternating chills and fever with vexing pain of the limbs, take it with decocted sweet wormwood wine. For blocked menses in women and unmarried girls, take it with decocted safflower wine. For blood dizziness with loss of consciousness, take it with a boy's urine mixed with warm wine. To hasten delivery, take it with egg white mixed in wine. For blood-qi disorders and blood lumps attacking and stabbing the heart and abdomen, take it with wine in which a heated steelyard weight has been quenched. For cold damage and wind-strike with clenched jaw, take it with decocted Mahuang tang and cover with a quilt to induce sweating; it heals at once. For fright palpitations and headache, take it with mint wine. For cough, take it with decocted mulberry root bark decoction. For blood wind attacking and pouring in, with itching of the whole body and numbness of the head and face, take it with wine in which stir-fried black soybeans have been steeped. Taken regularly before and after childbirth, it prevents the various illnesses, with divine effect.

## Notes

Hupo zelan jian (Baoqing-era additions): for the thirty-eight blood-qi disorders of women, the eight winds and five impediments, conglomerations, stabbing abdominal pain, paralysis, limb pain, breast stasis, a restless foetus, dead foetus, retained placenta, failed blood surging to the heart, dizziness, oedema, pain of the *chong* and *ren* and infertility; also for irregular menses, discharge, nausea and wasting; taken in the last month for easy delivery. The vehicle varies with the condition.
- Lycopus, artemisia anomala, moutan and red peony move blood; Siwu tang (angelica, lovage, peony, rehmannia), ginseng, atractylodes, dendrobium, morinda, acanthopanax and schisandra supplement; aconite, fennel and mugwort warm; angelica dahurica is added. Although named after amber, the formula in the base text contains none.
