# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 094 五皮散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wupi san (Five Peels Powder)

It treats men and women with stagnant spleen qi and wind-damp lodging and contending, the spleen channel receiving damp and qi not flowing, so that the head and face are puffy, the four limbs swollen and full, the heart and abdomen distended, qi rising urgently, the abdomen and flanks like a drum and the area around the navel distended and oppressed, interfering with eating; [the swelling] attacks upward and pours downward, coming and going without fixed place, with panting fatigue on movement.

wujiapi (acanthopanax bark)　digupi (lycium root bark)　shengjiangpi (ginger peel)　dafupi (areca husk)　fulingpi (poria skin; equal parts)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 3 *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, decoct down to eight-tenths, remove the dregs and take it fairly hot, at any time. Strictly avoid raw, cold, greasy and hard foods.

## Notes

Wupi san (bureau additions): an oedema formula for swelling of the face and limbs, abdominal distension and panting from stagnant spleen qi and wind-damp.
- Five peels: acanthopanax, lycium, ginger, areca husk and poria (hence the name); partly different from the later Wupi yin (which uses tangerine peel and mulberry bark).
