# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 073 換腿丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huantui wan (Leg-Changing Pill)

It treats deficiency of the three foot yin channels invaded in turn by wind, cold, summerheat and damp, with cramping, numbness, slackness and weakness, attacking the chest, flanks, shoulders and back above and pouring into the legs and knees below with pain, gradually turning into wind-damp leg qi, with difficulty walking, soles like fire, rising qi with rapid panting, and no desire to eat.

yiyiren (coix seed; stir-fried)　shinanye (photinia leaf)　shihu (dendrobium; sprouts removed, soaked in wine)　bixie (dioscorea hypoglauca; lightly roasted)　chuan niuxi (Sichuan achyranthes; sprouts removed, soaked in wine)　tiannanxing (arisaema; blast-fried)　qianghuo (notopterygium; rhizome head removed)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; rhizome head and forks removed)　huangqi (astragalus; rhizome head removed, roasted with honey)　danggui (Chinese angelica; sprouts removed, soaked in wine)　tianma (gastrodia; sprouts removed)　xuduan (dipsacus; 1½ liang each)　binglang (areca; 2½ liang)　mugua (chaenomeles; 4 liang)

Powder the above and form pills the size of wutong seeds with flour paste boiled in wine. Take fifty pills each time with warm wine or salt water as preferred.

## Notes

Huantui wan (Wu Zhige's additions): for leg qi (numbness, slackness, leg pain, burning soles, panting) from wind, cold, summerheat and damp invading deficient foot yin channels.
- Chaenomeles, coix, dioscorea, photinia and areca remove damp; achyranthes, dipsacus and dendrobium strengthen the legs; notopterygium, saposhnikovia, gastrodia and arisaema dispel wind; astragalus and angelica supplement qi and blood. The name means the legs feel as if "changed" for new ones.
