# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 060 大香連丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Da xianglian wan (Major Costus and Coptis Pill)

It is suitable for men and women with weak, deficient intestines and stomach and disharmony of cold and heat, diarrhoea with vexing thirst, undigested rice and grain, abdominal distension and borborygmus, glomus and oppression of the chest and diaphragm, and distension and fullness of the flanks and ribs; or dysentery with pus and blood, tenesmus, very frequent getting up at night and no desire for food; or inhibited urination, weary limbs and gradual emaciation and weakness.

huanglian (coptis; rhizome head and fibrous roots removed; 20 liang, stir-fried red together with 10 liang of evodia, the evodia discarded)　muxiang (costus root; not exposed to fire; 4 liang 8 *qian* 8 *fen*)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with vinegar paste. Take twenty pills each time, swallowed with rice water.

## Notes

Da xianglian wan (Wu Zhige's additions): for diarrhoea, thirst, indigestion, abdominal distension, borborygmus, chest glomus and flank distension from weak intestines and stomach with disharmony of cold and heat; for dysentery with pus and blood, tenesmus, frequent stools at night and poor appetite; and for inhibited urination, lassitude and wasting. Still used today as Xianglian wan for damp-heat diarrhoea and dysentery.
- Two ingredients: coptis stir-fried with evodia (the evodia discarded) and costus root.
