# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 063 十神湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shishen tang (Jusshinto; Ten Spirits Decoction)

It treats unseasonable weather in which pestilence spreads recklessly and many people fall ill. Regardless of whether yin and yang are both affected, and also for wind-cold-damp impediment, this medicine may be taken.

chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried)　mahuang (mao; ephedra; roots and nodes removed)　shengma (shoma; cimicifuga; 4 liang each)　gange (dried pueraria; 14 liang)　chi shaoyao (shakuyaku; red peony root)　baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; pulp removed)　zisu (perilla; coarse stalks removed)　xiangfuzi (cyperus; pounded, hairs removed; 4 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take three large *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water and five slices of fresh ginger, decoct down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it hot, at any time. For fever and headache add three scallion whites with roots. For fullness of the middle with qi excess, add a few slices of bitter orange and decoct together. Even parturient women, infants and the elderly may take it. In cold damage when exterior and interior patterns cannot be distinguished, guiding the meridians with this prevents changes, and its effect is not slight.

## Notes

Shishen tang (bureau additions): for epidemic colds due to unseasonable weather (pestilence), and for wind-cold-damp impediment.
- Mainly pueraria, with ephedra, cimicifuga, angelica dahurica, perilla and lovage to release the exterior, cyperus and tangerine peel to smooth qi, and red peony and licorice to harmonize; described as gentle enough for parturient women, infants and the elderly.
