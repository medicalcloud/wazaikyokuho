# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 020 膠艾湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jiao'ai tang (Gelatin and Mugwort Decoction)

It treats taxation damage of blood and qi with deficient, damaged *chong* and *ren*, excessive menses, dribbling leakage unceasing for days, and pain of the navel and abdomen; improper care in pregnancy with a restless foetus, abdominal pain and a sense of dragging down; or taxation damaging the uterine network vessels with "uterine obstruction" (bleeding in pregnancy), lumbar pain, oppression and confusion; or injury and disturbance causing the foetus to rise and surge against the heart with rushing and shortness of breath; and, from childbirth and nursing, deficient qi of the *chong* and *ren* unable to restrain, so that menstrual blood dribbles unceasingly, dragging on for days and months with gradual emaciation.

ejiao (donkey-hide gelatin; broken up, stir-fried dry)　xiongqiong (Sichuan lovage)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried; 2 liang each)　danggui (Chinese angelica)　aiye (mugwort leaf; lightly stir-fried; 3 liang each)　bai shaoyao (white peony root)　shu gan dihuang (prepared rehmannia; 4 liang each)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 3 *qian* with one *zhan* of water and six-tenths of wine, decoct down to eight-tenths, strain off the dregs and take it fairly hot, on an empty stomach before meals, three times a day; in severe cases continue through the night.

## Notes

Jiao'ai tang: for excessive menses, prolonged spotting and umbilical pain from taxation damage; for a restless foetus, abdominal pain and dragging sensation in pregnancy; for bleeding in pregnancy with lumbar pain and oppression; for the foetus surging upward with breathlessness; and for prolonged bleeding and wasting from deficient *chong* and *ren* after childbirth. Still used today as Xiong gui jiao'ai tang for abnormal uterine bleeding and threatened miscarriage.
- Siwu tang (angelica, lovage, white peony, prepared rehmannia) plus donkey-hide gelatin, mugwort and licorice—the same composition as the *Jingui yaolüe* Xiong gui jiao'ai tang—decocted in water and wine.
