# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 020 香連丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Xianglian wan (Costus and Coptis Pill)

It treats children with disharmony of cold and heat, diarrhea with vexing thirst, undigested rice and grain, abdominal pain and borborygmus; or dysentery with pus and blood, tenesmus, very frequent getting up at night, no desire for milk or food, wasting flesh, gradually turning into gan.

bai shizhi (white halloysite)　longgu (ryukotsu; dragon bone)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried)　huanglian (oren; coptis; fibrous roots removed, lightly stir-fried)　baifan (alum; calcined; half a liang each)

Pound and sift the above medicines into a powder, knead with flour paste boiled in vinegar and form pills the size of hemp seeds. Give ten pills per year of age with rice water, before feeding. For vexing thirst, give them with decocted ginseng decoction; adjust further as judged for the child's size; three or four times a day.

## Notes

Xianglian wan: for diarrhea, vexing thirst, undigested food, abdominal pain and borborygmus from disharmony of cold and heat in children, and for dysentery with pus and blood, tenesmus, frequent stools at night, poor appetite and wasting tending toward gan.
- Coptis and dried ginger harmonize cold and heat; white halloysite, dragon bone and alum astringe the intestines. Despite the name, the formula in the base text has no costus and differs from the Da xianglian wan (Daikorengan) of juan 6, no. 060 (coptis and costus).
