# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷中 054 傷寒十勧 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Ten Admonitions on Cold Damage

One: In cold damage, headache with a hot body is a yang pattern; hot medicines must not be taken.
Cold damage is transmitted through the three yin and three yang, six meridians in all. Of these, Taiyin illness has no headache and no fever; Shaoyin illness may paradoxically have fever but no headache; Jueyin illness may have headache but no fever. [Headache with fever] is therefore a yang pattern; if a physician recklessly gives hot medicines, death will certainly result.

One: In cold damage the toxic qi should be attacked directly; one must not supplement.
When pathogenic qi is in the meridians and collaterals, attacking it according to the pattern brings recovery and peace in three or four days. If a physician insists that life requires healthy qi and supplements instead, the toxic qi will spread and flourish, and many will surely be killed.

One: In cold damage, even if there is no desire for food, medicines that warm the spleen and stomach must not be taken.
Lack of appetite in cold damage is a matter of course, and there is no reason one should starve to death. Lizhong wan (Richugan) and the like must not be taken lightly; if taken in a yang illness they increase the heat, possibly beyond rescue.

One: Abdominal pain in cold damage may also be a heat pattern; warming medicines must not be taken lightly.
The *Nanjing* says: "Pain is excess." Therefore Zhongjing, discussing the pattern of abdominal fullness with intermittent pain, says that for severe pain rhubarb is added. That rhubarb is added for severe pain shows his meaning. Only when abdominal pain occurs with a cold body and cold limbs is it a yin pattern, requiring consideration. I often see physicians kill people by giving hot medicines at once because of abdominal pain.

One: For spontaneous diarrhea in cold damage, look at whether it is a yin or yang pattern; supplementing medicines and medicines that stop diarrhea must not be given as a rule.
In spontaneous diarrhea, apart from those with no fever and warm hands and feet, which belong to Taiyin, and those with a cold body and counterflow cold of the limbs, which belong to Shaoyin and Jueyin, all other cases of diarrhea with fever are yang patterns and should be treated according to the pattern by Zhongjing's methods. I often see physicians kill people by giving warming medicines and medicines that stop diarrhea at once because of diarrhea.

One: For pain of the chest and flanks or abdominal pain in cold damage, moxibustion must not be used recklessly.
I often see in villages that when this pattern occurs and there is no medicine, moxa is used at once, and frequently the toxic qi flares with the fire, causing swelling, distension, dyspnea and death. They do not know that chest and flank pain belongs to Shaoyang and abdominal distension and fullness to Taiyin; apart from these, only yin patterns may be treated with moxa.

One: For cold hands and feet in cold damage, look at yin and yang; do not treat all as yin patterns.
In treatment there is heat syncope and cold syncope, and few physicians can distinguish them. Giving hot medicines for heat syncope kills faster than a blade. A yang illness cannot produce reversal unless the heat is extreme; this is what Zhongjing means by "when heat is deep, reversal is deep". To give further hot medicines when the heat is deep—how could there be any recovery? When the body was hot at the onset of illness, and after three or four days the heat has gone deep, with constipation, red urine, or delirium and clouded consciousness, and other heat signs, yet reversal appears, it must be heat syncope; it should be purged urgently with Chengqi tang (Jokito). If at the onset the body is not hot, there is no constipation, the patient pulls clothes over himself, or has diarrhea or frequent urination, and cold limbs occurs without heat signs, it is cold syncope, and only then may Sini tang (Shigyakuto) and the like be used. The two reversals confuse people because the pulse is deep in both; but in heat syncope the pulse is deep and weak, and moreover in heat syncope the pulse at times returns and the fingernails are warm, whereas in cold syncope they are constantly cold. By this they can be distinguished.

One: When cold damage illness is already in the interior, sweat must not be induced with medicines.
In cold damage one must look at exterior and interior. If there is fever with aversion to cold, it is in the exterior, and sweating is appropriate. If there is no aversion to cold but rather aversion to heat, it is an interior pattern; if the physician induces sweating regardless, the sweat that comes out is not pathogenic qi but all genuine qi; before the pathogenic qi is removed the genuine qi is first exhausted, and many die. There are also patterns half in the exterior and half in the interior, and patterns with neither exterior nor interior signs; in these not only must one not purge, one must not induce sweating either, but treat according to the pattern with consideration.

One: In cold damage, drinking water is a sign of impending recovery, but the patient must not be allowed to drink to excess.
When the patient is very thirsty, water should be given to disperse the heat qi; hence Zhongjing regarded drinking water as a sign of impending recovery. Seeing this, people let patients drink freely, and as a result many develop vomiting, dyspnea, cough with counterflow, diarrhea, swelling, palpitations, water binding and inhibited urination. If the patient wants to drink a bowl, give only half a bowl, always keeping it somewhat insufficient; this is good.

One: When cold damage illness has just healed, one must not overeat or exert oneself, or eat mutton, engage in sexual activity, eat bone broths or drink wine.
When the illness has just healed, the spleen and stomach are still weak; if one eats too much it cannot be digested and the illness returns at once; this is called food relapse. When the illness has just healed, blood and qi are still deficient; if one exerts oneself too early the illness returns at once; this is called taxation relapse. Further, those with cold damage who eat mutton or engage in sexual activity all die; those who take bone broths or drink wine fall ill again.

## Notes

Ten Admonitions on Cold Damage (the end of the General Guide, middle volume): (1) headache with fever is a yang pattern—no hot medicines; (2) attack the toxic qi, do not supplement; (3) do not give spleen-warming medicines for loss of appetite; (4) abdominal pain may be a heat pattern (the *Nanjing*'s "pain is excess", Zhongjing's adding rhubarb); (5) for diarrhea distinguish yin and yang and do not routinely give tonics or astringents; (6) do not use moxa recklessly for chest, flank or abdominal pain; (7) distinguish heat syncope (purge with Chengqi tang) from cold syncope (Sini tang) in cold extremities; (8) do not induce sweating once the illness is in the interior; (9) give water for thirst but not to excess; (10) after recovery avoid overeating, exertion, mutton, sex, bone broths and wine (food and taxation relapse).
- From the Song-dynasty *Shanghan shiquan* of Li Zijian (included in the *Leizheng huoren shu* and other works).
