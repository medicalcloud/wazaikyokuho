# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 030 正気散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zhengqi san (Qi-Righting Powder)

[For] yin patterns of cold damage with aversion to cold and wind: the healthy qi drives out the cold. It treats choking obstruction of the chest and diaphragm, distension of the flanks and ribs, hard stuffiness below the heart, vomiting and diarrhea, vomiting of sour water, coughing counterflow, lassitude with a liking for lying down, and no desire for food.

gancao (kanzo; licorice; stir-fried; 7 *qian*)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel)　huoxiang (agastache; stalks removed)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes; 1 liang each)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark)　banxia (hange; pinellia; 3 liang each like the magnolia bark, powdered, mashed with 4 liang of fresh ginger, made together into cakes and lightly stir-fried)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with three slices of fresh ginger, one jujube and one *zhan* of water, decoct down to seven-tenths and take it fairly hot before meals. It also treats long-standing malaria and heart pain from diaphragm qi; take three doses a day. Taken regularly it smooths qi, loosens the middle and wards off pestilence. (One version lacks white atractylodes.)

## Notes

Zhengqi san (Shaoxing-era additions): for yin-type cold damage (cold-natured colds) with aversion to cold and wind, chest obstruction, abdominal distension, vomiting and diarrhea, acid reflux, cough, lassitude and poor appetite, for chronic malaria and heart pain from diaphragm qi.
- Agastache, magnolia bark, tangerine peel, pinellia, atractylodes and licorice—the core of the later Huoxiang zhengqi san (Kakkoshokisan).
- The base text reads 遂冷 for 逐冷 ("drives out the cold"); since the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions both read 逐冷, it has been emended (see the emendation).
