# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 080 銭氏白朮散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qian shi baizhu san (Master Qian's White Atractylodes Powder)

It is suitable, regardless of yin or yang, deficiency or repletion, for children with long-standing deficiency of the spleen and stomach, vomiting and diarrhoea very frequent and unceasing, fluids withered and exhausted, vexing thirst with much dryness, wanting only to drink water, inability to nurse or eat, emaciation, fatigue and little strength, and, through mistreatment, transformation into wind epilepsy.

renshen (ginseng)　baizhu (white atractylodes; not exposed to fire)　muxiang (costus root; not exposed to fire)　bai fuling (white poria; black skin removed)　huoxiang (agastache; earth and stalks removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried; 1 liang each)　gange (dried pueraria; chopped; 2 liang)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 1 *qian* with a small *zhan* of water, decoct down to half a *zhan*, remove the dregs and give it at a comfortable temperature, at any time, adjusted for the child's size. For severe thirst, decoct [more] and let the child drink at will.

## Notes

Qian shi baizhu san (Wu Zhige's additions): for unceasing vomiting and diarrhoea, exhausted fluids, vexing thirst, craving for water, poor feeding, wasting and weakness from long-standing spleen and stomach deficiency in children, including cases mistreated into wind epilepsy, regardless of pattern. Still used today for vomiting, diarrhoea and thirst in children.
- Si junzi tang (ginseng, atractylodes, poria, licorice) plus costus, agastache and pueraria (Qian Yi's Qiwei baizhu san).
