# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 017 雄朱丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Xiongzhu wan (Realgar and Cinnabar Pill)

It treats wind-strike with surging drool, rattling in the throat and diaphragm, dizzy eyes that will not open, deviation of the mouth and eyes, and loss of control of the hands and feet. It is suitable for all wind diseases whatsoever.

xionghuang (realgar; ground)　zhusha (cinnabar; ground)　longnao (borneol; ground)　shexiang (musk; ground; 1 *qian* each)　bai jiangcan (silkworm; silk and mouth removed; raw)　baifuzi (typhonium; raw)　tiannanxing (arisaema; washed; raw)　wushe (black-striped snake; skin and bones removed; raw; half a liang each)

Except for the ground items, powder the rest and form pills the size of wutong seeds with refined honey. For wind-strike with surging drool and jaws that will not open, first pound a clove of garlic to a pulp and smear it on the cheeks outside both jaw joints; then dissolve one pill in soybean-steeped wine and rub it on the gums, and the jaws will open at once; then dissolve one or two pills in mint wine and give them. For men's wind qi or women's blood wind with clenched jaws, simply dissolve the medicine in soybean-steeped wine and rub it on the gums, and they open at once. For head wind with dizziness, or dark wind with blackness before the eyes and a tendency to fall, quickly chew one or two pills and take them with mint wine.

## Notes

Xiongzhu wan: for drool, facial deviation and loss of limb control in wind-strike, and for all wind diseases.
- Silkworm, typhonium, arisaema and snake are used raw to expel wind-phlegm, with realgar, cinnabar, borneol and musk; for clenched jaws it describes smearing garlic on the cheeks and rubbing the medicine on the gums.
- The Siku quanshu edition calls it Xiongzhu dan (see the variants).
