# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 051 術香散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shuxiang san (Zedoary and Aromatics Powder)

It is suitable for women with blood wind and visceral qi, dizzy head and eyes, vexation and palpitation, hot, painful hands and feet, irregular menses, intermittent pain of the navel and abdomen, or frequent diarrhea, and reduced eating.

Tiantai wuyao (Tiantai lindera)　sanleng (sparganium; roasted in embers)　peng ezhu (zedoary; roasted in embers)　chuan danggui (toki; Sichuan angelica; rhizome head removed)　jingjie sui (schizonepeta spikes)　tianma (gastrodia)　guixin (cinnamon heart; not exposed to fire)　yanhusuo (corydalis)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; processed with ginger juice, stir-fried)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; 1 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder. Take 1 *qian* each time mixed in warm wine with a little ginger juice.

## Notes

Shuxiang san (Wu Zhige's additions): for dizziness, vexation, palpitation, hot painful extremities, irregular menses, umbilical pain, diarrhea and poor appetite from blood wind and visceral qi in women.
- Zedoary, sparganium, corydalis and angelica move blood; lindera and magnolia bark move qi; aconite and cinnamon heart warm; schizonepeta and gastrodia dispel wind. "Shu" in the name probably refers to zedoary (ezhu).
