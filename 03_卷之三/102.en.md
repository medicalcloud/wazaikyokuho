# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 102 木香流気飲 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Muxiang liuqi yin (Costus Qi-Flowing Drink)

It regulates and smooths the ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi, frees the flow of blood and vessels, eases and frees the sanjiao, and calms and harmonizes the five zang organs. It treats all qi blocked and stagnant, with distension of the chest and diaphragm, a bitter mouth and dry throat, vomiting and little eating, and running, pouring, stabbing pain of the shoulders, back, abdomen and flanks; and urgent dyspnea with phlegm-cough, puffiness of the face and eyes, swelling and fullness of the limbs, constipation and red, rough urine. It also treats excessive worry and thought with palpitation and pent-up accumulation, and leg qi with wind-heat gathering and binding into swelling and pain, with dyspnea and fullness, distension and urgency.

banxia (hange; pinellia; washed seven times in hot water; 2 liang)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed; 2 jin)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, processed with ginger and stir-fried)　qingpi (green tangerine peel; white removed)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched)　xiangfu (cyperus; stir-fried, hairs removed)　zisuye (perilla leaf; twigs and stalks removed; 1 jin each)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng)　chi fuling (bukuryo; red poria; black skin removed)　dried mugua (chaenomeles)　shichangpu (acorus)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes)　baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica)　maimendong (bakumondo; ophiopogon; 4 liang each)　caoguo ren (tsaoko seed)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed, not exposed to fire)　peng'ezhu (zedoary; roasted, sliced)　dafupi (areca husk)　dingxiangpi (clove bark)　binglang (areca)　muxiang (mokko; mokko; costus root; not exposed to fire)　huoxiang leaves (agastache; 6 liang each)　mutong (akebia; nodes removed; 8 liang)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 4 *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, three slices of ginger and two jujubes, decoct down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it hot. For headache in cold damage, as soon as one feels the illness coming on, decoct it with three *cun* of scallion white with roots: it makes yin and yang rise and descend, sweat comes and recovery is immediate. For spontaneous diarrhea, decoct it with non-glutinous rice. For women's blood qi and concretions, decoct it with mugwort and vinegar. All at any time.

## Notes

Muxiang liuqi yin (bureau additions): a 23-ingredient formula for qi blockage with chest distension, bitter mouth, vomiting, poor appetite and running stabbing pain of the shoulders, back, abdomen and flanks; dyspnea, phlegm-cough, edema, constipation and difficult urination; pent-up accumulation from worry; and wind-heat swelling in leg qi.
- Perilla leaf, cyperus, the tangerine peels, magnolia bark, costus, clove bark, areca, areca husk, tsaoko, agastache, zedoary and cinnamon move qi; ginseng, atractylodes, red poria, licorice and ophiopogon supplement; pinellia and acorus transform phlegm; akebia and chaenomeles drain damp; angelica dahurica is added. The Ershisi wei liuqi yin (juan 3, no. 107) follows later.
- The base text writes 乾木瓜石菖蒲 without a space, but these are two ingredients.
