# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 115 小理中湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Xiao lizhong tang (Minor Middle-Regulating Drink)

It treats disharmony of the spleen and stomach with cold in the middle surging upward, counterflow fullness of the chest and flanks, gripping pain of the heart and abdomen, excessive drinking with phlegm counterflow and nausea, or occasional vomiting, deficiency distension below the heart, obstruction without passage, reduced eating, shortness of breath, emaciation and fatigue; it warms the middle, expels water and removes damp. It also treats cold damp of the intestines and stomach with pouring diarrhoea, water and grain not separating and thunderous rumbling in the abdomen, vomiting and diarrhoea of sudden turmoil, reversal cold of the hands and feet, chest impediment and heart pain, counterflow qi and bound qi.

cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked in rice-washing water, dried over a fire; 5 liang)　shengjiang (fresh ginger; 5 jin)　gancao (licorice; used raw; 10 liang)　yan (salt; stir-fried; 15 liang)

Chop and crush the above and mill them together, leave to steep overnight, dry over a fire and mill into a fine powder. Take 1 *qian* each time mixed with boiling water, on an empty stomach.

## Notes

Xiao lizhong tang (Wu Zhige's additions): a drink for counterflow fullness of the chest and flanks and gripping abdominal pain when cold in the middle surges upward; for phlegm, nausea, vomiting, epigastric distension, poor appetite, breathlessness and wasting after excessive drinking; for pouring diarrhoea and borborygmus from cold damp; for vomiting and diarrhoea of sudden turmoil with cold limbs; and for chest impediment, heart pain and counterflow or bound qi.
- Four ingredients: black atractylodes, fresh ginger, licorice and salt.
- Emendation: "disharmony of the spleen and stomach" (*bu he*) reads *zhong he* in the base text; emended following the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions.
