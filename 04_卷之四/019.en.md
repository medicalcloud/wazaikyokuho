# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之四 019 温肺湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wenfei tang (Lung-Warming Decoction)

It treats lung deficiency with cold rheum lodged for a long time, which in attacks causes panting and cough so that one can neither sit nor lie, vomiting of phlegm and froth, and no desire for food.

bai shaoyao (white peony root; 6 liang)　wuweizi (schisandra; stalks removed, stir-fried)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　banxia (pinellia; boiled until cooked, dried over a fire)　chenpi (aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　xingren (apricot kernel)　gancao (licorice; stir-fried; 3 liang each)　xixin (asarum; rhizome head removed, washed; 2 liang)

Chop the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take three large *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, decoct down to eight-tenths, wring the liquid out through silk and take it after meals. The dregs of two doses are decocted again as one dose. One version omits white peony and asarum; it may be modified in use.

## Notes

Wenfei tang (Shaoxing-era additions): for long-standing cold rheum lodged in deficient lungs, with attacks of panting and cough that prevent sitting or lying, vomiting of frothy phlegm and poor appetite.
- Dried ginger, asarum, schisandra, cinnamon, pinellia, peony and licorice are close to Xiao qinglong tang without ephedra, with tangerine peel and apricot kernel added.
- The base text reads 久咳寒飲 for 久客寒飲 ("cold rheum lodged long"); since the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions both read 久客寒飲, it has been emended (see the emendation).
