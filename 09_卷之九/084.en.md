# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 084 増損四物湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zengsun siwu tang (Modified Four Substances Decoction)

(From the *Yijian fang*.) It treats women with insufficient qi and blood, weary limbs, lack of strength and scant qi. It also treats excessive postpartum bleeding with deficient, damaged ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi, disharmony of yin and yang and alternating fever and chills; all should take it.

danggui (toki; Chinese angelica)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried)　bai shaoyao (shakuyaku; white peony root; equal parts)

Cut the above into pieces. For each dose take 4 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, decoct down to six-tenths, remove the dregs and take it hot. If after childbirth there are chills and fever and stabbing pain in the abdomen, there is failed blood; Wuji san (Goshakusan) decocted with vinegar and Dasheng san should be taken. If the discharge has been excessive and there is still stabbing pain, these two medicines are also suitable. One formula, for menstrual blood congealed and stagnant in the abdomen with blood-qi pain, adds equal parts of zedoary and cinnamon to Siwu tang (Shimotsuto) and calls it Liuhe tang (Rokuwato). One formula, for unceasing bleeding and a restless fetus in pregnancy, adds equal parts of processed mugwort, dried ginger, licorice, donkey-hide gelatin and astragalus and calls it Jiao'ai tang. One method, for bloody dysentery, adds only gelatin and mugwort. For postpartum blood contention with dry mouth and vexing thirst, add trichosanthes and ophiopogon. For vexing heat with rough urination and constipation, add Dahuang taoren tang. For flank distension, add magnolia bark and unripe bitter orange. For deficiency vexation with inability to sleep, add bamboo leaves and ginseng. For great thirst with restlessness, add anemarrhena and gypsum. One formula, for blood deficiency in women with unbearable gripping pain of the heart and abdomen, removes rehmannia and adds dried ginger and calls it Sishen tang. In general, after childbirth, regardless of how much bleeding there is, three doses of Heishen san must be taken daily; where the bleeding is slight, alternate with Dasheng san. Only after the second seven-day period, when there is hardly any pain in the abdomen, should Siwu tang, Jianzhong tang and the like be taken. If taken too early, they supplement and lock in the failed blood, and the later trouble is not slight. Heishen san and Dasheng san are not blood-expelling medicines; they merely push out the old and bring in the new, and much use does no harm. People today often suspect that they expel blood and are cold in nature, but this is not so, as can be seen from the drugs they use. Once much malign blood has gone, supplementing gradually is not too late; one must not temporize and leave later trouble. Again, the ancient formulas use Sishun lizhong wan as a remedy for improving appetite after childbirth, but it is made into pills with honey and the licorice is doubled, so it is extremely sweet—how can it refresh the spleen? It is better simply to use Lizhong tang (Richuto) with a little less licorice. For those with habitual phlegm-fluid retention, Erchen tang (Nichinto) and the like are best. Again, for morning sickness in pregnant women the ancient formulas have Fuling wan (Bukuryogan) and Fuling tang (Bukuryoto), containing rehmannia, bamboo shavings, lovage and the like, supposed to settle vomiting, but taking them is seen to make it worse. Morning sickness in general always arises from habitual phlegm-fluid retention; using Erchen tang renamed Xiao fuling tang is extremely effective. This must be known.

## Notes

Zengsun siwu tang (bureau additions; cited from the *Yijian fang*): for lassitude, weakness and breathlessness from insufficient qi and blood in women, and for chills and fever with deficient ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi after heavy postpartum bleeding.
- Siwu tang without prepared rehmannia, plus ginseng, dried ginger and licorice. It lists modifications of Siwu tang (Liuhe tang, Jiao'ai tang, Sishen tang, etc.), argues that after childbirth failed blood should first be cleared with Heishen san and Dasheng san and supplementation with Siwu tang or Jianzhong tang begun only after fourteen days, criticizes the excessive sweetness of Sishun lizhong wan (juan 9, no. 033) and the use of Fuling wan and Fuling tang (juan 9, nos. 026–027) for morning sickness, and recommends Erchen tang (as Xiao fuling tang) instead.
