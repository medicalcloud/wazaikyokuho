# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 081 全蠍観音散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Quanxie guanyin san (Scorpion Guanyin Powder)

The patterns treated are the same as for the earlier Guanyin san.

shilian rou (lotus fruit flesh; stir-fried, plumule removed)　bai biandou (hyacinth bean; stir-fried)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; 2½ liang each)　shenqu (medicated leaven; stir-fried; 2 liang)　quanxie (whole scorpion)　qianghuo (notopterygium)　tianma (gastrodia; shoots removed)　fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia; shoots removed)　muxiang (mokko; costus root; blast-fried)　baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried)　huangqi (ogi; astragalus; pounded flat, brushed with honey, roasted; 1 liang each)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed; 1½ liang)

Make the above into a fine powder. For an infant one *zi*; for two or three years half a *qian*; for four or five years 1 *qian*; with one *zhan* or half a *zhan* of water and half or one jujube, decoct together down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and give it, at any time.

## Notes

Quanxie guanyin san (from here the layer of bureau additions): the same indications as the earlier Guanyin san (juan 10, no. 054), strengthened against wind by adding scorpion, notopterygium, gastrodia and saposhnikovia.
