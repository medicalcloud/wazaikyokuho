# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 001 膃肭臍丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wanaqi wan (Seal Genitals Pill)

It supplements deficiency and strengthens qi, warms the back and expels evil, augments essence and marrow, regulates the spleen and stomach, improves eating and brightens the complexion. It is suitable for the five taxations and seven damages with deficient, exhausted true qi, cold pain of the navel and abdomen, aching limbs, stiffness of the lower back and spine, slack, weak legs and knees, a dark sallow complexion, wasted flesh, dim eyes and tinnitus, a bitter mouth and dry tongue, empty rumbling in the abdomen, stabbing pain below the flanks, tasteless food, constant sorrow, many strange dreams at night and little vitality by day, slippery frequent urination with occasional dribbling, impotence or intercourse in dreams; and for all wind deficiency and chronic cold.

wanaqi (seal genitals; one pair, roasted with wine over a gentle fire until cooked)　naosha (sal ammoniac; ground and water-ground; 2 liang)　lean mutton (cooked, chopped to a pulp and ground)　sheep marrow (juice taken; 1 jin each)　chenxiang (aquilaria)　shenqu (medicated leaven; stir-fried; 4 liang each)

Simmer these six ingredients together in 1 *dou* of good ash-free wine in a silver vessel over a gentle fire into a paste; when cool, add the following medicines.

yangqishi (actinolite; boiled for a day in fermented rice water, finely ground, water-ground and dried over a fire for use)　renshen (ginseng; rhizome head removed)　buguzhi (psoralea; stir-fried in wine)　zhongru powder (stalactite; refined)　baji (morinda; core removed)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　roudoukou (nutmeg; shell removed)　zisuzi (perilla seed; stir-fried)　zhiqiao (bitter orange; pulp removed, stir-fried with bran)　muxiang (costus root)　bichengqie (cubeb)　huluba (fenugreek; stir-fried)　tianma (gastrodia; sprouts removed)　qingpi (green tangerine peel; white removed)　dingxiang (clove)　huixiang (fennel; imported by ship, stir-fried; 2 liang each)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　binglang (areca)　jilizi (tribulus; stir-fried)　dafuzi (areca seed; 2½ liang each)　shanyao (Chinese yam; 1½ liang)　congrong (cistanche; washed, sliced, dried over a fire; 4 liang)　bai doukou (white cardamom; shells removed; 1 liang)　large fuzi (aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed, boiled with half a jin of blue salt and 1½ *dou* of fermented rice water until the water is gone, sliced and dried over a fire; 8 liang)

Process each of the above medicines according to method, pound and sieve them into a powder, knead it into the paste to form a mass, pound it a thousand-odd times in a mortar and form pills the size of wutong seeds. Take twenty pills each time on an empty stomach with warm wine; salt water is also fine.

## Notes

Wanaqi wan (the first formula of juan 5, "treating the various deficiencies"): a large tonifying formula for the five taxations and seven damages with deficient true qi and cold of the lower origin—cold umbilical pain, stiff back, weak legs, dark complexion, wasting, dim eyes, tinnitus, many dreams, frequent urination, dribbling, impotence and dream intercourse.
- Seal genitals, mutton and sheep marrow are made into a paste with wine, combined with kidney-warming, yang-supplementing drugs (actinolite, stalactite, psoralea, morinda, cistanche, fenugreek, aconite, cinnamon) and aromatics (aquilaria, costus, clove, fennel).
