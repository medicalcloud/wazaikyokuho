# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之八 034 太岳活血丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Taiyue huoxue dan (Taiyue Blood-Quickening Elixir)

It treats men and women with external injuries and internal damage, dog bites and insect injuries, being kicked by a donkey or falling from a horse, injured and fractured hands and feet, all kinds of pain, static blood in the abdomen stabbing the flanks and pounding the heart; as well as paralysis of the left or right side, wandering pain, abscess swellings and fistulas of piles. It is also suitable for women with cold qi entering the abdomen and blocked blood vessels, postpartum failed blood pouring into the limbs, swollen, painful breast inflammation (*chuinai*), and gripping pain of blood and qi.

luanfa (human hair; washed clean in gleditsia water; 2 jin, sun-dried, stir-fried in a pot with 2 jin of clear sesame oil, frequently picked up and examined with the fingers, and stopped when it becomes brittle and tangled like paste-moss; it must not be over-fried)　lixie (chestnut wedge: when three chestnuts share one burr, the flat thin one in the middle is this; shell removed, thinly sliced, sun-dried)　zaojiaoci (gleditsia thorn; burned red-hot, quenched in rice vinegar, dried over a fire)　da heidou (large black soybean; wiped free of dust and dirt with a damp cloth, black skins removed, dried over a fire)　huasang zhi (flowering mulberry branches; arm-thick pieces burned over charcoal until the smoke is gone, quenched in rice vinegar, taken out and dried over a fire; 1 jin each)　bimaren (castor seed; ground separately and smeared on the ink; 3 liang)　ruxiang (frankincense; good quality, finely ground, boiled with a bowl of rice vinegar until well cooked and fragrant; 4 liang)　ximo (fine ink; half a jin: for one half, grind 3 liang of castor seed to a pulp in a mortar and smear it all over the ink, wrap in thin paper, seal with yellow clay, sun-dry, calcine red-hot with 50 jin of fire, set on the ground covered with a basin to let the fire qi out for as long as it takes to cook rice twice; for the other half, dissolve 2 liang of sal ammoniac in vinegar, smear it on the ink and roast dry)　naosha (sal ammoniac; bright, clean pieces, dissolved in vinegar and smeared on the ink; 2 liang)

Powder the above six ingredients, put them into the frankincense paste, pound three thousand times and form pills the size of a pellet. If the frankincense paste is insufficient, add flour paste boiled in vinegar. For severe pain take one pill each time; for mild cases half a pill. Using one *zhan* of wine without lime and a piece of frankincense the size of a bean, first grind away the frankincense, then grind away the medicine, decoct through three to five boils and take it warm at bedtime, lying in bed on the painful side. If sweating is desired, cover with clothes and quilts. Also smear the medicine on the injured place and rub it in. Avoid all wind-stirring things. When taken for women's various illnesses, also use 1 *qian* of powdered angelica and decoct as directed. Pregnant women must not take it.

## Notes

Taiyue huoxue dan: for pain from external injury, internal damage, dog and insect bites, falls from horses and fractures; static blood in the abdomen; hemiplegia; wandering pain; abscesses and anal fistulas; and for women's blocked blood vessels from cold, postpartum failed blood, breast inflammation and blood-qi pain. Contraindicated in pregnancy.
- Human hair, chestnut wedge, gleditsia thorn, black soybean and mulberry branches fried or burned, ink smeared with castor seed and sal ammoniac and calcined, made into pills with frankincense paste. Ground into wine, decocted and taken; also applied externally.
