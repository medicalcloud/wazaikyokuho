# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷中 004 論中風半身不遂 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On hemiplegia in wind stroke. It always arises from pathogenic wind striking the meridians and collaterals, so that blood and qi move slowly and the joints become slack; hence the hands and feet lose their use and the mouth and eyes are deviated. Qibao dan may be given. For hemilateral wind with halting, rough speech, give Xiao xuming tang (Shozokumeito). For hemilateral wind with wandering pain and numbness of the body, give Huoluo dan (Katsurakutan). For hemilateral wind with unsettled abstraction, give Paifeng tang. For hemilateral wind with abundant phlegm and drool, give Qingzhou bai wanzi. For hypertonicity with weak legs and clenched jaw, give Longhu dan (Dragon-Tiger Elixir). For paralysis with loss of use of the hands and feet, give Toubing dan (Ice-Penetrating Elixir). For hemilateral wind with hypertonic sinews and vessels, give Qufeng wan (Wind-Expelling Pill), Ruxiang chentong san, Ruxiang wan (Nyukogan) or Qibao dan.

## Notes

On hemiplegia in wind stroke: attributed to pathogenic wind in the meridians slowing blood and qi and slackening the joints; by symptom it recommends Qibao dan, Xiao xuming tang (speech impairment), Huoluo dan (wandering pain, numbness), Paifeng tang (abstraction), Qingzhou bai wanzi (phlegm), Longhu dan (hypertonicity, weak legs, clenched jaw), Toubing dan (paralysis), and Qufeng wan, Ruxiang chentong san and Ruxiang wan (hypertonic sinews).
