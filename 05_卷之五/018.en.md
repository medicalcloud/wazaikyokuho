# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 018 安腎丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Anshen wan (Kidney-Calming Pill)

It treats long-standing accumulation of yin cold in the kidney meridian, deficiency cold of the bladder, decline and exhaustion of the lower origin, hardness of hearing and parched lips, swelling and pain of the lower back and thighs, griping pain of the navel and abdomen, stabbing distension of both flanks, hard pain of the lower abdomen, damp itching of the lower parts, seminal emission in dreams at night, abstraction and frequent fright, dry skin, a lusterless face, a bland mouth without taste, no desire for food, loose stools, slippery frequent urination, an unclear mind and much forgetfulness. Taken regularly it supplements the original yang and augments kidney qi.

rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed, not exposed to fire)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; 16 liang each)　taoren (tonin; peach kernel; stir-fried with bran)　bai jili (tribulus; stir-fried, spines removed)　baji (morinda; core removed)　shanyao (Chinese yam)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed)　roucongrong (cistanche; soaked in wine, roasted)　shihu (sekkoku; dendrobium; roots removed, roasted)　bixie (dioscorea hypoglauca)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes)　poguzhi (psoralea; 48 liang each)

Powder the above and form pills the size of wutong seeds with refined honey. Take thirty pills each time with warm wine or salt water, on an empty stomach before meals. For small-intestine qi, take them with stir-fried fennel in salted wine.

## Notes

Anshen wan (Shaoxing-era additions): for hardness of hearing, swollen painful lower back and thighs, abdominal pain, hard lower abdominal pain, damp genital itching, seminal emission, fright, dry skin, poor appetite, loose stools, frequent urination and forgetfulness from long-standing yin cold in the kidney meridian and bladder.
- Cinnamon and aconite warm; morinda, cistanche and psoralea supplement kidney yang; Chinese yam, poria, atractylodes and dendrobium nourish the spleen and yin; dioscorea and tribulus dispel wind-damp; peach kernel moves the blood.
