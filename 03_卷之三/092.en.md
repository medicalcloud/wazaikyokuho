# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 092 千金大養脾丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qianjin da yangpi wan (Thousand-Gold Major Spleen-Nourishing Pill)

It is suitable for weak spleen and stomach with lodged cold and retained rheum, diaphragm qi with choking obstruction, stomach reflux with vomiting of food, glomus and fullness of the heart and chest, deficiency distension of the flanks and ribs, stabbing pain of the chest and abdomen drawing into the spine, eating little and frequently injured, a faint voice and shortness of breath, a bitter mouth and rough tongue, nausea and retching, much spitting and acid swallowing, and chronic illness with diarrhoea and deficient, slippery stomach and intestines; or when after a major illness the qi does not return to normal, food has no taste and the appearance is haggard; and much phlegm after drinking.

zhiqiao (bitter orange)　shenqu (medicated leaven)　chenpi (aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　mainie (barley sprouts; stir-fried)　huixiang (fennel)　baijiang (white dried ginger; blast-fried)　suosha (amomum; skin removed)　roudoukou (nutmeg)　sanleng (sparganium; blast-fried)　fuling (poria; skin removed)　liangjiang (galangal)　yiyiren (coix seed)　yizhi (alpinia oxyphylla; shells removed)　hujiao (black pepper)　muxiang (costus root)　bai biandou (hyacinth bean; stir-fried)　dingxiang (clove)　baizhu (white atractylodes)　hongdou (galangal fruit)　huoxiang (agastache; stalks removed)　shanyao (Chinese yam)　kugeng (bitter platycodon; stir-fried)　renshen (ginseng)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　peng'ezhu (zedoary; blast-fried)

Powder the above in equal parts and form pills the size of a pellet with refined honey. Take one pill each time, chewed thoroughly, washed down with plain hot water; warm wine is also fine; on an empty stomach before meals. Taken regularly it nourishes and benefits the spleen and stomach and greatly improves eating.

## Notes

Qianjin da yangpi wan (bureau additions): a 25-ingredient formula for lodged cold, retained rheum, diaphragm qi, stomach reflux, chest glomus, stabbing abdominal pain, poor appetite, shortness of breath and chronic diarrhoea from weak spleen and stomach, for poor recovery after major illness, and for phlegm after drinking.
- Ginseng, atractylodes, poria, licorice, Chinese yam, coix and hyacinth bean supplement; clove, black pepper, galangal, white ginger, galangal fruit, fennel, alpinia and nutmeg warm; costus, amomum, agastache, tangerine peel, bitter orange and platycodon regulate qi; medicated leaven, barley sprouts, sparganium and zedoary disperse food and accumulations.
- It is also written "Ganjin da yangpi wan" (in the table of contents of the Executive Yuan edition).
