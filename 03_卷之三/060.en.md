# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 060 金露丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jinlu wan (Golden Dew Pill)

(From the formula of Master Lin Chao, presented by Wang Yuanlan in the seventh year of Tianbao.) It treats accumulations and concretion lumps in the abdomen, long-standing and as large as a cup; yellow emaciation with old water and cough morning and evening; cold qi of many years with recurrent coiling, wringing, binding pain below the abdomen that surges into the heart and both flanks, penetrates the back and connects with the heart, the painful qi never ceasing, and qi circling below the navel like the gnawing of insects, unbearable. It also treats the ten kinds of water qi, stomach reflux with vomiting of food, frequent choking on food, the five kinds of haemorrhoidal fistula, soft-tissue qi and running wind like crawling insects, vexing heat of the hands and feet, restless sleep at night and endless talking in sleep. It also treats children's fright gan and women's five evils: dreaming of intercourse with ghosts, heaviness with no desire for food, a dreamlike daze unaware of human affairs, frequent near-death states, singing or weeping without settling, irregular menses, mania-like disturbance in the heart, wasting of the body, a condition hard to describe. Simply take this medicine and it will not fail once in ten thousand; all these illnesses are cured. No further details need be given.

sheng gan dihuang (dried raw rehmannia; chopped, dried over a fire)　beimu (fritillaria; core removed)　ziwan (aster; washed, sprouts removed, chopped, dried over a fire)　chaihu (bupleurum; rhizome head removed, chopped, dried over a fire)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried)　guixin (cinnamon heart; not exposed to fire)　renshen (ginseng; washed, rhizome head removed, sliced, dried over a fire)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; rhizome head removed, chopped, dried over a fire)　zhiqiao (bitter orange; soaked in hot water, pulp removed, stir-fried with bran)　shujiao (Sichuan pepper; seeds removed, stir-fried to sweat out the oil)　jiegeng (platycodon; washed, rhizome head removed, chopped, dried over a fire)　wuzhuyu (evodia; soaked seven times in hot water)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　xiongqiong (ligusticum; washed, rhizome head removed, chopped, dried over a fire)　changpu (acorus; soaked overnight in rice-washing water)　bai fuling (white poria; black skin removed, chopped, dried over a fire)　houpo (magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, processed with ginger juice)　biejia (turtle shell; roasted yellow with rice vinegar)　gansong (nardostachys; washed clean; 1 liang each)　caowutou (wild aconite; blast-fried)　huanglian (coptis; washed, chopped, dried over a fire; 2 liang each)　badou (croton seeds; core and membranes removed, boiled in vinegar through thirty boilings, dried over a fire; take 1 liang; oil not removed. When boiling, one must personally count the thirty boilings and then pour it out and dry it over a fire; if boiled too long the medicine loses its force. One version uses kansui instead)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with flour paste. Take five pills each time; children two. For phlegm trouble in the heart, take them with ginger decoction; for heart pain, with sour pomegranate-peel decoction; for mouth sores, with honey water; for headache, with gypsum decoction or scallion tea; for all spleen qi, with tangerine-peel decoction; for watery or qi diarrhoea, with a drink of boiled aged tangerine peel; for red dysentery, with licorice decoction; for white dysentery, with dried-ginger decoction; for red and white dysentery, with licorice and dried-ginger decoction; for choking oppression of the chest and diaphragm, with tetrapanax decoction; for women's blood qi, with angelica wine—or, if they do not drink wine, with angelica decoction; for mounting qi, miasmic qi, small-intestine qi and prolapse, with aconite decoction. For regular use and for all sorts of emergencies, simply take them with rice gruel, tea, wine or boiled water as preferred. For abdominal pain from cold damage, damage by wine and food, wine jaundice and jaundice, bound qi with glomus and blockage, and crane's-knee, take them with salt water or salted wine.

## Notes

Jinlu wan (Baoqing-era additions): said to be Master Lin Chao's formula presented by Wang Yuanlan in Tianbao 7 (748). Used for abdominal accumulations and concretions, jaundice, cough, chronic cold abdominal pain, water qi, stomach reflux, haemorrhoids and fistulas, running wind, children's fright gan, and women's five evils and irregular menses, with the vehicle varied by illness.
- In the same lineage as the earlier Wenbai wan (juan 3, no. 031): croton (boiled in vinegar) and wild aconite attack; dried ginger, cinnamon heart, Sichuan pepper and evodia warm; ginseng, poria and licorice supplement; coptis, bupleurum, fritillaria, aster, platycodon, saposhnikovia, ligusticum, acorus, bitter orange, magnolia bark, turtle shell, raw rehmannia and nardostachys are added.
