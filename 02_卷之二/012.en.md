# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 012 大柴胡湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Da chaihu tang (Daisaikoto; Major Bupleurum Decoction)

It may be taken for cold damage of more than ten days with pathogenic qi bound in the interior, alternating fever and chills, constipation, fullness, distension and pain of the abdomen, delirium, stuffiness and hardness in the heart, and inability to get food down; or no bowel movement for five or six days, stabbing pain around the navel and occasional restlessness; and malaria-like fever in the evening after sweating, combined with excess of the zang-fu organs and a forceful pulse.

zhishi (kijitsu; unripe bitter orange; pulp removed, stir-fried; half a liang)　chaihu (saiko; bupleurum; rhizome head removed; half a jin)　dahuang (daio; rhubarb; 2 liang)　banxia (hange; pinellia; washed seven times in hot water, sliced and dried over a fire; 2½ liang)　chi shaoyao (shakuyaku; red peony root)　huangqin (ogon; scutellaria; 3 liang each)

Make the five ingredients into a coarse powder and mix in the pinellia evenly. For each dose take three large *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, five slices of fresh ginger and one jujube, decoct down to one medium *zhan*, strain off the dregs and take it warm, after meals and at bedtime. This medicine treats cold damage with internal heat and interior excess. If there is body pain, the exterior pattern has not yet resolved, and it must not be taken.

## Notes

Da chaihu tang: for excess patterns of cold damage with pathogen bound in the interior—alternating fever and chills, constipation, abdominal fullness and pain, stuffiness and hardness below the heart, delirium and a forceful pulse. It corresponds to Da chaihu tang of the *Shanghan lun* (e.g. clause 103) and is still used for excess gastrointestinal and hepatobiliary disorders.
- Bupleurum and scutellaria harmonize the Shaoyang; rhubarb and unripe bitter orange purge interior excess; peony and pinellia regulate. It is not to be used while body pain (an exterior pattern) remains.
