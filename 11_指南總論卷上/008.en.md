# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷上 008 論炮炙三品薬石類例・草部 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Herb Section

Acorus: that growing on rocks with close-set nodes is good. To use, chop and lightly stir-fry, or simply dry over a fire.
Chrysanthemum flower: to use, remove twigs and stalks and dry over a fire before using in medicines.
Ginseng: to use, first remove the rhizome head, chop, dry over a fire and weigh before using in medicines. If the rhizome head is not removed it causes vomiting; be careful.
Asparagus root and ophiopogon: to use, first moisten slightly in hot water, pull out the core, dry over a fire, weigh and use.
Licorice: use large pieces. To use, first split open, roast lightly over a fire until yellow-red, then use in medicines. For the thin tips, merely scorch-fry; or use raw, according to the formula.
Prepared rehmannia: to use, wash clean, soak in wine for a day and night, strain out, steam for the time of two or three cookings of rice, dry over a fire, then use in medicines. If needed urgently, merely sprinkle with wine and steam; unsteamed is also acceptable, but soaking in wine and steaming is better. The raw dried kind is used raw without wine soaking.
Black atractylodes: to use, first soak in rice-washing water—five days in spring, three in summer, seven in autumn, ten in winter—changing the water daily; when the days are complete, scrape off the skin, dry over a fire, then use in medicines. In haste it may be used unsoaked, though it is somewhat drying.
Dodder seed: to use, first wash in water, let settle and remove sand and earth, then soak in good wine for a day and night, strain out, steam, pound to a coarse powder while hot, dry over a fire and then pound together with the other medicines. What does not pound fully, soak again for three to five days, take out, sun-dry slightly and pound; it will all break up at once; when hot it crushes easily.
Sichuan achyranthes: to use, first wash and remove the rhizome head, chop, soak in wine for a day and night, dry over a fire and use. If urgent, soak in wine and steam; unsteamed is also acceptable.
Bupleurum, peucedanum and the like: to use, first remove the rhizome head, wash, chop and dry over a fire before using in medicines.
White atractylodes, pubescent angelica, notopterygium and the like: to use, chop and dry over a fire before using in medicines.
Plantago seed: to use, stir-fry lightly until dry before using in medicines; simply drying over a fire is also acceptable.
Costus root: to use, do not expose to fire; chop finely and sun-dry. If making a fine powder, slicing thinly and drying over a gentle fire does no harm, but sun-drying is better.
Chinese yam, Sichuan lovage and the like: to use, chop and dry over a fire.
Coix seed: to use, stir-fry dry with glutinous rice.
Polygala: to use, first remove the core and dry over a fire before using in medicines. If the core is not removed it causes vexation and oppression. Soaking overnight in licorice decoction, straining out and drying over a fire is even better.
Gentian: to use, first remove the rhizome head, chop, soak overnight in licorice, strain out and sun-dry. In haste, unsoaked is acceptable.
Alisma: to use, soak overnight in wine, strain out and dry over a fire; unsoaked is also acceptable; if there is other processing, follow the formula.
Dendrobium: to use, first wash off the roots and earth, soak overnight in wine, strain out, steam and sun-dry before using in medicines. If urgent, unsteamed is acceptable; if there is other processing, follow the formula.
Morinda: to use, first remove the core, soak in wine for a day and night, chop and dry over a fire. If urgent, unsoaked is acceptable.
Coptis: to use, first remove the fibrous roots cleanly, chop, mix with honey and stir-fry dry over a gentle fire before using in medicines.
Tribulus: to use, pick clean, steam for a full day, sun-dry, pound in a wooden mortar until the spines are all gone, mix with wine, steam again, take out and sun-dry.
Astragalus: to use, first split open, smear with honey and roast until slightly red, then slice thinly, dry over a fire and weigh before using in medicines.
Cistanche: to use, first wash in warm water, scrape off the coarse scaly skin, cut up, soak in wine for a day and night, strain out and dry over a fire. If needed urgently, soak in wine and boil, grind to a paste, or dry over a fire.
Saposhnikovia: to use, first remove the rhizome head and pieces with forked heads or forked tails, wash, chop and dry over a fire before using in medicines. Forked heads cause madness and forked tails cause chronic illness; be most careful.
Typha pollen: this is the yellow pollen on cattails; identify it carefully and do not mistakenly use pine pollen. To use, dry over a fire through three layers of paper until yellow, steam for half a day, then dry over a fire again; this is excellent. To break blood and disperse swelling use it raw; to supplement and stop bleeding stir-fry it.
Dipsacus: to use, first chop, soak in wine for a full day, strain out and dry over a fire before using in medicines. If urgent, unsoaked is acceptable.
Asarum: to use, first remove earth and shoots and dry over a fire before using in medicines.
Schisandra: to use, first pick clean, removing twigs and stalks. For decoctions, crush it.
Cnidium seed: to use, first stir-fry lightly over a gentle fire before using in medicines.
Capillaris: to use, first remove roots and earth, chop finely and dry over a fire before using in medicines; do not let it meet fire [directly].
Vaccaria seed: to use, first steam whole for a full day, then soak overnight in sour rice water, strain out at dawn and dry over a fire before using in medicines.
Dried ginger: to use, first blast-fry until it cracks; only then may it be used in medicines.
Sophora flavescens root: to use, in any quantity, first soak overnight in thick glutinous rice-washing water, strain out, steam for a full day, then slice finely and dry over a fire; this is excellent.
Chinese angelica: to use, first remove dust and about one *fen* of the rhizome head, tips and hard parts, soak overnight in wine, strain out and dry over a fire before use; or lightly stir-fry, according to the formula. To supplement blood use the head section; to stop pain and break blood use the tail. If both are used together, it is better not to use it at all, for taking it has no effect.
Ephedra: to use, first remove roots and nodes, cut into one-*cun* lengths to open the fibers, boil separately through ten-odd boils and skim off the froth, then take out, chop and dry over a fire. If [the froth] is not entirely removed it causes vexation and oppression. If urgent, simply removing roots and nodes will do.
Akebia: to use, first chop and remove the nodes before using in medicines.
Peony root: to use, chop and dry over a fire before using in medicines.
Dianthus: to use, only the stamens and husks are used, not the stems and leaves. If they are used together, it causes qi blockage in the throat and urinary incontinence.
Epimedium: to use, stir-fry mixed with sheep fat until the fat is used up; for each jin prepared use 4 liang of sheep fat.
Scutellaria: to use, first chop and lightly stir-fry before using in medicines.
Cibotium: to use, first singe off the hair cleanly over a fierce fire, soak overnight in wine, steam and dry over a fire. If urgent, it need not be soaked in wine.
Aster: to use, first wash clean of earth and lightly stir-fry before using in medicines.
Pyrrosia: to use, first wipe off the yellow hairs with coarse cloth and stir-fry dry with sheep fat before using in medicines. If urgent, lightly roasting will do.
Dioscorea hypoglauca: to use, first wash clean, soak in wine for a day and night and dry over a fire; this is best. If urgent, this does not apply.
Cynanchum: to use, first remove the shoots, soak overnight in glutinous rice-washing water, strain out and steam.
Mugwort leaf: to use, first remove twigs and stalks, pound into floss, mix evenly with thin glutinous rice gruel and dry over a fire. Or stir-fry over a gentle fire, though it may then be hard to pound.
Arctium seed: to use, pick clean so there are no mixed seeds, then mix with wine, steam for a full day, take out, dry over a fire, pound separately to powder, then use in medicines.
Gastrodia: to use, first wrap in paper, soak until wet, roast in hot ashes until cooked, take out, soak overnight in wine, then dry over a fire and use in medicines.
Asafoetida: to use, first grind to powder in a clean bowl, then roll it over a vessel of hot wine before using in medicines.
Lesser galangal: to use, first chop, mix evenly with a little sesame oil and stir-fry.
Stemona root: to use, split open with a bamboo knife, remove the core, soak overnight in wine, strain out, chop finely and dry over a fire.
Fennel: to use, take the kind imported by ship, rinse clean, soak overnight in wine, strain out, sun-dry and stir-fry. If urgent, simply stir-fry.
Moutan bark: to use, pick clean, mix with wine, steam, chop finely and sun-dry before using in medicines.
Sparganium and zedoary: to use, first boil in vinegar, chop and dry over a fire; or blast-fry in hot ashes until cooked.
Psoralea: its nature is fundamentally very drying, toxic and hot. To use, soak overnight in wine, strain out, then soak in east-flowing water for three days and nights, steam again, sun-dry and use in medicines. If urgent, stir-fry with salt until fragrant and remove the salt.
Amomum: to use, first stir-fry with the husk over a gentle fire until heated through, remove the husk and take the kernels for use in medicines.
Aconite, tianxiong (tenyu) and the like: to use, first blast-fry until cracked and cooked, remove skin and navel, and dry over a fire before using in medicines.
Sichuan aconite (wutou [uzu]): to use, first blast-fry until cracked and cooked, remove skin, navel and tip, slice and dry over a fire.
Nutmeg: to use, first wrap in dough and blast-fry in hot ashes until the dough is cooked, remove the dough, chop and dry over a fire.
Pinellia: to use, first soak in boiling water, wait until warm and wash off the sliminess; do this seven times before use. For decoctions, slice and use whole. If it still stings the throat, pound to powder, pound with an equal amount of fresh ginger, grind and combine into a mass, steep overnight, pinch into cakes and dry over a fire. Pounding again into powder, combining again with ginger, steeping and drying is even better; this is excellent for decoctions.
Rhubarb: to use, either steam it, or blast-fry in hot ashes until cooked; to obtain a fierce [purgative] effect, dry it raw over a fire.
Inula flower: also called jinfeicao. To use, steam before putting into medicines; in haste it may be unsteamed.
Dichroa root: to use, chop, soak in wine for a day and night and steam before using in medicines.
Arisaema and typhonium: to use, blast-fry in hot ashes until cracked before using in medicines; if there are other methods, follow the formula.
Aristolochia fruit: to use, lightly roast before using in medicines.
Drynaria: to use, scrape off all the yellow skin and hairs with a knife, chop finely, mix with wine, steam for a day, take out and sun-dry. In haste, simply dry over a fire without steaming.
Fenugreek: to use, lightly stir-fry and put into medicines.
Quisqualis: to use, first blast-fry with the husk in hot ashes, then remove the husk, take the kernels, dry over a fire and use in medicines.
Platycodon, euphorbia root, corydalis, lepidium seed, morning-glory seed and the like: lightly stir-fry all before using in medicines.
Sichuan lovage and angelica dahurica: chop and dry over a fire before using in medicines.

## Notes

The Herb section of the processing examples: methods for each herbal drug (e.g., ginseng's rhizome head must be removed or it causes vomiting; polygala's core or it causes oppression; forked saposhnikovia causes madness or chronic illness; typha pollen raw to break blood, fried to stop bleeding; angelica head to supplement blood and tail to break blood; ephedra boiled and skimmed; pinellia washed seven times and processed with ginger).
- Quick methods for emergencies are also given; largely based on the *Leigong paozhi lun* and similar works.
