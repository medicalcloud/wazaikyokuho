# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 072 沈香蓽澄茄散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chenxiang bichengqie san (Aquilaria and Cubeb Powder)

It treats insufficiency of the lower channels with accumulated cold harboured within, bowstring tension of the navel and abdomen with pain drawing into the lower back and spine, a withered yellow complexion, reversal cold of the hands and feet, deficiency fullness of the flanks and ribs, a weary spirit, spontaneous diarrhoea, and slippery frequent urination.

fuzi (aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed) 4 liang　chenxiang (aquilaria)　bichengqie (cubeb)　huluba (fenugreek; lightly stir-fried)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　huixiang (fennel; imported by ship, lightly stir-fried)　buguzhi (psoralea; lightly stir-fried)　bajitian (morinda; core removed)　muxiang (costus root)　chuanlian (toosendan fruit; blast-fried, stones removed; 1 liang each)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; half a liang)　taoren (peach kernel; skin and tips removed, stir-fried with bran; 2 liang)

Make the above together into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one large *zhan* of water and a little powdered salt, decoct down to eight-tenths, remove the dregs and take it fairly hot. It is also effective for all qi pain of the "blind intestine" and small intestine. Take on an empty stomach before meals.

## Notes

Chenxiang bichengqie san (Shaoxing-era additions): for umbilical tension, pain drawing into the back, sallow complexion, cold limbs, flank fullness, fatigue, diarrhoea and frequent urination from insufficiency of the lower channels with internal accumulated cold, and for qi pain of the "blind intestine" and small intestine.
- Aconite, Sichuan aconite, cinnamon, fenugreek, psoralea, morinda and fennel warm the lower origin; aquilaria, cubeb, costus and toosendan move qi and stop pain; peach kernel is added.
