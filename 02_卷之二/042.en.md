# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 042 人参養胃湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Renshen yangwei tang (Ginseng Stomach-Nourishing Decoction)

It treats external contraction of wind-cold with internal damage from raw and cold food, aversion to cold with vigorous fever, clouding and pain of the head and eyes and stiffness of the limbs. Regardless of whether the pattern is wind or cold, or of the distinction between interior and exterior, all can be treated. First have the patient sleep covered with a thick quilt and give several doses of this medicine in succession, assisted by thin gruel and the like, so that the four limbs sweat slightly and moistly. When the sweat has dried, gradually remove the quilt and carefully avoid wind from outside; [the illness] will naturally resolve and disperse. If there was sweating from the start, one should still warm and moisten to harmonize and resolve it; if residual heat remains, regulate it gently with Shensu yin; if headache persists, take Rusheng bingzi with strongly decocted ginger and scallion-white decoction. Once these three patterns are removed, there is no need for medicine; simply regulate eating and drinking and keep appropriately warm or cool, and recovery will come naturally. In general, the reason the ancients did not dare lightly induce sweating in colds is simply that ephedra opens the interstices, and if it is not used appropriately it leads the true qi to leak out, causing deficiency and turning into other patterns. This medicine is a mild prescription that merely warms the middle and releases the exterior without causing undue disturbance. It also wards off miasmic qi of mountain mists, and is especially good taken regularly against seasonal pestilence.

banxia (pinellia; washed seven times in hot water)　houpo (magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, processed with ginger juice)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked overnight in rice-washing water, washed, sliced and stir-fried; 1 liang each)　huoxiang leaves (agastache; washed free of earth)　caoguo (tsaoko; skin and membranes removed)　fuling (poria; black skin removed)　renshen (ginseng; half a liang each)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried; 2½ *qian*)　juhong (red tangerine peel; 7½ *qian*)

Cut the above into pieces. For each dose take 4 *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, seven slices of ginger and one smoked plum, decoct down to six-tenths, remove the dregs and take it hot.

It also transforms malaria arising from food damage to the spleen, and deficiency cold of the spleen, stomach and middle duct with vomiting counterflow and nausea. For cold malaria, cold epidemics and aversion to cold, add aconite in each case; this is Shiwei buhuanjin san (Ten-Ingredient Priceless Powder).

## Notes

Renshen yangwei tang (from here the layer of Chunyou-era additions): for chills and fever, headache and stiffness from external wind-cold combined with internal damage from raw and cold food. As a mild formula without ephedra, it describes in detail how to induce sweating and the after-care (Shensu yin for residual heat, Rusheng bingzi for headache).
- Pingwei san (black atractylodes, magnolia bark, tangerine peel, licorice) with pinellia, poria, agastache, tsaoko and ginseng added. It is also used for malaria from food damage and spleen-stomach deficiency cold; with aconite added it is called Shiwei buhuanjin san.
- Shensu yin is the next formula (juan 2, no. 043); Rusheng bingzi is in juan 3.
