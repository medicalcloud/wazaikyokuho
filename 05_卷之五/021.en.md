# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 021 養気丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yangqi dan (Qi-Nourishing Elixir)

It treats the myriad deficiencies and hundred damages with deficient, exhausted original qi of the spleen, true yang not secured, disharmony of the sanjiao, excess above and deficiency below, phlegm-fluid retention of the middle duct attacking upward with clouding and dizziness of the head and eyes; the eight winds and five impediments; or sudden wind stroke with phlegm surging into the upper diaphragm, halting sluggish speech, a clouded spirit and disordered qi, like paralysis; and running piglet and kidney qi surging up into the chest and abdomen and reaching both flanks, with unbearable distension and stabbing pain. [It treats] yin and yang above and below with qi failing to rise and descend, inability to eat, a dull complexion, swollen limbs, the five kinds of water qi, leg qi surging upward, weary pain of the lower back and spine, intercourse with ghosts in dreams at night and night sweats on waking, cold stomach with heart pain, slippery frequent urination drawing on the lower abdomen, slack, weak feet and knees and difficulty walking. For women's long-standing cold sea of blood with red and white vaginal discharge and no children for years, and for yin-toxin cold damage with a blue face and curled tongue, retracted genitals and difficulty speaking, cold limbs and loss of consciousness, quickly give a hundred pills, pouring them in with ginger and jujube decoction; yang returns at once and the patient never fails to live. Or for exposure to pathogenic cold with vomiting and diarrhea of sudden turmoil, counterflow cold of the hands and feet, all six pulses deep and hidden, blue-black lips and mouth and attacking, stabbing pain of the abdomen and flanks; and men's weak, timid yang, aching legs and knees, empty rumbling of the abdomen and navel and spontaneously slippery stools; and also vexing congestion of the diaphragm and stomach and empty rumbling of phlegm-fluid retention that a hundred medicines have not cured. Taken regularly it assists and nourishes the genuine qi, generates yang and drives out yin, is warm and mild without overstepping, grinds down cold stagnation, digests food, calms the five zang organs, harmonizes the six fu organs, and keeps the hundred illnesses away. When travelling one should carry this medicine and take it in emergencies; it is of great benefit.

yuyuliang (uyoryo) shi (limonite; refined in fire seven times, quenched in vinegar seven times, powdered)　zishiying (murasakisekiei; fluorite; calcined once)　chishizhi (shakusekishi; red halloysite; calcined once; half a jin each)　daizheshi (hematite; calcined seven times, quenched in vinegar seven times, powdered; 1 jin)　cishi (magnetite; calcined ten times, quenched in vinegar ten times; half a jin)

Store these five minerals separately, grind each into a fine powder and then grind it further with water. Ladle off the clear [suspension] onto paper held in a bamboo sieve, so that the fine powder stays on the paper and the water drips through, until it is all ladled off. When dry, put each into a storage jar, seal it with clay mixed with salt water and paper fiber, and dry it in the shade. Divide 50 jin of good hard charcoal into five lots of 10 jin, burn each red to make a furnace, calcine the five medicines in them, and cover with paper ash. After two days, when the fire is out and the ash cold, calcine again; do this three times, then bury them in a pit in the ground for two days to remove the fire toxin, grind again and add the following medicines.

fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; 2 liang)　roucongrong (cistanche; washed clean, soaked overnight in wine, dried over a fire; 1½ liang)　danggui (toki; Chinese angelica; soaked overnight in wine, dried over a fire)　huixiang (fennel; stir-fried)　poguzhi (psoralea; stir-fried in wine until fragrant and cooked)　muxiang (mokko; costus root; not exposed to fire)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　baji (morinda; soaked in salt water, pounded, core removed)　roudoukou (nutmeg; wrapped in flour and roasted)　dingxiang (clove)　shanyao (Chinese yam)　lurong (rokujo; deer antler velvet; roasted with ghee)　bai fuling (bukuryo; white poria; skin removed)　chenxiang (aquilaria)　yuanzhi (onji; polygala; core removed; 1 liang each)

Process each of these according to method, grind together into a powder, and then add:

ruxiang (frankincense; ground separately)　wulingzhi (flying-squirrel droppings; sand removed, ground separately)　moyao (myrrh; sand and grit removed, ground; 1 liang each)

Grind these three together with the other medicines, and then add:

zhusha (cinnabar; calcined or steamed)　yangqishi (yokiseki; actinolite; lightly calcined, or simply boiled in wine)　zhongru powder (stalactite; 1 liang each)

These three are ground separately and added at the time [of compounding].

Grind all the above together, sieve into a fine powder, form pills with a paste of boiled glutinous rice flour, fifty pills to each liang, dry them in the shade, put them in a cloth bag and rub them until glossy. Take five to ten pills each time on an empty stomach, swallowed with warm wine, or with ginger and salt decoction or jujube decoction; women swallow them with mugwort and vinegar decoction.

## Notes

Yangqi dan (from here the layer of Baoqing-era additions): for the myriad deficiencies with exhausted spleen qi and unsecured true yang—dizziness, wind stroke, running piglet, edema, leg qi, back pain, dream intercourse, night sweats, frequent urination and difficulty walking from excess above and deficiency below; for women's cold sea of blood, vaginal discharge and infertility; for yin-toxin cold damage with cold limbs and unconsciousness (a hundred pills given urgently); for sudden turmoil and impotence; and as a travel medicine.
- Five minerals (limonite, fluorite, red halloysite, hematite, magnetite) are calcined, quenched in vinegar, water-ground and calcined three times in charcoal fires to remove fire toxin, in an elaborate procedure; kidney-warming drugs (aconite, deer antler velvet, cistanche, psoralea, morinda, cinnamon), aromatics (aquilaria, costus, clove), frankincense, myrrh, flying-squirrel droppings, cinnabar, actinolite and stalactite are added in stages.
