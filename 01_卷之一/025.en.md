# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 025 川芎丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chuanxiong wan (Sichuan Lovage Pill)

It disperses wind congestion, transforms phlegm and drool, frees the throat and diaphragm and clears the head and eyes. It treats headache with spinning dizziness, palpitation with vexing heat, stiffness of the neck and nape, stiff and weary shoulders and back, vexing pain of the limbs, itching skin, a clouded head and painful eyes, nasal congestion with a heavy voice, and wandering wind on the face like crawling insects.

chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　longnao bohe (borneol mint; leaves, dried over a fire; 75 liang each)　xixin (asarum; washed; 5 liang)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; sprouts removed; 25 liang)　jiegeng (platycodon; 100 liang)　gancao (licorice; scorched; 35 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder, knead with refined honey and divide each 1½ liang into fifty pills. Take one pill each time, chewed thoroughly, with clear *la* tea, after meals and at bedtime.

## Notes

Chuanxiong wan: for headache, dizziness, stiff shoulders and back, nasal congestion and wandering facial wind from wind congestion.
- Sichuan lovage, borneol mint, asarum and saposhnikovia scatter wind from the head; platycodon and licorice free the throat and diaphragm. Its composition is close to the later Chuanxiong chatiao san.
- The Siku quanshu edition gives 50 liang of asarum (see the variants).
