# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 066 活絡丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huoluo dan (Network-Vessel-Quickening Elixir)

It treats men with deficiency of the qi of the original viscus and women with long-standing cold of the spleen blood, with wind evils and damp-toxin qi of all kinds lingering in the channels and network vessels and flowing into the feet and hands, so that the sinews and vessels cramp and the fists clench, or there is red swelling, difficulty walking, heaviness of the lower back and thighs and a cramping pain in the soles; and when [the qi] rushes up into the abdomen and flanks with distension, oppression and blockage of the chest and diaphragm, no desire for food, and oppression and confusion as it rushes the heart; as well as all painful wind running and pouring, with pain all over the body.

chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　caowu (wild aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　dilong (earthworm; earth removed)　tiannanxing (arisaema; blast-fried; 6 liang each)　ruxiang (frankincense; ground)　moyao (myrrh; ground; 2 liang 2 *qian* each)

Make the above into a fine powder, mix in the ground medicines evenly and form pills the size of wutong seeds with wine-and-flour paste. Take twenty pills each time, on an empty stomach and at noon, washed down with cold wine; schizonepeta tea is also fine.

## Notes

Huoluo dan (from here the layer "famous formulas of various schools added by Wu Zhige"): for cramping, swelling and pain of the limbs and difficulty walking from wind and damp toxin lodged in the channels, for leg qi rushing upward, and for all painful wind.
- Sichuan and wild aconite, arisaema and earthworm expel wind-damp and phlegm and open the channels; frankincense and myrrh move the blood and stop pain. This is the formula later called Xiao huoluo dan.
