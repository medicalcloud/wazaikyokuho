# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 043 七棗湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qizao tang (Seven Jujubes Decoction)

It treats weak, deficient spleen and stomach that have received cold qi internally, with pouring diarrhea, water and grain not separating, distension and fullness of the abdomen and flanks, gripping pain of the navel and abdomen, counterflow qi below the heart, deficiency rumbling in the abdomen, vomiting and nausea, stuffiness and oppression of the chest and diaphragm, fatigue with little strength, and no desire for food.

huixiang (fennel; earth removed, stir-fried)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　suosha (amomum; kernels taken; 8 liang each)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, processed with ginger; 1 jin)　yizhi (alpinia oxyphylla; husk removed; half a jin)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried; 4 liang)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; 6 liang)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water and seven large jujubes broken open, decoct together down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm before meals on an empty stomach.

## Notes

Qizao tang: for pouring diarrhea, abdominal and flank distension, gripping umbilical pain, counterflow qi, borborygmus, vomiting, nausea, chest stuffiness, fatigue and poor appetite from weak spleen and stomach with cold qi.
- Sichuan aconite, dried ginger, fennel and alpinia warm; magnolia bark and amomum move qi; licorice is added; decocted with seven jujubes (hence the name).
