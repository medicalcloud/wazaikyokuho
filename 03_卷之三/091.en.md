# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 091 降気湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jiangqi tang (Qi-Lowering Decoction)

It treats discomfort of the middle duct with distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen, congestion and stagnation of yin and yang with qi failing to rise and descend, choking obstruction of the chest and diaphragm, dyspnea and shortness of breath, dry retching with vexing fullness, cough with phlegm and drool, no taste in the mouth, a liking for lying down with reduced eating, chronic cold and prolonged fluid retention accumulating without dispersing, propping binding below the flanks and a constant feeling of oppression. It specifically treats leg qi surging upward with hard fullness of the heart and abdomen, swollen limbs and interference with eating.

zisuye (perilla leaf; stalks removed; 4 liang)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, processed with ginger juice)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed, not exposed to fire)　banxia (hange; pinellia; washed seven times in hot water to remove the slipperiness)　chuan danggui (toki) (Sichuan angelica; rhizome head removed)　qianhu (peucedanum; rhizome head removed, washed)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched; 3 liang each)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed; 3½ liang)

Cut the above into pieces. For each dose take 2 to 3 *qian* with one large *zhan* of water and three slices of fresh ginger, decoct down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm, at any time. Taken regularly it disperses phlegm-fluid retention, scatters stagnant qi and improves eating.

## Notes

Jiangqi tang (bureau additions): for chest obstruction, dyspnea, cough, phlegm and poor appetite from disordered rising and descending of qi, for chronic cold and prolonged fluid retention, and for leg qi surging upward.
- Its composition is close to the earlier Suzi jiangqi tang (Soshikokito) (juan 3, no. 056) with perilla leaf in place of perilla seed.
