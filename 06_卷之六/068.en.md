# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 068 固腸散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Guchang san (Intestine-Securing Powder)

It is suitable for weak, deficient spleen and stomach that have received cold qi internally, with pouring diarrhea, water and grain not separating, disharmony of cold and heat, dysentery with pus and blood, less red than white, or like fish brains, slippery intestines with abdominal pain, very frequent stools, distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen, and reduced eating with little strength.

chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; stir-fried; 20 liang)　muxiang (mokko; costus root; not exposed to fire; 1 liang)　roudoukou (nutmeg; used raw)　yingsuqiao (poppy husk; stalk and cap removed, roasted with honey; 3 liang each)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; 2½ liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of wine, two slices of fresh ginger and one jujube, decoct together down to seven-tenths and take it warm, at any time. For those who do not drink wine, it may be decocted in water. Avoid wine, flour, fishy food and the like.

## Notes

Guchang san (Wu Zhige's additions): for pouring diarrhea, dysentery with pus and blood (more white than red) or like fish brains, abdominal pain, frequent stools, abdominal fullness, poor appetite and weakness from weak spleen and stomach with cold qi and disharmony of cold and heat.
- Mainly aged tangerine peel; poppy husk and nutmeg astringe; dried ginger warms; costus and licorice are added; decocted in wine.
