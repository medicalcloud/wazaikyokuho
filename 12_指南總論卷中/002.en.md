# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷中 002 論諸風之由 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the origins of the various winds. Wind stroke always arises from disharmony of yin and yang, bias of the qi of the zang-fu organs, loss of measure of the ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi, confusion of blood and qi, excessive injury by joy and anger, immoderate eating and drinking and unrestrained desires, so that the meridians are either empty or blocked and the body is deficient with interstices not tight. When the qi of pathogenic wind strikes a person, the onset is sudden: for loss of consciousness, tidal surging of drool with clouded, blocked consciousness and a stiff tongue unable to speak, first give Tongguan san (Tsukansan; Pass-Opening Powder) to sniff into the nose, then Zhibao dan (Shihotan; Supreme Jewel Elixir). This medicine is cool in nature; it may be given to fairly robust people, but must not be given to those with qi deficiency or the elderly, who should be given only the following medicines. For sudden wind stroke with taut sinews and dizziness, Qibao dan (Seven Treasures Elixir) may be given. For wind stroke with hemiplegia, deviated mouth and eyes, hypertonic sinews and bone pain, give Xiao xuming tang (Shozokumeito; Minor Life-Prolonging Decoction) or Zhuifeng yingtong wan. For wind stroke with pathogenic qi entering the zang organs, manic speech and abstraction, give Paifeng tang (Wind-Expelling Decoction). For wind stroke with paralysis of the hands and feet, mostly give Qingzhou bai wanzi (Qingzhou White Pills). For wind stroke with stiff neck and back and clenched jaw, give Sanwuqi san (Three-Five-Seven Powder). For wind stroke with trembling hands and feet and slack, weak lower back and legs, give Huoluo dan (Katsurakutan; Network-Vessel-Quickening Elixir) or Qibao dan. For the elderly with weak legs, give Huangqi wan (Ogigan; Astragalus Pill).

## Notes

On the origins of the various winds: causes of wind stroke (disharmony of yin and yang, biased visceral qi, disordered ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi and blood, excessive emotion, diet and desires leading to empty or blocked meridians and loose interstices) and medicines by symptom (for collapse, phlegm and stiff tongue, sniffing Tongguan san then Zhibao dan, but not Zhibao dan for the deficient or elderly; Qibao dan for taut sinews and dizziness; Xiao xuming tang and Zhuifeng yingtong wan for hemiplegia; Paifeng tang for manic speech; Qingzhou bai wanzi for paralysis; Sanwuqi san for stiff neck and clenched jaw; Huoluo dan for trembling; Huangqi wan for weak legs in the elderly).
- All the formulas named are found in this book, mainly in juan 1, "Treating the various winds".
