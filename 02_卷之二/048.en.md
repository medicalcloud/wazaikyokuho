# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 048 葱白散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Congbai san (Sohakusan; Scallion Powder)

It resolves cold damage in any season: headache and vigorous fever, stiffness of the neck and back, vexing pain of the joints, aversion to cold and wind, weary limbs, irregular stools, red, rough urine, vomiting counterflow, vexing thirst and no desire for food. It also treats wind damage and contraction of cold with headache, fever, nasal congestion with a heavy voice and cough with phlegm and drool, and miasmic qi of mountain mists and seasonal pestilence.

chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked in rice-washing water)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes; 2 liang each)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched)　shigao (sekko; gypsum; calcined)　gange (dried pueraria; dried over a fire; 1 liang each)　mahuang (mao; ephedra; roots and nodes removed; 3 liang)

Make the above medicines into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, three slices of ginger and two *cun* of scallion white, decoct down to seven-tenths and take it hot, at any time. To induce sweating, decoct three doses together, cover with bedding, until sweat appears.

## Notes

Congbai san (Wu Zhige's additions): for headache, vigorous fever, neck stiffness, joint pain and chills in seasonal cold damage (colds), for vomiting and thirst, for cough and nasal congestion from wind damage, and for miasma and epidemics.
- Ephedra, pueraria and lovage release the exterior; gypsum clears heat; the two atractylodes remove damp. It is decocted with scallion white.
