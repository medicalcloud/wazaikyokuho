# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 065 左経丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zuojing wan (Channel-Assisting Pill)

It treats diseases of the sinews and bones with loss of control of the hands and feet and inability to walk or move. Provided the sinews and vessels have not been injured by acupuncture or moxibustion, four or five pills will certainly work. This medicine is especially able to circulate the nutritive and defensive qi and guide the channels and network vessels; it specifically treats the three channels of the heart, kidney and liver. A slight dribbling difficulty of urination after taking it is the sign of its effect. (Another formula [of the same name] appears later.)

mubiezi (momordica seed; shell removed, ground separately)　baijiaoxiang (liquidambar resin; ground)　wulingzhi (flying-squirrel droppings)　caowutou (wild aconite; raw, skin and navel removed; 3½ liang each)　danggui (Chinese angelica; earth removed; 1 liang)　banmao (blister beetles; 100, heads, legs and wings removed, roasted until cooked with a little vinegar)

Powder the last four ingredients and mix them evenly with the first two; take 1 jin of skinned black soybeans pounded raw into flour, boil it with vinegar into a paste, knead the medicine with it and form pills the size of a euryale seed. Take one pill each time ground in wine.

## Notes

Zuojing wan (Chunyou-era additions): for loss of limb control and inability to walk from diseases of the sinews and bones—though said not to work if the sinews have been injured by acupuncture or moxibustion.
- Highly toxic drugs (wild aconite, momordica seed, blister beetle) open the channels, while liquidambar resin, flying-squirrel droppings and angelica move the blood. Dribbling urination after taking it is the sign of effect (probably an action of the blister beetle).
- The Siku quanshu edition reads 小便快利 ("urination flows freely") instead (see the variants). Another formula of the same name appears later.
