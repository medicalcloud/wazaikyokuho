# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 023 牛黄金虎丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Niuhuang (goo) jinhu dan (Bezoar Golden Tiger Elixir)

It treats acute wind stroke with rigidity of the body and back, clenched mouth and loss of voice, cramping of the sinews and vessels, dry nose and black face, vigorous fever over the whole body, oily sweat, staring eyes and blue lips, confusion and oppression of the heart-spirit, a body as if drunk, congestion of phlegm and drool, and a sound like sawing in the chest, diaphragm and throat.

tianxiong (tenyu; aconite tuber; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; 12½ liang)　baifan (alum; calcined)　tianzhuhuang (tabasheer; ground)　tiannanxing (arisaema; washed in hot water, dried over a fire and powdered, kneaded into cakes with ox bile and dried over a fire until cooked; if there is no ox bile, steam it in prepared wine for seven days and nights)　nifen (calomel; ground; 25 liang each)　niuhuang (bezoar; ground; 2½ liang)　raw longnao (borneol; ground; 5 liang)　gold leaf (800 leaves, for coating)　xionghuang (yuo; realgar; ground and water-ground; 150 liang)

Powder the above, knead with refined honey, make ten pills from each 1½ liang and coat with gold leaf. Dissolve one pill in freshly drawn water and pour it in; prop the patient up in a sitting position so that the medicine moves and dissolves. After a while dissolve another pill in fresh mint juice and pour it in; recovery is immediate. People who are stout but constitutionally deficient, with much drool and a tendency to wind, should always carry this medicine for emergencies. If one suddenly feels darkness before the eyes, oppression and confusion of the heart and diaphragm, and drool with a tendency to fall, and there is no time to dissolve the medicine, quickly chew one pill and take it with freshly drawn water. For children's acute fright wind, a child of one year takes one pill the size of a mung bean dissolved in fresh mint juice and poured in, adjusted at the time according to age. Pregnant women must not take it.

## Notes

Niuhuang jinhu dan: for acute wind stroke with rigidity, clenched mouth, high fever, oily sweat and rattling phlegm.
- It uses a large amount of realgar, expels phlegm with aconite, alum, tabasheer, arisaema and calomel, and opens the orifices with bezoar and borneol; stout people with much drool are advised to carry it; contraindicated in pregnancy.
- The base text reads 口禁 for 口噤 ("clenched mouth"); since the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions both read 口噤, it has been emended (see the emendation).
