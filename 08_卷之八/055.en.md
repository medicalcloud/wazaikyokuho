# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之八 055 烏蛇膏 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wushe gao (Black Snake Paste)

It treats all wind swellings: toxic qi of wind evil lodging in the skin from outside, steaming and breaking out into swellings that arise in no fixed place, wandering back and forth with intermittent itching or pain; or exuberant wind toxin attacking and pouring into sores, red, hot and with much pus, with tight edges.

wuzhuyu (evodia)　gaoben (Chinese lovage)　duhuo (pubescent angelica)　xixin (asarum)　bai jiangcan (silkworm; silk and mouth removed, stir-fried)　banxia (pinellia)　shujiao (Sichuan pepper; seeds removed, stir-fried)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia)　chi shaoyao (red peony root)　danggui (Chinese angelica)　guixin (cinnamon heart)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　xiang baizhi (fragrant angelica dahurica; half a liang each)　wushe (black-striped snake)　huangla (yellow beeswax; 2 liang each)　ganxie (dried scorpion)　fuzi (aconite; skin and tip removed; 1 liang each)

Chop the above finely and decoct over a gentle fire in 2 jin of refined lard from the twelfth month, until the angelica dahurica turns red-black; strain off the dregs through silk floss, add the wax and store in a porcelain vessel. Each time take a little and rub it in until hot, three times a day.

## Notes

Wushe gao (Wu Zhige's additions): an ointment rubbed in for wandering, itchy or painful swellings from wind-evil toxin, red, purulent sores from exuberant wind toxin, and all wind swellings.
- Black-striped snake, scorpion, silkworm, pubescent angelica, Chinese lovage, asarum, saposhnikovia, angelica dahurica and lovage dispel wind; evodia, Sichuan pepper, aconite and cinnamon warm; angelica, red peony and pinellia are added; made with twelfth-month lard and yellow wax.
