# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 044 大七香丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Da qixiang wan (Major Seven Fragrances Pill)

It treats men and women with cold of the original qi of the spleen and deficient, deficient stomach qi, no desire for food, choking obstruction of the heart and diaphragm gradually becoming diaphragm qi, spleen diarrhea and dysentery, stabbing and pouring qi, drunkenness with vomiting of wine, cold strings and stomach reflux, and vomiting and diarrhea of sudden turmoil.

xiangfuzi (cyperus; stir-fried; 192 liang)　mainie (barley sprouts; stir-fried; 100 liang)　dingxiangpi (clove bark; 330 liang)　suoshaner (amomum)　huoxiang (agastache; leaves; 250 liang each)　gansong (nardostachys)　wuyao (lindera; 64 liang each)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; stir-fried)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed, washed; 250 liang each)

Powder the above and form pills the size of a pellet with refined honey. Chew one pill each time and take it with salted wine or salt water. For women's spleen blood qi with irregular menses, chew it with stir-fried ginger wine; vinegar decoction is also fine; there is great divine effect. Avoid raw, cold, fatty and greasy foods.

## Notes

Da qixiang wan (Shaoxing-era additions): for poor appetite, chest obstruction (diaphragm qi), diarrhea, stabbing qi pain, vomiting after drinking, cold strings, stomach reflux and sudden turmoil from cold and deficiency of the spleen and stomach, and for women's blood qi and irregular menses.
- Cyperus, clove bark, agastache, amomum, nardostachys, lindera and tangerine peel move qi; cinnamon warms; barley sprouts digest food.
