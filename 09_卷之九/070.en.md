# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 070 調中湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Tiaozhong tang (Middle-Regulating Decoction)

It should be taken urgently for postpartum deficiency and timidity of the intestines and stomach invaded by pathogenic cold, and when, before the full month, drinking cold things and exposure to wind allow [pathogen] to take advantage of the deficiency to assail and lodge in the membranes, scattering into the abdomen and flanks, with abdominal pain coming in bouts; or pain as if stabbed by an awl or knife, flowing into the large intestine, with undigested water and grain, rushing diarrhea and borborygmus; or red and white discharge with swelling distension of the flanks; or wandering pain without fixed place.

danggui (toki; Chinese angelica)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　bai shaoyao (shakuyaku; white peony root)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried)　liangjiang (lesser galangal; 1 liang each)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; half a liang)

Cut the above into a powder. For each dose take three spoonfuls (*qian bi*) with three *zhan* of water, decoct down to one *zhan*, remove the dregs and take it hot.

## Notes

Tiaozhong tang (bureau additions): for bouts of abdominal pain, stabbing pain, diarrhea, borborygmus, red and white stools, flank distension and wandering pain when pathogenic cold enters postpartum deficient intestines and stomach, or after cold drinks and exposure to wind within the first month.
- Angelica, lovage and white peony harmonize the blood; aconite, cinnamon and lesser galangal warm; licorice is added.
