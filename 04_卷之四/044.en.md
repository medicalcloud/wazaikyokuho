# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之四 044 四七湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Siqi tang (Four-Seven Decoction)

It treats the qi of joy, anger, grief, thought, worry, fear and fright binding into phlegm and drool, shaped like torn floss or like a plum stone, lodged in the throat so that it cannot be coughed out nor swallowed down; this is caused by the seven qi. It is also suitable for glomus and fullness of the middle duct with unsmooth qi, or congested exuberant phlegm and drool with rising qi and urgent panting, or phlegm-rheum binding in the middle with vomiting counterflow and nausea. (From the *Yijian fang*.)

banxia (pinellia; 5 liang)　fuling (poria; 4 liang)　zisuye (perilla leaf; 2 liang)　houpo (magnolia bark; 3 liang)

Cut the above into pieces. For each dose take 4 *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, seven slices of fresh ginger and one jujube, decoct down to six-tenths, remove the dregs and take it hot, at any time. If excessive thought has left yin and yang unseparated and the clear and turbid interfering with each other, with white turbid urine, taking Qingzhou baiwanzi with this medicine is most apt. It is especially suitable for women's morning sickness. Also called Houpo banxia tang (Magnolia Bark and Pinellia Decoction), or Da qiqi tang (Major Seven Qi Decoction). The *Ju fang* has a Qiqi tang using 5 liang of pinellia and 1 liang each of ginseng, official cinnamon and licorice, decocted with ginger, which greatly treats the seven qi and wringing pain of the heart and abdomen; but its taste is too sweet, and it may not necessarily stop pain and smooth qi. One formula, for damage by the seven emotions with discomfort of the middle duct, qi failing to rise and descend, and distension and fullness of the abdomen and flanks, uses half a jin of stir-fried cyperus, 6 liang of red tangerine peel and 1 liang of licorice, decocted; it is especially good. Those fond of novelties say it depletes qi, but that is not so: when one has this illness, one takes this medicine.

## Notes

Siqi tang (bureau additions; from the *Yijian fang*; the last formula of juan 4): for "plum-stone qi"—phlegm formed by bound emotional qi, felt in the throat like torn floss or a plum stone that can be neither coughed up nor swallowed (corresponding to what is now called globus sensation)—and for epigastric glomus, phlegmy panting, vomiting from phlegm-rheum, turbid urine from excessive thought and morning sickness.
- Pinellia, poria, perilla leaf and magnolia bark with ginger and jujube: the same composition as Banxia houpo tang in the chapter on women's miscellaneous diseases of the *Jingui yaolüe*; still widely used today as Banxia houpo tang.
- It records the view that the *Ju fang*'s Qiqi tang (juan 3, no. 020) is too sweet to relieve pain, and a formula of cyperus, red tangerine peel and licorice, rebutting the criticism that it depletes qi (text of the *Yijian fang*).
