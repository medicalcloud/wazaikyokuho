# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 056 追風散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zhuifeng san (Wind-Chasing Powder)

It treats one-sided and general headache, whether of many years or recent. It also treats long-standing liver deficiency with weakened blood and qi and wind-toxin qi attacking upward, with headache, dizziness and spinning eyes, palpitation with vexing heat, aching pain in all the joints, a clouded head and painful eyes, nasal congestion with a heavy voice, stiffness of the neck and back, itching skin, wandering wind on the face like crawling insects, and all head wind; and in addition women's blood wind attacking and pouring with clouding and pain of the head and eyes. It treats all of these. Taken regularly it clears the head and eyes, frees the throat and diaphragm, disperses wind congestion and transforms phlegm and drool. (Another formula [of the same name] appears later.)

chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin, navel and tips removed)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; rhizome head and forks removed)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage; washed)　bai jiangcan (silkworm; silk and mouth removed, lightly stir-fried)　jingjie (schizonepeta; stalks removed)　shigao (gypsum; calcined until crumbly, ground)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried; 1 liang each)　baifuzi (typhonium; blast-fried)　qianghuo (notopterygium; rhizome head removed, washed, chopped)　quanxie (whole scorpion; tail stings removed, lightly stir-fried)　baizhi (angelica dahurica)　tiannanxing (arisaema; blast-fried)　tianma (gastrodia; rhizome head removed)　dilong (earthworm; earth removed, roasted; half a liang)　ruxiang (frankincense; ground)　caowu (wild aconite; blast-fried, skin and tips removed)　moyao (myrrh; finely ground)　xionghuang (realgar; finely ground; 1 *fen* each)

Make the above into a fine powder. Take half a *qian* each time mixed with a little good tea, after meals and at bedtime.

## Notes

Zhuifeng san (Baoqing-era additions): for one-sided and general headache old or new, headache, dizziness, neck stiffness and facial wandering wind from wind toxin rising on liver deficiency, and for women's blood-wind headache.
- The two aconites, arisaema, typhonium, scorpion, silkworm and earthworm expel wind-phlegm; lovage, notopterygium, angelica dahurica, schizonepeta and saposhnikovia scatter wind from the head; gypsum restrains heat.
- Another formula of the same name appears later in juan 1.
