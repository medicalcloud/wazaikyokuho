# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 019 大順散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Dashun san (Great Smoothing Powder)

It treats being affected by summerheat with latent heat, drinking too much, so that the spleen and stomach receive damp, water and grain are not separated, the clear and turbid interfere with each other, and the qi of yin and yang counterflows, with sudden turmoil (huoluan), vomiting and disharmony of the viscera and bowels.

gancao (licorice; cut into one-*cun* lengths; 30 jin)　ganjiang (dried ginger)　xingren (apricot kernel; skin and tips removed, stir-fried)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed, roasted; 4 jin [each])

First stir-fry the licorice in white sand until it is about eight-tenths yellow and cooked; then add the dried ginger and stir-fry together until the ginger cracks; then add the apricot kernels and stir-fry together again until they stop crackling. Sieve off the sand, then add the cinnamon, and pound and sieve everything together into a powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one medium *zhan* of water, decoct down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm. If there is vexation and agitation, mix it in well-flower water. At any time. It may also be taken stirred into boiling water.

## Notes

Dashun san: for sudden turmoil (vomiting and diarrhoea) when excessive cold drinking in hot weather has damaged the spleen and stomach with damp and cold—a warming formula for illness from cold in summer rather than from heat itself.
- A large amount of licorice, with dried ginger and apricot kernels stir-fried in white sand, and cinnamon; the order of sand-frying is described.
