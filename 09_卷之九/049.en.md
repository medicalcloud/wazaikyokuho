# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 049 琥珀黒散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Hupo hei san (Kohakukokusan; Amber Black Powder)

It treats all illnesses of parturient women: before delivery, a dead fetus; difficult, transverse and breech labor. After delivery, retained placenta, the cord broken first, pain of the whole body, dry mouth and oppressed heart, and sudden loss of speech. [It also treats] cases where blood dizziness with blurred vision is mistaken for dark wind; alternating fever and chills mistaken for malaria; puffy swelling of the limbs mistaken for water qi; manic, confused speech with visions of ghosts and spirits mistaken for pathogen possession; distension and fullness of the abdomen and flanks with unremitting vomiting mistaken for stomach reflux; constipated, rough stools and blood in the urine mistaken for the five stranguries. And when the lochia is not fully discharged and the menses have not yet returned, and daily life and diet are not kept within the prohibitions, blood-qi illness gathers into lumps or scatters and surges upward, with hurried breathing and heart pain, cough with much spittle, deficiency heat of the limbs, fright in sleep and night sweats, failing signs of flooding, stabbing pain around the navel, or a red face turning into steaming bone disorder—all should take much of it. Postpartum nosebleed, blackening of the mouth and nose, qi rising in the throat with dyspnea, and wind stroke with clenched jaw are all hard to treat, and this must be taken urgently. In general, taking one or two doses before delivery calms the spirit and smooths the fetus. Even without illness after childbirth, taking one or two doses within seven days disperses the various illnesses. If fright and fear produce other patterns, it should be taken continuously until effective.

hupo (amber; ground separately)　zhusha (cinnabar; ground separately)　baicaoshuang (pot soot; ground separately)　Xinluo bai fuzi (bushi; Silla typhonium tuber; blast-fried)　songmo (pine-soot ink; burned)　heiyi (black clothing, i.e. kitchen dust)　xuemao hui (blood-cat ash, i.e. carp scales, burned and powdered; half a liang each)　shexiang (jako; musk; ground)　chuan danggui (toki; Sichuan angelica; rhizome head removed)　bai jiangcan (silkworm; stir-fried, silk and mouth removed; 1 *fen* each)

Powder the above. Take 2 *qian* each time mixed in stir-fried ginger and warm wine with a boy's urine, before meals.

## Notes

Hupo hei san (Wu Zhige's additions): for all illnesses of parturient women (dead fetus, difficult, transverse or breech labor, retained placenta, body pain, dry mouth, loss of speech), listing postpartum blood disorders easily mistaken for dark wind, malaria, water qi, possession, stomach reflux or strangury; also for disorders from neglect while the lochia persists, and urgently for postpartum nosebleed, blackened mouth and nose, dyspnea and clenched jaw; prophylactic use before and after delivery is recommended.
- Black drugs—amber, cinnabar, pot soot, pine-soot ink, kitchen dust and carp-scale ash—with typhonium, silkworm, musk and angelica.
