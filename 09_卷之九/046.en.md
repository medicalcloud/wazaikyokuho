# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 046 済危上丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jiwei shang dan (Danger-Relieving Superior Elixir)

(From the twenty-first discussion of the *Baoqing ji*.) It discusses excessive discharge after childbirth with extreme deficiency producing wind. Women are governed by ying-nutrient blood; when childbirth causes too much blood loss, qi has nothing to govern, so that the lips turn green, the flesh is cold and sweat comes out, the eyes close and the spirit is clouded, and life hangs by a thread. Wind medicines must not be used by mistake; this should be taken urgently.

Taiyin xuanjing (selenite)　wulingzhi (flying squirrel droppings; sand and stones removed)　liuhuang (sulfur; old, red-colored pieces)　ruxiang (frankincense; ground)

Take equal parts of these four ingredients, stir-fry over a gentle fire until they bind into granules, and grind extremely fine.

sangjisheng (mulberry mistletoe; it must be genuine)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed, cleaned, then weighed)　ejiao (akyo; donkey-hide gelatin; stir-fried with clamshell powder)　juanbai (selaginella; roots removed, used raw)

Take equal parts of these four ingredients; when prepared, dry them over a fire and powder.

Grind all eight ingredients together, knead with raw rehmannia juice, pound a thousand times and form pills the size of wutong seeds. Take twenty pills with warm wine or angelica wine, before meals.

## Notes

Jiwei shang dan (from here the layer of Wu Zhige's additions): citing the twenty-first discussion of the *Baoqing ji*, for extreme deficiency producing "wind" after heavy postpartum bleeding (green lips, cold flesh, sweating, closed eyes, clouded consciousness): wind medicines must not be used, and this is to be given urgently.
- Selenite, flying squirrel droppings, sulfur and frankincense fried into granules, plus mulberry mistletoe, tangerine peel, donkey-hide gelatin and selaginella, made into pills with raw rehmannia juice.
