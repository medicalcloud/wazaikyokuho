# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 004 柴胡石膏散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chaihu shigao san (Saikosekkosan; Bupleurum and Gypsum Powder)

It treats seasonal pestilence with vigorous fever and aversion to wind, headache and body pain, nasal congestion and dry throat, a feeling of fullness in the heart and chest, alternating fever and chills, phlegm-excess cough and thick sticky mucus and spittle.

chi shaoyao (shakuyaku; red peony root)　chaihu (saiko; bupleurum; sprouts removed)　qianhu (peucedanum; sprouts removed)　shigao (sekko; gypsum; calcined)　gange (dried pueraria; 50 liang each)　shengma (shoma; cimicifuga; 25 liang)　huangqin (ogon; scutellaria)　sangbaipi (mulberry root bark; 37½ liang each)　jingjie spikes (schizonepeta; earth removed; 37 liang)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, three slices of fresh ginger and a dozen or so fermented soybeans, decoct together down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it fairly hot. Children divide it into three doses, adjusting further according to size. At any time.

## Notes

Chaihu shigao san: for seasonal pestilence with vigorous fever, aversion to wind, headache, body pain, alternating fever and chills and productive cough.
- Bupleurum, peucedanum, pueraria, cimicifuga and schizonepeta release the exterior; gypsum, scutellaria and mulberry bark clear lung and stomach heat; red peony cools the blood. It is decocted with fermented soybeans.
