# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 078 経進地仙丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jingjin dixian dan (Earth Immortal Elixir Presented to the Throne)

It treats men's five taxations and seven damages, with deficient, exhausted kidney qi, deficient essence and spirit, difficulty walking, tasteless food, dim eyes and parched ears, a dark sallow complexion and withered, dry skin; and women's deficient, cold sea of blood, irregular menstruation, cold zang organs with few children, and foulness of the lower parts. It also treats all hemorrhoidal and fistula sores, rectal bleeding from intestinal wind, and all wind and qi disorders.

renshen (ninjin; ginseng)　huangqi (ogi; astragalus; 1½ liang each)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried)　chuanjiao (Sichuan pepper; seeds and unopened ones removed, lightly stir-fried to sweat out the oil)　congrong (cistanche; soaked in wine and dried over a fire; 4 liang each)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; white)　gancao (kanzo; licorice)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes; 1 liang each)　tusizi (toshishi; dodder seed; soaked in wine)　fupenzi (rubus fruit)　tiannanxing (arisaema; washed in hot water, processed with ginger juice and dried over a fire)　fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia; rhizome head removed)　baifuzi (typhonium)　heshouwu (fleeceflower root; 2 liang each)　niuxi (goshitsu; achyranthes; rhizome head removed, soaked in wine for two nights; 4 liang)　gouji (kuseki; cibotium; hairs removed)　chixiaodou (sekishozu; rice bean)　gusuibu (drynaria; hairs removed)　wuyao (lindera)　qianghuo (notopterygium)　bixie (dioscorea hypoglauca; 2 liang each)　mubiezi (momordica seed; shell removed)　dilong (earthworm; earth removed; 3 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with flour paste boiled in wine. Take thirty pills each time, increasing to forty, swallowed with warm wine on an empty stomach. (This formula was entered into the *Daozang jing* by Tao Yinju, who says: At that time a certain man's mother had contracted a wind-qi disease in childhood; later she suffered attacks of cramping, binding, pain and numbness, and for a long time could not get up. A hundred treatments failed, and she lay in bed for more than fifty years until her fat and flesh were wasted away and only sinews and bones remained. Then [her son] obtained this formula from a lay devotee, compounded it according to the formula and gave her two doses a day. After only some five hundred doses the mother's illness suddenly vanished; her white hair turned black again and lost teeth grew back. At eighty her complexion was like that of a twenty-year-old, her strength was doubled and her hearing and sight were sharp. At that time an old servant used to steal and take the medicine; in the depth of winter he wore thin hemp clothes and walked on frost and snow without showing cold, carried loads twice as heavy as usual and walked as if flying. People suspected he was possessed by a ghost, so they beat him to death and buried him in the sand by the water. After a long time he caused strange happenings again, and the local custom says: "Whenever a slave dies and becomes a ghost, just break his shins and he cannot move." So they dug him up and broke his shins, and saw that his bones were solid throughout and golden yellow in color; breaking his arms showed the same. Its effect is truly extraordinary. Yinju says: had this servant not been beaten to death, he would have become an earth immortal.)

## Notes

Jingjin dixian dan (bureau additions): a large tonifying formula for men's five taxations and seven damages and kidney-qi deficiency, women's cold sea of blood, irregular menses and infertility, hemorrhoids and fistulas, rectal bleeding, and all wind and qi disorders.
- Ginseng, astragalus, atractylodes, poria and licorice supplement qi; aconite, Sichuan pepper, cistanche, dodder, rubus, achyranthes and cibotium warm and supplement the kidneys; Sichuan aconite, arisaema, saposhnikovia, typhonium, notopterygium, dioscorea, momordica seed and earthworm dispel wind.
- The bracketed passage is an original note giving the legend that Tao Hongjing (Tao Yinju) included it in the Daoist Canon, explaining the name "earth immortal". It is legendary and cannot be treated as fact.
