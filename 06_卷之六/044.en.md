# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 044 胃風湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Weifeng tang (Stomach Wind Decoction)

It is suitable for adults and children in whom wind-cold, taking advantage of deficiency, has entered and lodged in the intestines and stomach, with undigested water and grain, pouring diarrhea, deficiency fullness of the abdomen and flanks, borborygmus with gripping pain; and for damp toxin of the intestines and stomach with discharge like bean juice or of static blood, without limit day and night.

baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes)　xiongqiong (Sichuan lovage)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; rhizome head removed)　bai shaoyao (shakuyaku; white peony root)　danggui (toki; Chinese angelica; shoots removed)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed; equal parts)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one large *zhan* of water and a hundred-odd grains of millet, decoct together down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it fairly hot on an empty stomach. For children reduce the dose according to their strength.

## Notes

Weifeng tang: for indigestion, pouring diarrhea, abdominal and flank fullness, borborygmus and gripping pain when wind-cold enters the intestines and stomach through deficiency, and for discharge like bean juice or of static blood from damp toxin of the intestines and stomach. Still sometimes used today for chronic diarrhea and rectal bleeding.
- Bazhen tang without prepared rehmannia and licorice, plus cinnamon (ginseng, atractylodes, poria, angelica, lovage, peony, cinnamon); decocted with millet.
