# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之四 025 丁香五套丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Dingxiang wutao wan (Clove Five-Set Pill)

It treats weak stomach qi and blocked, rough triple burner unable to diffuse and move water and grain, which therefore become phlegm-rheum binding and gathering between the chest and diaphragm, causing clouding and dizziness of the head and eyes, distension and fullness of the chest and diaphragm, cough with urgent breathing, vomiting and abdominal pain. When it lurks in the middle duct it also causes pain in the arms so that they cannot be raised, and heaviness of the lower back and thighs. If it does not disperse for a long time it flows into the spleen; the spleen hates damp, and on receiving water it swells; when swollen it cannot digest water and grain, and the abdomen becomes empty-full and one cannot eat. This medicine governs it.

nanxing (arisaema; each piece cut into a dozen or so lumps, soaked together with the pinellia in water for three days, changing the water daily; then 2 liang of alum crushed and stirred into the water, soaked another three days, washed clean and dried over a fire)　banxia (pinellia; cut open; 2 liang each)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried)　baizhu (white atractylodes)　liangjiang (galangal)　fuling (poria; 1 liang each)　dingxiang (clove; not exposed to fire)　muxiang (costus root)　qingpi (green tangerine peel)　chenpi (aged tangerine peel; white pith removed; half a liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder; grind 1 liang of medicated leaven and 2 liang of barley sprouts together into powder, make a paste of it, knead the medicine and form pills the size of wutong seeds. Take fifty to seventy pills each time with warm boiled water, at any time. Taken regularly it warms the spleen and stomach, removes chronic cold, disperses lingering stagnation, transforms food and drink, and wards off fog and dew, wind and cold, miasmic pestilence of mountain mists and improper unseasonable qi. For wine aggregation and lodged rheum with phlegm and water not dispersing, when repeated decoctions have had no effect, taking it works like a charm.

## Notes

Dingxiang wutao wan (Chunyou-era additions): for dizziness, chest distension, cough, vomiting and abdominal pain from phlegm-rheum formed when weak stomach qi and a blocked triple burner fail to move water and grain, for arm pain and heavy legs when it lurks in the middle duct, and for abdominal fullness and inability to eat when it flows into the spleen.
- Arisaema and pinellia processed in alum water transform phlegm; dried ginger, galangal and clove warm; atractylodes and poria remove damp; costus and the tangerine peels move qi; pills are made with a paste of medicated leaven and barley sprouts.
