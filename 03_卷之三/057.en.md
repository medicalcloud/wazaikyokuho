# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 057 安中散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Anzhong san (Anchusan; Middle-Calming Powder)

It treats spleen pain and stomach reflux, whether of many years or recent, with vomiting of sour water, cold pathogenic qi lingering and stagnating within, lodged accumulations not dispersing, distension and fullness of the chest and diaphragm, attacking and stabbing in the abdomen and flanks, nausea and vomiting, a yellow face and wasted flesh, and weary limbs. It also treats women's stabbing blood-qi pain and heavy pain attacking and pouring from the lower abdomen into the lower back.

xuanhusuo (corydalis; skin removed)　liangjiang (galangal; stir-fried)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried)　huixiang (fennel; stir-fried)　rougui (cinnamon bark; 5 liang each)　muli (borei; oyster shell; calcined; 4 liang)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; stir-fried; 10 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder. Take 2 *qian* each time mixed in hot wine; women take it mixed in weak vinegar decoction. Those who do not drink wine take it stirred into salt water. All at any time.

## Notes

Anzhong san (Baoqing-era additions): for old or recent spleen (stomach) pain, stomach reflux and acid regurgitation, chest distension, stabbing pain of the abdomen and flanks, nausea and emaciation from lodged pathogenic cold, and for women's blood-qi pain from the lower abdomen to the lower back. It is the source of the Anzhong san still used for stomach pain and heartburn.
- Corydalis, galangal, dried ginger, fennel and cinnamon warm and stop pain; oyster shell checks acid; licorice harmonizes. The modern Anzhong san usually has amomum in place of dried ginger.
