# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 067 塩煎散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yanjian san (Salt-Decocted Powder)

It treats all cold qi in men and women attacking and surging into the chest and flanks, pain from the front and back of the chest through the spine, with stiffness when turning the neck; or deficiency cold of the spleen and stomach with no desire for food, occasional vomiting, cramps of sudden turmoil, cold pain of the navel and abdomen and unceasing diarrhea; and labor-like stabbing pain of the bladder, hanging small-intestine qi, and pain of the inner and outer kidneys (scrotum). It also treats women's stabbing blood-qi pain, blood accumulations and blood conglomerations with griping pain around the navel. Another formula appears later.

caoguo ren (tsaoko seed; skin removed, roasted)　suosha (amomum; shells removed, kernels taken)　binglang (areca; blast-fried, chopped)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed)　roudoukou (nutmeg; roasted)　qianghuo (notopterygium; rhizome head removed)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked for two nights in rice-washing water)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　bichengqie (cubeb)　zhiqiao (bitter orange; pulp removed, stir-fried with bran)　liangjiang (galangal; stir-fried in oil)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed)　damaiya (barley sprouts; stir-fried)　huixiang (fennel; stir-fried)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage; washed, chopped)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched; 2 liang each)

Mill the above medicines into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water and one *zi* of salt, decoct together down to eight-tenths and take it on an empty stomach before meals.

## Notes

Yanjian san (Baoqing-era additions): for chest, flank and back pain from cold qi, for poor appetite, vomiting, sudden turmoil, cramps, abdominal pain and diarrhea from spleen-stomach deficiency cold, for bladder pain, small-intestine qi and scrotal pain, and for women's blood-qi and blood-accumulation pain.
- Tsaoko, amomum, nutmeg, cubeb, galangal and fennel warm; black atractylodes, magnolia bark, tangerine peel and poria remove damp; areca and bitter orange send qi down; notopterygium and lovage are added; decocted with salt. Another formula of the same name appears later.
