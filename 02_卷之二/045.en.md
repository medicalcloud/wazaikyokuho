# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 045 対金飲子 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Duijin yinzi (Worth-Its-Weight-in-Gold Drink)

There is no illness it does not cure. Taken regularly it secures the original yang, augments qi, strengthens the spleen and improves eating, harmonizes the stomach and expels phlegm, so that the ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi are naturally regulated and free-flowing and cold and heat cannot invade. This medicine is extremely effective for cold damage in any season.

houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; skin removed, roasted with ginger juice)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked overnight in rice-washing water)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; 2 liang each)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed, stir-fried yellow; half a jin)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 3 *qian* on an empty stomach with one *zhan* of water and two coin-slices of ginger, brew it like tea to obtain eight-tenths, and decoct the remaining dregs twice more to take. For pestilence, seasonal qi, and cold damage of the two toxins with headache and vigorous fever, decoct it with five scallion whites with roots and thirty fermented soybeans; after several doses sweat comes and the patient is relieved. If sweat has not yet been obtained, give thin gruel and cover thickly with clothes to obtain sweat, and recovery is immediate. For the five taxations and seven damages with heat in the palms and soles, vexation, agitation and restlessness, and aching pain of the limbs, decoct it with bupleurum (rhizome head removed). For phlegm-cough and attacks of malaria, decoct it with ginger-processed pinellia; for qi pain of the zang organ itself, with fennel; for water-qi swelling and fullness, with mulberry bark; for women's red and white vaginal discharge, with astragalus; for damage by wine, add clove; for food damage, add lesser galangal; for diarrhea in any season, add nutmeg; for wind disease, add schizonepeta spikes; for cold, painful thighs and knees, add achyranthes; for cramping of the whole body and deficiency congestion, add lycium root bark; for numb thighs, add dodder seed; for white dysentery, add evodia; for red dysentery, add coptis; for head wind, add Chinese lovage; for cramps and sudden turmoil, add nanmu bark. Each of these assistants is added in the amount of only one *zhu*. Regardless of age, before or after childbirth, for the five taxations and seven damages, the six extremes and eight pathogens, tinnitus and dim eyes, dream emissions and night sweats, heaviness of the limbs, aching pain of the thighs and knees, and women's long-standing cold womb and irregular menses—if one dose is taken every day on an empty stomach, it improves the complexion, fills out the body, regulates the sanjiao, strengthens the sinews and bones, dispels cold qi and eases the heart and chest; its divine effect cannot be described.

## Notes

Duijin yinzi (from here the layer of Wu Zhige's additions): the same four ingredients as Pingwei san (Heiisan) (black atractylodes, magnolia bark, tangerine peel, licorice), with more tangerine peel, said to treat seasonal cold damage and many other illnesses, with a long list of additions according to symptoms.
- The added drugs (bupleurum, pinellia, fennel, mulberry bark, astragalus, clove, galangal, nutmeg, schizonepeta, achyranthes, lycium bark, dodder, evodia, coptis, Chinese lovage, nanmu bark) are each only one *zhu*.
- The base text reads 只加一銖; the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions read 止加一銖 (same meaning).
