# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷下 015 論咳嗽喘急 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On cough and urgent panting. In general, cough always comes from the lungs. When physicians discuss it carefully and dispense medicine, there are roughly three kinds: due to cold, due to wind, and due to heat. Wind and cold come from outside; heat arises from within. With wind and cold, the channels themselves receive the evil; with heat, the channels and the viscera and bowels fume and ride upon one another to produce illness. Disperse wind, warm cold, regulate heat. "Draining" means draining the lung channel, not the viscera and bowels; lepidium seed, mulberry root bark and the like should be used. Cough due to wind worsens on meeting wind; due to cold, it becomes severe on meeting cold; due to heat, it breaks out on exposure to heat. There is also a very reliable test: simply ask what happens at night after drinking wine in the evening. If the cough worsens after drinking, there is heat; if it lessens, there is cold. Green-white drool indicates cold; thick yellow indicates heat. Dispense medicine according to the pattern.

For sudden cough from contracting wind-cold, with thick sticky cough and spittle and blockage of the chest and diaphragm, give Jinfeicao san, Banxia wan, Kuanhua gao, Huagai san, Wusou wan, Runfei wan, Kuanfei san, Qingjin wan, the children's Runfei san or Kuandonghua san. On cold cough: for recurrent cold cough, perhaps with green phlegm, worse at night, give Xixin wuweizi tang, Yangzhong tang, Wusou wan, Yu shanren jiangqi tang, Renshen huoxiang tang, Hujiao lizhong wan, Wenfei xixin tang, Zhongru bufei tang, Xiaoyin wan, Beizhu wan, Dingxiang banxia wan, Fenxinqi yin or Shen ling baizhu san. For aversion to wind with cold, give Xiao qinglong wan together with Kuanfei san and Renshen kuanhua gao. On heat cough: for discomfort of the chest and diaphragm, congested qi exuberant above, a red face and dry mouth and tongue, give Jinfeicao san, Da ejiao wan, Renshen yangfei wan, Qingxin yinzi, Renshen kuanhua gao, Banxia wan, Huagai san or Renshen runfei wan. For wind-phlegm with congested heat in the upper diaphragm, dry throat and vomiting of blood, give Chensha huatan wan, Da ejiao wan, Lajian wan, Renshen yangfei wan, Jinfeicao san, Qingzhou bai wanzi, Chuanxiong wan or Jisu wan. For long-standing cough and for the weak and elderly, give Wenfei wan, Renshen bufei tang, Dingxiang banxia wan, Renshen huoxiang tang or Huatan wan. When cold and heat interlock—a pattern common in autumn and winter—give Kuandonghua san, Banxia wan, Huagai san, Renshen kuanhua gao, Renshen runfei wan, the children's Runfei wan, Qingjin wan or Runjin san, one packet as two doses; especially suitable for the elderly. For panting with hurried breathing and inability to lie down, give Dingchuan tang, Ruiying wan, Lajian san, Jiangqi tang, Kuanhua san, Runjin san, Renshen yangfei wan, Huagai san and the like. For spitting of pus and blood, give Kuandonghua san or Jiuxian san.

## Notes

On cough and panting: all cough comes from the lungs; causes are wind and cold (external) and heat (internal); disperse wind, warm cold, regulate heat; "draining" means draining the lung channel (lepidium, mulberry root bark). Diagnostic clues: worsening on exposure to wind, cold or heat; worse after evening wine indicates heat, better indicates cold; green-white sputum indicates cold, thick yellow heat. Formulas are listed for sudden wind-cold cough, cold cough, heat cough, wind-phlegm with blood, chronic cough in the weak and elderly, autumn-winter cough with mixed cold and heat, panting that prevents lying down, and spitting of pus and blood.
