# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷下 032 論産後諸疾 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the various illnesses after childbirth. They all arise from heavy blood loss just after delivery, dry and scant fluids, deficiency of both yin and yang, and timid, weak viscera and bowels. One must on no account induce sweating, or use medicines that turn down accumulations to cause vomiting or purging, nor supplement with overly drying, hot medicines; use only warm, mild medicines, adjusting gradually to regulate. Seizing on the urgency of the moment and giving decoctions recklessly produces other illnesses instead, and the responsibility is not light; this must be strictly guarded against. Just after delivery both blood and qi are deficient; one must not supplement too much, lest chills and fever increase. It is best to let the lochia be fully discharged; give Heishen san, Dasheng san, Zelan san or Puhuang san. Stupor, dizziness, oppression and mental confusion after heavy postpartum blood loss all arise because so much blood has been lost that qi and blood have nothing to govern them; give Xiongqiong tang or Dasheng san mixed in rice water; if ineffective, give Huaruishi san or Bao'an gao. It is good for the parturient woman always to smell the vapour of vinegar poured on charcoal. For sudden fever after childbirth with cramping of the whole body, give Siwu tang together with Renshen danggui san or Xiaoyao san. Lumps in the abdomen after childbirth moving up and down with pain all arise from lochia not fully discharged, the new blood contending with the failed blood; give Heishen san or Puhuang san decocted as before, together with Pengjian wan or Hupo wan. When after childbirth the lochia has just begun to come down and suddenly stops, with alternating chills and fever, quiet by day and worse at dusk, and wild, confused speech as if seeing ghosts and spirits, this is heat entering the blood chamber; give Hupo wan, or Siwu tang decocted with 1 *qian* by weight of bupleurum added. If it does not subside, use Xiao chaihu tang decocted with two or three crushed stalks of raw rehmannia added. After childbirth, with much blood lost, scant fluids and both yin and yang deficient, even when there is cold damage or a seasonal illness that ought to be sweated, sweating must on no account be excessive; give only Qinggu san, Wuji san, Baidu san, Shenzhu san, Hejie san, Dasheng san and the like. Sudden pain of the navel and abdomen six or seven days after childbirth always arises from cold qi entering through the deficiency in the course of breathing; give Danggui jianzhong tang, Lizhong wan, Tongzhen wan, Wenjing tang, Da chenxiang wan or Yanjian san; if it does not subside, take Huixiang wan swallowed with Pingwei san. For severe postpartum abdominal pain, give Huaruishi san or Taiyue huoxue dan. Postpartum vexing thirst with a desire for cold drinks always arises from heavy blood loss, declining, scant yin qi and visiting yang riding upon it; the interior should be assisted: give Siwu tang with two mume fruits added to each dose; if the thirst does not stop, give Wuling san mixed in boiled water. For postpartum fright wind with wild, confused speech as if facing spirits and ghosts and an unsettled spirit, give Longhu dan, three pills as one dose, with good cinnabar ground and mixed in warm wine, together with Hupo wan. For postpartum blood wind increasing frequently, with stupor and unconsciousness, a body as if pricked by needles, hair falling with the comb, or deficiency swelling of the whole body, give Longhu dan, three pills as one dose, chewed and taken with angelica-decocted wine; for itching, also give Renshen jingjie san. For failure of milk after childbirth, one must not give blood-moving medicines, which produce other illnesses; give only medicines that nourish blood and qi, and the blood vessels will naturally flourish and the milk regulate itself: give Siwu tang, Shu gan dihuang wan, Baiwei wan, Neibu danggui wan, Yiyin dan, Dashun san, Zelan san and the like. For postpartum constipation or rough stools, due to lack of fluids within and dryness of the intestines and stomach, one must on no account purge with fierce medicines, lest other illnesses arise; give Siwu tang with half a *qian* of green tangerine peel (white removed) added to each dose, mixed evenly and decocted together. If still blocked, give Maren wan or Sanhe san.

## Notes

On postpartum illnesses: after delivery fluids are scant and yin and yang deficient, so sweating, emetics and purgatives, and overly hot, drying tonics are forbidden; regulate gradually with mild, warm medicines. It covers clearing the lochia (Heishen san, Dasheng san and others), stupor from blood loss (Xiongqiong tang, Dasheng san, vinegar-charcoal vapour), postpartum fever (Siwu tang, Renshen danggui san, Xiaoyao san), moving abdominal lumps (Heishen san, Puhuang san, Pengjian wan, Hupo wan), heat entering the blood chamber (Siwu tang with bupleurum, Xiao chaihu tang with raw rehmannia), cold damage after childbirth (only mild dispersing medicines), cold-induced umbilical pain on days six or seven (Danggui jianzhong tang and others), severe abdominal pain (Huaruishi san, Taiyue huoxue dan), vexing thirst (Siwu tang with mume, Wuling san), fright wind (Longhu dan, Hupo wan), blood wind (Longhu dan, Renshen jingjie san), failure of lactation (no blood-moving drugs; nourish blood and qi with Siwu tang and others), and constipation (no fierce purgatives; Siwu tang with green tangerine peel, Maren wan, Sanhe san).
