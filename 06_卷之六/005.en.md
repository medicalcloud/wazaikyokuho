# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 005 八正散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Bazheng san (Eight Rectifications Powder)

It treats adults and children with evil heat in the heart channel and all kinds of pent-up toxin, with dry throat and parched mouth, great thirst with drinking, palpitation and a hot face, vexation, agitation and restlessness, red eyes with painful pupils, parched lips and nosebleed, sores of the mouth and tongue, and swollen, painful throat. It is also suitable for red, rough urine, or dribbling urinary blockage, and heat strangury and blood strangury.

cheqianzi (plantago seed)　qumai (dianthus)　bianxu (knotgrass; also called dibianzhu)　huashi (talc)　shanzhizi ren (gardenia kernel)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　mutong (akebia)　dahuang (rhubarb; wrapped in dough and roasted in embers, dough removed, sliced, dried over a fire; 1 jin each)

Make the above into a powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water and rush pith, decoct down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm, after meals and at bedtime. For children give small amounts according to their strength.

## Notes

Bazheng san: for dry throat and mouth, thirst, palpitation, hot face, agitation, red eyes, nosebleed, mouth sores and sore throat from evil heat in the heart channel and pent-up toxin, and for dark, rough urination, urinary blockage, heat strangury and blood strangury. Still used today for damp-heat strangury of the lower burner such as cystitis (painful, frequent urination, haematuria).
- Plantago seed, dianthus, knotgrass, talc and akebia promote urination and relieve strangury; gardenia and rhubarb purge heat; licorice is added—eight ingredients (hence "eight").
