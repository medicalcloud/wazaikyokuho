# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 061 秘伝降気湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Michuan jiangqi tang (Secretly Transmitted Qi-Lowering Decoction)

It treats illness with heat above and deficiency below in men and women. Whenever excessive eating and drinking injure the spleen and stomach, and unrestrained wine and sex deplete the kidney origin, water and earth attack each other, yin and yang are separated and blocked, and qi then fails to rise and descend. With heat above there is clouding and dizziness of the head and eyes, phlegm excess with vomiting, discomfort of the chest and diaphragm, a dry throat and tasteless food; with weakness below there is no strength in the lower back and legs, rough constipation, tenesmus, and cold pain of the navel and abdomen. If treated with cool medicines, the spleen qi becomes timid and weak with borborygmus and diarrhea; if treated with warm medicines, the upper jiao becomes congested and hot with sores of the mouth and tongue; and also leg qi attacking upward and long-standing dysentery that will not heal. One should first take this medicine and then treat with the principal medicine; it never fails.

sangbaipi (mulberry root bark; stir-fried; 2 liang)　gusuibu (drynaria; hairs removed, stir-fried)　caoguo ren (tsaoko seed; skin removed, roasted)　wujiapi (gokahi; acanthopanax bark; soaked in wine for half a day, stir-fried yellow)　banxia (hange; pinellia; powdered raw, made into cakes with fresh ginger juice, crushed again and stir-fried)　jiegeng (kikyo; platycodon)　hezi (chebule; blast-fried, stone removed; half a liang each)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; stir-fried)　zhiqiao (bitter orange; pulp removed, stir-fried with bran)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed, stir-fried yellow)　chaihu (saiko; bupleurum; rhizome head removed)　digupi (lycium root bark; stir-fried yellow; 1 liang each)

Make the above into a coarse powder, mix evenly, steam it again for one full day and night and dry it in the sun. For each dose take 2 *qian* with three perilla leaves, three coin-slices of ginger and one *zhan* of water, decoct together down to seven-tenths and take it after meals at a comfortable temperature. Taken regularly it regulates and smooths the ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi, frees the sanjiao, opens the diaphragm, transforms phlegm and harmonizes the five zang organs. For phlegm-cough add pinellia leaven when decocting; for deficiency of heart and lungs, add ginseng and poria; for heat of the upper diaphragm, add northern scutellaria; for great deficiency of the lower body, add a little blast-fried aconite—and if aconite is used, add plenty of ginger; for women's blood deficiency, add angelica.

## Notes

Michuan jiangqi tang (Baoqing-era additions): for heat above with deficiency below and disordered rising and descending of qi after excess of food, wine and sex (above: dizziness, phlegm, vomiting, dry throat; below: weak legs, constipation, tenesmus, cold abdominal pain). When neither cool nor warm treatment works, one first lowers qi with this and then treats with the main formula.
- Mulberry bark, lycium bark and bupleurum clear heat above; pinellia, tangerine peel, bitter orange, platycodon, tsaoko and chebule send qi and phlegm down; drynaria and acanthopanax supplement below. Modifications by symptom are given.
