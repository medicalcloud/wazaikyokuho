# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 100 棗肉平胃散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zaorou pingwei san (Sonikuheiisan; Jujube Stomach-Calming Powder)

It is suitable for disharmony of the spleen and stomach with no desire for food, distension, fullness and stabbing pain of the heart, abdomen, flanks and ribs, a bitter mouth without taste, chest fullness with shortness of breath, retching and nausea, belching and acid swallowing, a withered yellow complexion, an emaciated, weak body, lassitude with a liking for lying down, a heavy body with painful joints, frequent spontaneous diarrhea, or outbreaks of sudden turmoil; and for the five chokings and eight glomuses, diaphragm qi and stomach reflux.

chen jupi (kippi; aged tangerine peel; [white] removed)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, processed with ginger and stir-fried until fragrant; 3 jin 2 liang each)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; chopped and stir-fried)　shengjiang (shokyo; fresh ginger)　hongzao (red jujubes; 2 jin each)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; coarse skin removed, soaked in rice-washing water for two days, stir-fried; 5 jin)

Chop the above, mix evenly, add water to about half a *cun* above the surface, boil until the water is gone, take out and dry over a fire, then mill into a fine powder. Take 2 *qian* each time stirred into salt water, on an empty stomach before meals. Taken regularly it regulates qi and warms the stomach, transforms old food, disperses phlegm-fluid retention and wards off the unseasonable qi of wind, cold, chill and damp in any season.

## Notes

Zaorou pingwei san (bureau additions): the same indications as Pingwei san (Heiisan) (juan 3, no. 018), with fresh ginger and red jujubes added and all simmered dry together before powdering.
