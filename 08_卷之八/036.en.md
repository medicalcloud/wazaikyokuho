# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之八 036 花蕊石散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huaruishi san (Ophicalcite Powder)

For all wounds from blades, arrows and arrowheads, for injuries from blows and falls, and for cat and dog bites, even when near death, quickly sprinkle the medicine on the wound; the blood turns into yellow water, and with another sprinkling the patient revives at once without further pain. For internal damage with blood entering the viscera and bowels, heat a boy's urine, add a little wine, mix in one large *qian* and give it; it works at once. If gored by an ox with the intestines protruding but undamaged, quickly push them back in, sew up the abdominal skin with thread made from the fine fibres at the tips of mulberry root bark, sprinkle the medicine on the stitches, and the bleeding stops and the patient lives at once. If there is no mulberry root bark, raw hemp thread will do. In no case should the opening of the wound be sealed and bandaged, lest pus and blood form. If the wound is dry, moisten it with saliva and then sprinkle the medicine on. For women after childbirth with failed blood not fully discharged, blood confusion and blood dizziness, malign blood rushing to the heart, a foetus dead in the abdomen, or the placenta not coming down so that death is near—so long as the chest is warm, quickly mix 1 *qian* in a boy's urine and give it; foul matter like slices of pig's liver is passed, and for the rest of her life she will not suffer blood wind or blood-qi disorders. If there is blood above the diaphragm, it turns into yellow water and is vomited at once or passes out with the urine, with immediate effect.

liuhuang (sulphur; top-coloured, bright and clear pieces, pounded into a coarse powder; 4 liang)　huaruishi (ophicalcite; pounded into a coarse powder; 1 liang)

Mix these two ingredients evenly. First seal an earthenware jar large enough to hold the medicine with glue-clay mixed with paper fibre; when the clay is dry put the medicine inside, seal the mouth tightly with clay, dry it thoroughly in a drying basket until heated through, then set it on a square brick on which the characters of the eight trigrams and five phases are written. Using one *cheng* of charcoal, stack baskets around it, and from the *si* and *wu* hours (9 a.m.–1 p.m.) light the fire from below so that it gradually penetrates upward; whenever fire falls down, pick it up at once and put it back on top, continuing through the night until the fire is cold and the charcoal burned out. Leave it another night; when the jar is completely cold, take out the contents, grind fine, sift extremely fine through a silk sieve, store in a porcelain box, and use as described above.

## Notes

Huaruishi san: sprinkled on bleeding wounds from blades, arrows, blows and cat or dog bites; also for internal bleeding, for an ox-goring with protruding intestines (pushed back and sutured with mulberry-bark thread, then dusted with the powder), for postpartum failed blood, dizziness, dead foetus and retained placenta, and for blood above the diaphragm.
- Ophicalcite and sulphur sealed in a jar and calcined for a day and night, the jar set on a brick inscribed with the eight trigrams and five phases; the base text has a diagram of these (the five phases and eight trigrams), which is not included here.
- Emendation: "sealed" (*fengkou liao*) reads *fengkou zi* in the base text; emended following the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions.
