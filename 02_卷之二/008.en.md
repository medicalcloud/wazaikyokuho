# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 008 五積散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wuji san (Goshakusan; Five Accumulations Powder)

It regulates the middle and smooths qi, removes wind-cold and transforms phlegm-fluid retention. It is suitable for chronic cold of the spleen and stomach with distension and pain of the abdomen and flanks, phlegm lodged in the chest and diaphragm, vomiting counterflow and nausea; or external contraction of wind-cold together with internal damage from raw and cold food, with stuffiness and oppression of the heart and abdomen, clouding and pain of the head and eyes, stiffness of the shoulders and back, lassitude of the limbs, alternating fever and chills and inability to eat; and women's disordered blood and qi with griping pain of the heart and abdomen, irregular menses, or blocked menses.

baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed)　danggui (toki; Chinese angelica; rhizome head removed)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　shaoyao (shakuyaku; peony root)　banxia (hange; pinellia; washed seven times in hot water; 3 liang each)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　zhiqiao (bitter orange; pulp removed, stir-fried)　mahuang (mao; ephedra; roots and nodes removed; 6 liang each)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked in rice-washing water, skin removed; 24 liang)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; scorched; 4 liang)　jiegeng (kikyo; platycodon; rhizome head removed; 12 liang)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed; 4 liang)

Make the cinnamon bark and bitter orange separately into a coarse powder; make the [other] thirteen ingredients together into a coarse powder, stir-fry it over a gentle fire until it changes color, spread it out to cool, and then mix in the cinnamon and bitter orange powder evenly. For each dose take 3 *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water and three slices of fresh ginger, decoct down to one medium *zhan*, remove the dregs and take it fairly hot. For cold qi rushing and surging, with distension, fullness and stabbing pain of the heart, flanks, navel and abdomen, stomach reflux with vomiting, diarrhea with clear undigested food, and for strings and aggregations, abdominal masses, and qi pain of the bladder and small intestine, decoct it with three slices of roasted ginger and a little salt. For cold damage or seasonal epidemics with headache and body pain, aversion to wind, fever and stiff, painful neck and back, add three *cun* of scallion white and seven fermented soybeans. If there is only aversion to cold, or the body is not very hot, with stiff limbs or cold hands and feet, add seven grains of stir-fried evodia and a little salt. For disordered cold and heat with cough, dyspnea and fullness, decoct it with jujube. For difficult labor, decoct it with one *ge* of vinegar. All at any time.

## Notes

Wuji san: said to treat the "five accumulations" (cold, damp, qi, blood and phlegm); for cold of the spleen and stomach with fullness and vomiting, combined external wind-cold and internal damage from cold food, and women's irregular menses and abdominal pain. It is still widely used for cold-induced back pain, neuralgia, dysmenorrhoea and colds.
- Ephedra, angelica dahurica and cinnamon release the exterior; black atractylodes, magnolia bark, tangerine peel, pinellia, poria and licorice (Pingwei san [Heiisan] and Erchen tang [Nichinto]) remove damp and phlegm; angelica, lovage and peony regulate the blood; dried ginger and cinnamon warm; bitter orange and platycodon smooth qi.
- All but the cinnamon and bitter orange are stir-fried until they change color ("cooked" Wuji san); the additions vary with the pattern.
