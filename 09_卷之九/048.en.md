# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 048 南岳魏夫人済陰丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Nanyue Wei furen jiyin dan (Lady Wei of the Southern Peak's Yin-Relieving Elixir)

It treats women childless from long-standing cold of blood and qi and those who have repeatedly miscarried. All arises from deficiency damage of the *chong* and *ren* vessels and old illness harboured in the uterus, with menses at irregular times, sudden unceasing bleeding, two periods in a month, early or late, or flooding and spotting, the thirty-six disorders, accumulations, aggregations and conglomerations, cold pain below the navel and white turbid urine; all these disorders prevent pregnancy and birth, to the point of ending the line. It treats the hundred illnesses after childbirth; taken regularly within a hundred days, it removes old blood, generates new blood, brings pregnancy and makes the child robust. It also treats men's disorders of blood loss.

qinjiao (large-leaf gentian)　shihu (dendrobium; roots removed, soaked in wine, dried over a fire)　gaoben (Chinese lovage; rhizome head removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　canbu (silkworm cloth; burned to ash)　jiegeng (platycodon; stir-fried; 2 liang each)　jingmo (capital ink; calcined, quenched in vinegar, ground)　fuling (poria; skin removed)　renshen (ginseng; rhizome head removed)　muxiang (costus root; blast-fried)　taoren (peach kernel; skin and tips removed, stir-fried; 1 liang each)　shu gan dihuang (prepared rehmannia; washed, steamed with wine, dried over a fire)　xiangfu (cyperus; stir-fried, hairs removed)　zelan (lycopus; stalks removed; 4 liang each)　danggui (Chinese angelica; rhizome head removed)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried)　xixin (asarum; shoots removed)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　mudanpi (moutan bark; 1½ liang each)　shanyao (Chinese yam)　chuanjiao (Sichuan pepper; seeds removed, stir-fried; 3 *fen* each)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked in rice-washing water, skin removed; 8 liang)　dadou huangjuan (soybean sprout; stir-fried; half a sheng)　nuomi [glutinous rice; stir-fried; 1 sheng (one version gives 3 liang each for Chinese yam and Sichuan pepper)]

Make the above into a fine powder, knead with refined honey, and make six pills to each liang. Take one pill each time, chewed thoroughly, on an empty stomach before meals, with warm wine or vinegar decoction as preferred.

## Notes

Nanyue Wei furen jiyin dan (Wu Zhige's additions): the first part of its indications nearly matches the Zishiying wan (juan 9, no. 016)—infertility and habitual miscarriage from deficient *chong* and *ren* with old uterine disorders—and it is also used for postpartum illnesses and blood loss in men.
- Prepared rehmannia, angelica, lovage, ginseng, poria, Chinese yam and dendrobium supplement; lycopus, peach kernel, moutan, cyperus, ink and silkworm-cloth ash move and stop blood; cinnamon, dried ginger, Sichuan pepper and asarum warm; large-leaf gentian and Chinese lovage dispel wind; black atractylodes, soybean sprout, glutinous rice, costus, platycodon and licorice are added. It bears the name of Lady Wei of the Southern Peak (like the Zhenling dan, juan 5, no. 079).
