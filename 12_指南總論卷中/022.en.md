# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷中 022 論傷寒傷風証候 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the signs of cold damage and wind damage. Wind damage always arises from taking off clothes and catching a chill, being blown upon by wind, so that one shivers with cold in the bones and hair standing on end, with aversion to wind and spontaneous sweating: this is the pattern of wind damage. When, blown on by wind, the body becomes cold of itself, with aversion to wind and no sweating, this is the pattern of cold damage.

## Notes

On distinguishing cold damage from wind damage: shivering with aversion to wind and spontaneous sweating after exposure is wind damage; chill with aversion to wind and no sweating is cold damage (the presence of sweat distinguishes them).
