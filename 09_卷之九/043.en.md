# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 043 神仙聚宝丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shenxian jubao dan (Divine Immortal Treasure-Gathering Elixir)

It treats women with a deficient, cold sea of blood upon which wind-cold rides from outside, contending and binding without dispersing, accumulating into lumps or hard conglomerations; as well as blood and qi attacking and pouring in with pain of the abdomen and flanks, cramping distension of the lower abdomen, or occasional deficiency rumbling, a withered yellow complexion, puffy limbs, and, as the menses are about to come, first a state like serious illness, with flow sometimes heavy, sometimes scant, red and white vaginal discharge, unceasing flooding, fright palpitations and forgetfulness, frequent urination, or discharge of white fluid, occasional deficiency heat, night sweats and emaciation. Whether during pregnancy, after childbirth or in unmarried girls, all should take this medicine. Taken regularly it calms the heart-spirit, removes pathogenic qi, expels failed blood, nourishes new blood and enables conception.

moyao (myrrh; ground separately)　hupo (amber; ground separately)　muxiang (mokko; costus root; roasted in embers, powdered)　danggui (toki) (Chinese angelica; washed, dried over a fire, powdered; 1 liang each)　chensha (cinnabar; ground separately)　shexiang (jako; musk; ground separately; 1 *qian* each)　di ruxiang (drop frankincense; ground separately; 1 *fen*)

Grind the above fine and mix evenly, drip in cooled boiled water, pound and form pills, fifteen to each liang. Take one pill each time ground into warm wine. For an inauspicious fetal state with abdominal pain and all difficult labors, grind it into warm wine with a boy's urine. For postpartum blood dizziness, failed blood rushing to the heart, clenched jaw and stiff tongue, or lochia not fully discharged with thirst and facial swelling, grind it into decocted mume decoction mixed with a boy's urine. For postpartum weakness of strength where other medicines do not act quickly, grind it into a boy's urine. For irregular menses in unmarried girls, half a pill each time ground into warm wine, at any time.

## Notes

Shenxian jubao dan (from here the layer of Chunyou-era additions): for accumulations and conglomerations from wind-cold bound in a deficient, cold sea of blood, abdominal and flank pain, lower abdominal distension, sallowness, edema, premenstrual malaise, irregular flow, discharge, flooding, fright palpitations, forgetfulness, frequent urination, white discharge, deficiency heat, night sweats and wasting—in pregnancy, after childbirth or in girls; with special vehicles for difficult labor, postpartum dizziness, retained lochia and postpartum weakness.
- Myrrh, amber, frankincense and angelica move static blood; costus and musk move qi; cinnabar settles.
