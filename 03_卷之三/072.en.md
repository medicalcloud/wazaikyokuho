# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 072 白沈香散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Bai chenxiang san (White Aquilaria Powder)

It treats all cold qi attacking and surging into the heart and abdomen, distension and fullness of the flanks and ribs, belching of sourness and acid swallowing, choking obstruction of the chest and diaphragm and reduced eating. Taken regularly it sinks qi and harmonizes the spleen and stomach.

chuan baijiang (haisho; white dried ginger from Sichuan; stir-fried)　banxia (hange) qu (pinellia leaven)　bai fuling (bukuryo; white poria)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried until cooked, skin removed)　hezi rou (chebule flesh)　dried shanyao (Chinese yam)　chenxiang (aquilaria)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes; roasted)　muxiang (mokko; costus root)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; rhizome head removed; 1½ liang each)　dingxiang (clove; half a liang)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; 6 *qian*)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take two large *qian* with one medium *zhan* of water, three slices of fresh ginger, three jujubes and one slice of chaenomeles, decoct down to seven-tenths and take it before meals.

## Notes

Bai chenxiang san (Chunyou-era additions): for flank distension, acid reflux, chest obstruction and poor appetite from cold qi attacking the heart and abdomen.
- Ginseng, atractylodes, poria, licorice and Chinese yam supplement; aconite, white ginger and clove warm; aquilaria and costus sink and move qi; pinellia leaven and chebule are added.
- The base text writes 白朮（煨）木香 without a space, but these are two ingredients.
