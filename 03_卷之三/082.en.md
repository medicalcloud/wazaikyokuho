# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 082 集香丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jixiang wan (Gathered Fragrances Pill)

It treats all qi disorders with pain and oppression of the chest and diaphragm, distension and fullness of the flanks and ribs, pain of the heart and abdomen, belching and acid swallowing, retching and nausea and no desire for food; or excessive damage by wine with disharmony of the spleen and stomach.

bai doukou ren (white cardamom)　suoshaner (amomum)　muxiang (costus root; not exposed to fire)　jianghuang (turmeric; 4 liang each)　dingxiang (clove; not exposed to fire; 6 liang)　xiangfuzi (cyperus; stir-fried, hairs removed; 4 liang 8 *qian*)　shexiang (musk; ground; 8 *qian*)　gancao (licorice; 16 liang, of which 2 liang go into the medicine and 14 liang are pounded for juice and simmered into a paste)

Except for the ground medicine, mill the above into a fine powder, mix in the musk evenly and knead with the licorice paste into pills the size of wutong seeds. Take one or two pills each time, chewed thoroughly and swallowed with the saliva, at any time. Taken regularly it loosens the middle and smooths qi, disperses hangover, improves eating, grinds down accumulated stagnation and removes concretion lumps.

## Notes

Jixiang wan (Wu Zhige's additions): for chest pain, flank distension, abdominal pain, acid reflux, nausea and poor appetite in qi disorders, and for damage from wine.
- A "gathering" of aromatics (white cardamom, amomum, costus, clove, cyperus, turmeric, musk) made into pills with licorice paste, chewed and swallowed.
