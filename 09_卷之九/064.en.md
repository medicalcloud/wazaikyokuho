# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 064 牡丹散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Mudan san (Botansan; Moutan Powder)

It treats blood deficiency and taxation fatigue with vexing heat of the five hearts, painful limbs, a clouded, heavy head and eyes, palpitation and red cheeks, dry mouth and throat, fever with night sweats, and reduced eating with a liking for lying down; as well as blood and heat contending with obstructed menses, distending pain of the navel and abdomen, and chills and fever like malaria. It also treats unmarried girls with weak blood and deficient yin, disharmony of the ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi, phlegm cough and tidal fever, an emaciated body, gradually becoming steaming bone disorder.

ganqi (kanshitsu; dried lacquer; stir-fried)　sumu (sappanwood)　guijian (euonymus twig)　peng ezhu (zedoary; blast-fried; 1 *fen* each)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; half roasted in salt water, half raw)　danggui (toki; Chinese angelica)　guixin (cinnamon heart)　mudanpi (moutan bark)　shaoyao (shakuyaku; peony root)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　honghua (safflower)　yanhusuo (corydalis; stir-fried)　moyao (myrrh; ground finely separately)　wuyao (lindera; 1 liang each)

Powder the above. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water and decoct down to seven-tenths, at any time.

## Notes

Mudan san (bureau additions): the indications nearly duplicate those of the Xiaoyao san (Shoyosan) (juan 9, no. 013), with "obstructed menses" in place of "irregular menses".
- The formula differs from Xiaoyao san: dried lacquer, sappanwood, euonymus twig, zedoary, safflower, corydalis, myrrh, moutan, angelica and peony strongly move static blood; cinnamon heart, lindera, tangerine peel and licorice are added.
