# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷下 004 論小腸気疾 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On small-intestine qi disorders. Small-intestine qi, bladder running piglet, mounting qi and similar illnesses all arise from weak kidney qi and long-standing cold of the bladder, upon which wind-damp rides, damaging the kidney meridian, so that qi stagnates and does not disperse, with stabbing pain of the lower abdomen and one-sided drawing of the kidney meridian. Supplementing medicines must not be given at once; first free, guide and disperse: give Suzi jiangqi tang (Soshikokito), Wuling san (Goreisan), Pancong san, Yanjian san, Chuanlian san, Da chenxiang wan (Taijinkogan) or Huixiang wan (Uikyogan); also chew stir-fried fennel and burned salt thoroughly and wash down with hot wine; or Wuxiang san (Gokosan), Zhengyuan san, Bichengqie san and the like. For urinary blockage, give Jishexiang san (Keizetsukosan) or Wuling san. When the pain has somewhat receded, simply regulate with Huixiang wan swallowed with Pingwei san (Heiisan).

## Notes

On small-intestine qi, running piglet and mounting (weak kidney qi and long-cold bladder ridden by wind-damp, with stabbing lower abdominal pain and one-sided drawing): no immediate supplementation; first free and disperse with Suzi jiangqi tang, Wuling san, Pancong san, Yanjian san, Chuanlian san, Da chenxiang wan, Huixiang wan and others; Jishexiang san or Wuling san for urinary blockage; then Huixiang wan with Pingwei san.
