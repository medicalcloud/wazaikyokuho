# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 037 木香散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Muxiang san (Mokkosan; Costus Root Powder)

It treats weak, deficient spleen and stomach harboring wind-cold within, with pouring diarrhea, undigested water and grain, gripping pain below the navel, thunderous rumbling in the abdomen, stuffiness and oppression of the chest and diaphragm, and deficiency distension of the flanks and ribs; as well as long-standing dysentery from accumulated cold with slippery, uncontrollable intestines, emaciated, weary limbs and inability to eat.

dingxiang (clove)　muxiang (mokko; costus root)　danggui (toki; Chinese angelica; rhizome head removed, washed, dried over a fire)　roudoukou ren (nutmeg kernel; blast-fried)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched; 20 liang each)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; skin and navel removed, boiled in vinegar, sliced, dried over a fire)　chi shizhi (red halloysite; 10 liang each)　huoxiang ye (agastache leaf; washed, dried over a fire; 40 liang)　hezi pi (chebule fruit rind; 15 liang)

Powder the above. For each dose take one large *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, add two slices of fresh ginger and one jujube, decoct together down to six-tenths and take it warm, on an empty stomach before meals.

## Notes

Muxiang san: for pouring diarrhea, indigestion, gripping pain below the navel, borborygmus, chest stuffiness and flank distension from weak spleen and stomach with wind-cold, and for incontinence, wasting and poor appetite in long-standing dysentery from accumulated cold.
- Mainly agastache; clove and costus move qi; aconite warms; nutmeg, chebule and red halloysite astringe the intestines; angelica and licorice are added.
