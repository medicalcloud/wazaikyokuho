# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷中 053 論寒熱瘴瘧証 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the signs of miasmic malaria.

On the pattern of cold-heat miasmic malaria. Miasmic malaria, although an epidemic illness, is contracted more shallowly or deeply according to age and deficiency or excess, and is by no means uniform. Some have fever without chills, the whole body like fire, with headache, vexing thirst and delirium; some have chills without fever, silent stupor and fatigue, cold limbs and pain of the navel and abdomen; some are cold outside and hot inside; some hot outside and cold inside; some half cold and half hot; some become mute and cannot speak; some vomit, some have diarrhea, some have both; medicines must be used according to the pattern. If one merely calls it miasmic illness and treats all alike, not one in ten thousand will respond. If during attacks there is more heat than cold, or heat within and cold without, or heat only without cold, the whole body like fire, headache, vexing thirst, agitation and oppression of the heart and chest, delirious and confused speech, constipation and rough urination, and attacks at no fixed times, give Xiao chaihu tang (Shosaikoto), Baidu san, Shengma gegen tang (Shomakakkonto), Laisu san, Congbai san (Sohakusan) or Shenzhu san. For vexing thirst, give Wuling san (Goreisan) or Zhuye tang (Chikuyoto). For delirium with chest oppression, give Wuling san with cinnabar finely ground and mixed in evenly, taken in cold or hot water, together with Da zhibao dan, and also two or three of the children's Jinbo wan as one dose. For headache, give Taiyang dan, Bailong wan, Chatiao san and the like. If there is less heat and more cold, or cold within and heat without, or half cold and half hot, or aching joints and pain of the navel and abdomen, give Buhuanjin zhengqi san (Fukankinshokisan), Renshen (ninjin) qinggu san, Zhengqi san, Sheng sanzi, Wuji san (Goshakusan), Xiangsu san (Kososan), Jianzhong san, Caoguo yin (Sokain), Jiahe san or Laisu san and the like. If there is no fever but only cold, or vomiting, or diarrhea, or both, with cold limbs, sweat pouring like rain and silent stupor and fatigue, give Zhufu tang, Sizhu san (Shichusan), Jiahe san, or Lurong wan, Erjiang wan or Shihua san. If after taking the above medicines the vomiting and diarrhea do not stop, the limbs are cold, spontaneous sweat pours like rain and urination is frequent, give thirty to fifty pills of Laifu dan swallowed with Shen ling baizhu san (Jinryobyakujutsusan); in severe cases decoct Jiangfu tang combined with Wuling san. For thirst, give Shen ling baizhu san. This pattern is common in the two Guang (Guangdong and Guangxi) and on the borders of Zhangzhou, and does not occur elsewhere. In dispensing medicine for this pattern one must inquire carefully to ascertain the facts and not use medicines lightly, lest a life be ruined. Remember above all not to dispense hot medicines lightly. If the attacks come at set times—daily, every other day, or once every three to five days—and when they come there is more heat than cold, or heat only without cold, give Xiao chaihu tang, Baidu san and the like before the attack; on the day of the attack, take Lutian yin, Sheng yinzi or Shengjin wan. For vomiting with phlegm and drool, Changshan yin (Jozanin), Xiaoshu wan and the like are divinely effective. If there is less heat and more cold, or only cold without heat, or half cold and half hot, give much Buhuanjin zhengqi san, Jianzhong tang, Zhengqi san, Pingwei san (Heiisan), Hepi san, Jiahe san and the like before the attack; at the beginning of the fifth watch on the day of the attack, take Changshan yin or Caoguo yin. For long-standing attacks, [give] Kexiao bing or Lingnüe dan; these must not be taken by the elderly and weak—judge for yourself. When a pregnant woman suffers from malaria, it is difficult to use medicines; simply give Caoguo yin, together with Pingwei san with a little salt taken in warm wine. After recovery from miasmic malaria, eat gruel or soft rice. Further, the spleen and stomach should be constantly regulated and harmonized: give Huangqi jianzhong tang (Ogikenchuto), Si junzi tang (Shikunshito), Jiahe san, Shen ling baizhu san, Caopi san, Zhuapi san, Jianpi tang, Pingwei san, Heqi san, Sishi wan, or Da and Xiao yangpi wan. Strictly avoid raw and cold things, wine, fruit and sexual activity, and [avoid] bathing for half a month.

## Notes

On miasmic malaria (a malaria-like endemic illness of the south), under the heading "On the signs of miasmic malaria": presentations vary with age and constitution (heat only, cold only, cold outside and heat inside or vice versa, half and half, mutism, vomiting, diarrhea), so it must not be treated uniformly. Medicines are listed by type: for predominant heat Xiao chaihu tang, Baidu san and others; for predominant cold Buhuanjin zhengqi san, Wuji san and others; for cold only with vomiting, diarrhea, cold limbs and profuse sweating Zhufu tang, Laifu dan, Jiangfu tang and others. It is endemic to Guangdong, Guangxi and Zhangzhou, and hot medicines must not be given lightly. It also covers timing (Xiao chaihu tang before the attack, Lutian yin, Shengjin wan, Changshan yin or Caoguo yin on the day), Kexiao bing and Lingnüe dan for chronic cases, Caoguo yin and Pingwei san in pregnancy, and convalescent care of the spleen and stomach with prohibitions.
