# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 057 蚵蚾丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Kebo wan (Toad Pill)

It treats the five gan and eight dysenteries of children: irregular nursing and feeding and mismanagement of cold and warmth, with bristling hair and scorched fur, withered, haggard skin, thin legs and a big belly, open cranial sutures and a sunken chest, gradual emaciation, intermittent chills and fever, night sweats and cough, nodes at the back of the head, lumps in the abdomen, urine turbid like rice-washing water, purulent dysentery with green sediment, plucking the eyebrows and biting the fingers, eating earth and sweet and sour things, vomiting undigested food, frequent vexing thirst, a clouded spirit, red nose and dry lips, small worms coming out, roundworms biting the heart, gan eyes and sparrow eyes—this is called *dingxi*. Treated with this medicine, its efficacy is divine.

bai wuyi (white elm seed paste; skin removed)　huanglian (oren; coptis; fibrous roots removed)　kebo (toad; soaked in wine, bones removed, dried over a fire)　hu huanglian (picrorhiza; 1½ liang each)　qingdai (indigo; half a liang, for coating)

Mill the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of millet grains with pig's bile and flour paste. Take thirty pills each time, swallowed with rice water, after meals and at bedtime, three times a day.

## Notes

Kebo wan (from here the layer of Wu Zhige's additions): for the five gan and eight dysenteries of children (bristling hair, withered skin, thin legs and big belly, open sutures, sunken chest, wasting, chills and fever, night sweats, cough, occipital nodes, abdominal lumps, turbid urine, purulent dysentery, plucking eyebrows, biting fingers, eating earth, vomiting, thirst, clouded spirit, red nose, dry lips, worms, gan eyes and night blindness), a condition called *dingxi*.
- Toad and white elm seed paste kill worms; coptis and picrorhiza clear gan heat; indigo forms the coating; made into pills with pig's bile.
