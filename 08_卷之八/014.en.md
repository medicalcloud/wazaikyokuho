# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之八 014 法制熟艾 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Fazhi shu'ai (Properly Prepared Moxa)

It governs the hundred illnesses by moxibustion.

aged yellow mugwort (in any quantity: pick the leaves, put them in a mortar and pound lightly with a wooden pestle until well worked, sift through a fine sieve to separate out the green residue, pound and sift again, three times in this way, then sift separately through a horsehair sieve, pound and sift again, until it is soft, fine, yellow and well worked)

The above governs the hundred illnesses by moxibustion. People of the world mostly apply moxibustion without proper method, merely enduring pain and suffering, and seldom cure illness. Now from the *Shenghui fang*, *Qianjin fang*, *Waitai miyao* and other works the methods for locating points by measure, the size of the cones, the number of cones and so on have been gathered and are recorded below.

Method of fixing the measure: on the patient's middle finger—the left for a man, the right for a woman—take the distance between the two transverse creases on the inner side of the second segment as one *cun*. The measures for locating points and making cones all follow this method.

Method of marking moxibustion points: when marking points, the body must be level and straight and the limbs not curled up. When marking while sitting, do not bend forward or back; when marking while standing, do not lean to one side. If the point is not correct at the time of moxibustion, it is of no benefit; one merely burns the flesh and endures pain in vain. If the point was marked sitting, apply moxa sitting; if lying, apply lying; if standing, apply standing. Otherwise the point will not be found.

Method of making moxa cones: when lighting and applying moxa, the base of the cone must be 3 *fen* wide and also 3 *fen* high. If smaller than this, it does not cover the point, does not hit the channel, the fire qi does not travel, and it cannot remove the illness. For strong, robust people it may be made somewhat larger. For children within one full year, it may be the size of a grain of wheat.

Method of lighting: since ancient times, in treating illness with fire moxibustion, the fires of eight kinds of wood have been avoided. Now that wood fire is not used for moxibustion, the various troubles are not incurred and long-standing ailments are also removed. Light a lamp with clear oil and ignite a mugwort stem on the lamp to light the moxa; this also moistens the moxa sore, and until it heals there is no pain. A wax candle is even better. Also, a fire-pearl (burning lens) held to the sun with mugwort to receive the light produces fire; this fire is good for treating illness. Next, a fire-mirror held to the sun with mugwort to draw it produces fire; this fire is also good.

Method of timing the application: moxa may be applied only after noon, a time when, it is said, yin qi has not yet arrived, so that moxibustion never fails to take. Before noon and at dawn the grain qi is deficient, causing dizziness and collapse; moxa must not be applied lying down then—be careful, be careful. This is the general rule, but in emergencies it need not be followed. If one encounters overcast or fog, rising wind and snow, sudden violent rain, scorching heat, thunder, lightning or a rainbow, stop for the time being; wait until it is clear and bright, then apply moxa again. During moxibustion one must not be overfull, very hungry, drink wine or eat raw, hard things; also avoid anxiety and worry, angry shouting and cursing, sighing and lamenting, and everything inauspicious—avoiding these is greatly auspicious.

Method of treating moxa sores that fail to develop: when moxibustion is used to treat an illness that has not eased through spring, summer, autumn and winter, even if the number of cones is sufficient, once a sore forms and pus comes out, the ailment heals. If no sore forms and no pus comes out, the illness does not heal. The *Jiayi jing* says: "When moxa sores do not develop, heat the sole of an old shoe by moxibustion and iron with it; in three days they develop, pus comes out and the illness naturally heals." Now use three to five red-skinned scallions, remove the green part, roast them in hot ashes until cooked, crack them open and iron the moxa sore with them while hot ten-odd times; in three days the sore develops by itself, breaks at once, the pus comes out and the illness heals.

Method of washing moxa sores: when moxibustion is used to treat an illness, as soon as the fire is stopped, decoct red-skinned scallion and mint and pour the pleasantly warm decoction over the area of one or two *cun* around the moxa sore, to drive the wind qi out from within the opening of the sore; this also keeps the channels flowing without stagnation beneath the sore, so that the sore naturally breaks and the illness heals. After the burn scab of the moxa sore comes off, decoct equal parts of southeast-facing peach branches and somewhat green, tender peach bark, and pour the pleasantly warm decoction over the sore; these two are particularly able to protect the moxa sore from the various winds. If the inside of the sore is black, festering and ulcerated, add coriander and wash with a pleasantly warm decoction of equal parts of the three, and good flesh will naturally grow in the sore. If the pain of the moxa sore is unbearable and has not eased for a long time, add coptis and wash with a decoction of equal parts of the four, with immediate divine effect.

Method for the number of cones: the *Qianjin fang* says: "Where numbers of cones are given, if a man in his prime meets an illness whose root is deep and severe, the number may be double that of the formula. If the person is old, young, weak or emaciated, it may be halved. According to Bian Que's moxibustion method there are cases of up to five hundred or a thousand cones; all are adjusted at the time."

Method of reckoning the location of the human spirit: on the 1st day, the big toe; 2nd, the outer ankle; 3rd, the inner thigh; 4th, the lower back; 5th, the mouth, tongue, throat and uvula; 6th, the little toe; 7th, the inner ankle; 8th, the wrist of the foot; 9th, the buttocks; 10th, the back and lower back; 11th, the bridge of the nose; 12th, the hairline; 13th, the teeth; 14th, the stomach duct; 15th, the whole body; 16th, the chest and breasts; 17th, Qichong; 18th, inside the abdomen; 19th, the instep; 20th, below the knee; 21st, the little finger; 22nd, Futu; 23rd, Ganshu; 24th, the hand *yangming* and both flanks; 25th, the foot *yangming*; 26th, the hands and feet; 27th, the knees; 28th, the genitals; 29th, the knees, shins and temples; 30th, from Guanyuan down to the soles of the feet. On the days when the human spirit is in the above places, moxibustion [there] is forbidden. If the illness is urgent, this rule need not be followed.

## Notes

Fazhi shu'ai: instructions for preparing moxa and methods of moxibustion gathered from the *Shenghui fang*, *Qianjin fang*, *Waitai miyao* and others—measuring the *cun* by the patient's finger, posture for locating points, cone size, ways of lighting, timing, weather and prohibitions, inducing moxa sores, washing them, number of cones, and the daily location of the "human spirit" where moxa is forbidden. Not a drug formula but a general account of moxibustion.
- One *cun* is the distance between the creases of the second segment of the middle finger (the middle-finger body inch). Cones are 3 *fen* at the base. Moxa is applied after noon, and suppuration of the moxa sore is taken as the sign of cure.
