# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 095 四君子湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Si junzi tang (Four Gentlemen Decoction)

It treats deficiency of the nutritive and defensive qi, weakness of the viscera and bowels, distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen, complete lack of desire to eat, borborygmus with diarrhoea, and retching and vomiting. It is very suitable to take.

renshen (ginseng; rhizome head removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　fuling (poria; skin removed)　baizhu (white atractylodes; equal parts of each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take two *qian* with one *zhan* (cup) of water and decoct down to seven-tenths; take it at a comfortable warmth, at any time. It may also be taken with a little salt, stirred into plain hot water. Taken regularly, it warms and harmonizes the spleen and stomach, improves eating and drinking, and wards off cold evils and miasmic mists.

## Notes

Si junzi tang: the basic qi-tonifying formula for spleen-stomach qi deficiency with poor appetite, diarrhoea and vomiting.
- Ginseng, licorice, poria and white atractylodes in equal parts. It became the foundation of many later formulas, such as Liu junzi tang (adding tangerine peel and pinellia).
- In this book it belongs to the layer "secret formulas newly added from the bureaus' experience" (a later addition) in the chapter "treating all qi disorders" of juan 3.
- "Stirred into plain hot water" (白湯点) means dissolving the powder in hot water and drinking it.
