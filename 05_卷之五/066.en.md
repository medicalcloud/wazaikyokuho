# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 066 崔氏烏頭丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Cui shi wutou wan (Master Cui's Aconite Pill)

It is suitable for wind-cold evil qi entering and riding on the network vessels of the heart, or the bowels and viscera suddenly contracting wind-cold which rises and rides on the heart, causing sudden heart pain, sometimes drawing into the spine, now better and now worse, not healing over a long time.

fuzi (aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　chi shizhi (red halloysite; 3 liang each)　shujiao (Sichuan pepper; seeds and closed fruits removed, stir-fried to sweat out the oil)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried; 2 liang each)

Pound these six items, sift into a fine powder, knead with honey and form pills the size of wutong seeds. Take three pills each time with warm wine; when one feels [the medicine] reach the painful place, the pain stops at once. If it does not stop, increase to five or six pills, until effective. If taken early in the morning with no sensation, take another three pills at noon and another three at night. For long-standing heart pain, take three pills every morning, gradually increasing to ten; after finishing one prescription it will never recur for the rest of one's life. Avoid pork and raw scallions.

## Notes

Cui shi wutou wan: for sudden heart pain (radiating to the back, or long-standing) from wind-cold evil entering the heart network vessels or wind-cold riding on the heart.
- Aconite, Sichuan aconite, Sichuan pepper, dried ginger and cinnamon disperse cold; red halloysite is added. This is the *Jingui yaolüe* Wutou chishizhi wan (Sichuan pepper, aconite, fuzi, dried ginger, red halloysite) plus cinnamon. Master Cui is probably Cui Zhiti (quoted in the *Waitai miyao*), but this has not been verified.
