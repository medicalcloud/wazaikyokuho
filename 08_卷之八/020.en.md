# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之八 020 腸風黒散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Changfeng hei san (Chofukokusan; Intestinal Wind Black Powder)

The patterns treated are the same as for the Changfeng hei san in the section on diarrhea and dysentery.

jingjie (schizonepeta; burned; 2 liang)　zhiqiao (bitter orange; pulp removed; 2 liang burned, 1 liang stir-fried)　luanfa (human hair; burned)　huaihua (sophora flower; burned)　huaijiao (sophora fruit; burned; 1 liang each)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried)　weipi (hedgehog skin; 1½ liang each)

Put together in a porcelain bottle the medicines to be burned, seal with yellow clay, burn so that three-tenths of their nature is preserved, let the fire qi out, and pound and sift together with the licorice and bitter orange into a powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, decoct down to seven-tenths and take it warm on an empty stomach; it may also be taken mixed in warm wine.

## Notes

Changfeng hei san (from here the layer of Chunyou-era additions): the same indications as the Changfeng hei san in the diarrhea section (juan 6, no. 054), but a different formula.
- Schizonepeta, bitter orange, human hair, sophora flower, sophora fruit and hedgehog skin are burned black with nature preserved; licorice is added to stop bleeding.
