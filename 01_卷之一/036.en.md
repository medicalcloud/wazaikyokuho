# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 036 八風散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Bafeng san (Eight Winds Powder)

It treats wind qi attacking upward with clouding and dizziness of the head and eyes and stiff, vexing pain of the limbs, or itching and painful wind sores of the skin; and disordered cold congestion with nasal congestion and a heavy voice.

huoxiang (agastache; earth removed; half a jin)　baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica)　qianhu (peucedanum; rhizome head removed; 1 jin each)　huangqi (ogi; astragalus; rhizome head removed)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; rhizome head removed; 2 jin each)　qianghuo (notopterygium; rhizome head removed)　fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia; rhizome head removed; 3 jin each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one medium *zhan* of water and a little mint, decoct together down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm after meals. One large *qian* mixed in clear *la* tea is also fine. For children's deficiency wind, mix half a *qian* in frankincense and clear *la* tea, adjusting the dose to the child's size.

## Notes

Bafeng san: for clouding of the head, limb pain and wind sores of the skin from wind qi attacking upward, and for nasal congestion.
- Notopterygium, saposhnikovia, angelica dahurica, peucedanum and agastache scatter wind; ginseng, astragalus and licorice supplement qi.
