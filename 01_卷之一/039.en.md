# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 039 骨砕補丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Gusuibu wan (Drynaria Pill)

It is to be taken for wind deficiency of the liver and kidneys attacking upward and pouring downward, with cramping of the sinews and vessels, joint pain, puffiness of the head and face, little strength in the arms, stiff and painful lower back and spine, slack, weak legs and knees, difficulty bending and stretching, and difficulty walking.

jingjie spikes (schizonepeta)　baifuzi (typhonium; blast-fried)　niuxi (goshitsu; achyranthes; soaked in wine and dried over a fire)　roucongrong (cistanche; soaked overnight in wine, sliced and dried over a fire; 1 liang each)　gusuibu (drynaria; hairs removed, stir-fried)　weilingxian (clematis root; sprouts removed)　suosharen (amomum; half a liang each)　dilong (earthworm; earth removed, lightly stir-fried)　moyao (myrrh; 2½ *qian* each)　zirantong (pyrite; quenched in wine nine times)　caowutou (wild aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　banxia (hange; pinellia; washed seven times in hot water; half a liang each)

Make the above together into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with flour paste boiled in wine. Take five to seven pills each time with warm wine; women take them with vinegar decoction or angelica wine. Not to be taken in pregnancy. At any time.

## Notes

Gusuibu wan: for cramping, joint pain, back pain, weak legs and difficulty walking from wind deficiency of the liver and kidneys.
- Drynaria, achyranthes and cistanche supplement the liver, kidneys, sinews and bones; wild aconite, clematis, earthworm, schizonepeta and typhonium dispel wind; myrrh and pyrite move the blood. Contraindicated in pregnancy.
