# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 086 如神丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Rushen wan (Nyoshingan; Divine Pill)

It treats all cold and hot qi, disperses aggregation qi, harmonizes the spleen and stomach and supplements the lower origin.

tiannanxing (arisaema; blast-fried)　qianghuo (notopterygium)　baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried)　jing sanleng (sparganium; soaked in vinegar, blast-fried and pounded)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　banxia (hange; pinellia; washed fourteen times in hot water, stir-fried dry with ginger juice)

Powder the above in equal parts and form pills the size of wutong seeds with flour paste boiled in vinegar. Take twenty to thirty pills each time on an empty stomach with ginger and salt decoction. For diarrhea, take thirty pills with Eryi tang. For children's red dysentery, take three to five pills with licorice and tangerine-peel decoction, adjusted to the child's size; for white dysentery, with dried-ginger decoction.

## Notes

Rushen wan (Wu Zhige's additions): for all cold and hot qi and aggregation qi, said to harmonize the spleen and stomach and supplement the lower origin; also used for diarrhea and children's red and white dysentery.
- Arisaema and pinellia transform phlegm, sparganium disperses accumulations, aconite and dried ginger warm, notopterygium and angelica dahurica dispel wind. The Eryi tang mentioned is not given at this point in the book.
