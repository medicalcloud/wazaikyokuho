# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 017 建中散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jianzhong san (Middle-Fortifying Powder)

It treats disharmony of the spleen and stomach with stagnant qi in the middle duct, chronic cold and lodged rheum accumulating without dispersing, stabbing pain of the heart and abdomen, distension of the flanks and ribs, vomiting with phlegm counterflow, belching and acid swallowing, borborygmus and diarrhoea, undigested water and grain, weary limbs and no desire for food.

Qingzhou zao (jujubes from Qingzhou)　houpo (magnolia bark; processed with ginger juice; 1 jin each)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried)　banxia (pinellia; washed in hot water to remove the slipperiness)　gancao (licorice; 5 liang each)　chenpi (aged tangerine peel; white pith removed; 8 liang)

Boil the above six ingredients in 3 *dou* of water until the water is gone, then dry them over a fire.

caodoukou (Katsumada galangal seed; skin removed)　renshen (ginseng)　huoxiang (agastache)　hezi (chebule; blast-fried, skin taken)　bai fuling (white poria; skin removed)　baizhu (white atractylodes; 1 liang each)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water and three slices of fresh ginger, decoct down to six-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm, before meals.

## Notes

Jianzhong san: for qi stagnation in the middle duct with abdominal pain, flank distension, vomiting, acid reflux, diarrhoea, indigestion, lassitude and poor appetite from chronic cold and lodged rheum.
- Jujube, magnolia bark, dried ginger, pinellia, licorice and tangerine peel are first boiled dry in water, then Katsumada galangal, ginseng, agastache, chebule, poria and atractylodes are added. The Siku quanshu edition calls it Jianzhong tang (see the variants).
