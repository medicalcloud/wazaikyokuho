# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 088 追風散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zhuifeng san (Wind-Chasing Powder)

The patterns it treats are the same as for the earlier Zhuifeng san.

bai jiangcan (silkworm; silk and mouth removed, stir-fried)　quanxie (whole scorpion; lightly stir-fried)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried)　jingjie (schizonepeta; 2 liang each)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia; rhizome head and forks removed)　shigao (sekko; gypsum; ground; 4 liang each)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage; 3 liang)　shexiang (jako; musk; ground; 1 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder. Take half a *qian* each time mixed in good tea, after meals and at bedtime. It clears the head and eyes, frees the throat and diaphragm, disperses wind congestion and transforms phlegm and drool.

## Notes

Zhuifeng san (bureau additions): treats the same patterns as the earlier Zhuifeng san (juan 1, no. 056), with fewer ingredients.
- Sichuan aconite, silkworm and scorpion expel wind-phlegm; lovage, schizonepeta and saposhnikovia scatter wind from the head; gypsum restrains heat; musk opens.
