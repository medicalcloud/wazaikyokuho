# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 058 分心気飲 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Fenxinqi yin (Heart Qi-Separating Drink)

It treats all disharmony of qi in men and women, most often caused by worry, sorrow, thought and anxiety and by anger injuring the spirit, or by grief at mealtimes, or by things not going as one wishes, so that constrained, suppressed qi lingers and stagnates without dispersing, lodges between the chest and diaphragm and cannot flow freely, causing stuffiness and oppression of the heart and chest, deficiency distension of the flanks and ribs, choking obstruction, belching and acid swallowing, retching and nausea, clouding and dizziness of the head and eyes, weary limbs, a withered yellow complexion, a bitter mouth and dry tongue, reduced eating and daily wasting; or deficiency constipation of the large intestine; or deficiency stuffiness of the chest and diaphragm after illness with no desire for food.

muxiang (mokko; costus root; not exposed to fire)　sangbaipi (mulberry root bark; stir-fried; half a liang each)　dingxiangpi (clove bark; 1 liang)　dafuzi (areca seed; blast-fried)　jiegeng (kikyo; platycodon; rhizome head removed, stir-fried)　maimendong (bakumondo; ophiopogon; core removed)　caoguo ren (tsaoko seed)　dafupi (areca husk; roasted)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, processed with ginger juice)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; chopped; half a liang each)　xiangfuzi (cyperus; stir-fried, hairs removed)　zisu (perilla; stalks removed)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　huoxiang (agastache; 1½ liang each)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; 1 liang)

Cut the above into pieces. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, three slices of fresh ginger, one jujube (broken open, stone removed) and ten rush pith stems, decoct down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm, at any time. Another formula appears later.

## Notes

Fenxinqi yin (Baoqing-era additions): for qi constrained by worry, anxiety and anger and stagnating in the chest, with chest stuffiness, flank distension, difficulty swallowing, acid reflux, nausea, dizziness, lassitude, sallow complexion, poor appetite and wasting, for deficiency constipation, and for stuffiness after illness.
- Perilla, cyperus, costus, clove bark, tangerine peel, areca husk and seed, tsaoko, magnolia bark and agastache move constrained qi; ginseng, atractylodes and licorice supplement; mulberry bark, platycodon and ophiopogon moisten and lower the lungs. Another formula of the same name appears later.
