# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 041 七聖丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qisheng wan (Seven Sages Pill)

It treats exuberant congestion of wind qi with phlegm and heat binding and contending, a clouded, heavy head and eyes, thick sticky snivel and spittle, a vexed heart and red face, dry throat and parched mouth, an unrefreshed spirit, unquiet sleep at night, stiffness of the shoulders and back, glomus and oppression of the chest and diaphragm, distension and fullness of the abdomen and flanks, fullness with heavy pain of the lower back, constipation, and red, rough urine.

chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　muxiang (costus root; raw)　qianghuo (notopterygium; rhizome head removed)　binglang (areca; raw; half a liang each)　yuliren (bush cherry kernel; skin removed)　dahuang (rhubarb; steamed and dried over a fire, 1 *fen* used raw; 1 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with refined honey. Take fifteen to twenty pills each time with warm boiled water, after meals and at bedtime. It is most suitable in miasmic regions. Adjust further according to the deficiency or repletion of the viscera.

## Notes

Qisheng wan: for heavy head and eyes, sticky phlegm, vexation, red face, dry mouth and throat, insomnia, stiff shoulders and back, chest glomus, abdominal and flank distension, back pain, constipation and dark, rough urine from exuberant wind qi with bound phlegm-heat; especially suitable in miasmic regions.
- Rhubarb and bush cherry kernel purge; areca and costus move qi; lovage, notopterygium and cinnamon are added—seven ingredients (hence "seven").
