# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 016 化虫丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Huachong wan (Worm-Transforming Pill)

Children's illnesses often involve the various worms. They stir either because of weakness of the bowels and viscera or because of eating sweet and fatty food. When they stir there is pain in the abdomen, swelling and gathering during attacks, moving up and down, pain without rest, also attacking the heart with pain, crying and screaming with eyes closed, arching the body and flailing the hands, oppression and confusion of the heart-spirit, retching of drool and froth or vomiting of clear water, emaciated, weary limbs, a green-yellow complexion, food taken in but not becoming flesh, sometimes cold and sometimes hot, listless and silent, with no clear sign of where the illness lies. If the worms are not treated, offspring and parents breed each other without end, and when they reach one *chi* in length they harm the person.

hufen (lead white; stir-fried)　heshi (carpesium fruit; earth removed)　binglang (areca)　kulian gen (chinaberry root; floating bark removed; 50 liang each)　baifan (alum; dried; 12½ liang)

Powder the above and form pills the size of hemp seeds with flour paste. A one-year-old takes five pills in warm sour rice water with one or two drops of raw sesame oil, mixed evenly; warm rice water is also fine; at any time. The fine, small worms are all transformed into water, and the large ones come down by themselves.

## Notes

Huachong wan: for children's worms (stirred by weak viscera or by sweet, fatty food) causing attacks of abdominal pain, swelling, pain attacking the heart, screaming, arching, vomiting, wasting, poor complexion, chills and fever and listlessness. Untreated worms are said to multiply and harm the child when they reach one *chi*.
- Lead white, carpesium, areca, chinaberry root and dried alum kill worms. It contains lead.
