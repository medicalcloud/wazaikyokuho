# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 052 鉄弾丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Tiedan wan (Iron Pellet Pill)

It treats sudden wind-strike with a clouded, confused spirit, clenched jaws, a fixed staring gaze, convulsions of the hands and feet, deviation of the mouth and face, surging drool with blocked speech, cramped sinews and aching bones, paralysis with one side withered, or numbness and insensibility, or itching at any time—and all wind diseases and pain of the limbs from blows and injuries. It frees the channels and network vessels and quickens the blood.

ruxiang (frankincense; ground separately)　moyao (myrrh; ground separately; 1 liang each)　chuan wutou (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin, tips and navel removed, powdered; 1½ liang)　shexiang (musk; finely ground; 1 *qian*)　wulingzhi (flying-squirrel droppings; soaked in wine, rinsed free of sand and grit, dried in the sun; 4 liang, powdered)

First grind the frankincense and myrrh finely in a cool shady place, then add the musk, then the powdered medicines, and grind again; knead the medicine with drops of water into pills the size of a pellet. Take one pill each time ground and dissolved in mint wine, after meals and at bedtime.

## Notes

Tiedan wan (Shaoxing-era additions): for unconsciousness, clenched jaws, convulsions and paralysis in sudden wind-strike, and for limb pain from injuries.
- Frankincense, myrrh and flying-squirrel droppings move the blood and stop pain; aconite dispels wind-cold; musk frees the channels.
