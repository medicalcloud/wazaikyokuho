# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 049 桂枝湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Guizhi tang (Keishito; Cinnamon Twig Decoction)

It treats Taiyang wind stroke in which yang is floating and yin is weak—with floating yang, heat arises of itself; with weak yin, sweat comes out of itself—with huddled aversion to cold, shivering aversion to wind, feverish heat, noisy nose and dry retching.

guizhi (keishi; cinnamon twig; bark removed)　shaoyao (shakuyaku; peony root; 1½ liang each)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; 1 liang)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, three slices of fresh ginger and three jujubes broken open, decoct together to obtain seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm, at any time. Only at the beginning of spring may it be used [as is]; from the end of spring to before the Summer Solstice, half a liang of scutellaria may be added; after the Summer Solstice add half a liang of anemarrhena and 2 liang of gypsum, or half a liang of cimicifuga. If the patient is constitutionally deficient and cold, make no additions. Do not take it if there is no sweating.

## Notes

Guizhi tang (Wu Zhige's additions): for Taiyang wind stroke (fever, spontaneous sweating, aversion to wind, noisy nose, dry retching): Guizhi tang of *Shanghan lun* clause 12.
- Cinnamon twig, peony and licorice with ginger and jujube. The seasonal additions of scutellaria, anemarrhena, gypsum and cimicifuga resemble the teaching of Zhu Gong's *Leizheng huoren shu*; it is forbidden without sweating (*Shanghan lun* clause 16).
