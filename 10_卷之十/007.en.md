# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 007 至聖丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Zhisheng dan (Supreme Sage Elixir)

It treats all fright wind and upward-hanging gaze, with eyes turned upward, convulsions of the hands and feet and manifold signs. Dissolve one pill of the medicine in warm water and drip it into the nose to cause three to five sneezes, then give two pills with mint decoction, and recovery follows at once. For long-standing five gan disorders with a distended abdomen and large head, thin, small limbs, a liking for eating mud and earth, no desire for milk or food, a liking for biting the fingernails, occasional plucking of the eyebrows, sparse hair and green veins on the belly, and for long-standing diarrhea and dysentery, give two pills with rice water. For long-standing gan roundworms biting the heart with pain that comes and goes, give two pills with decocted chinaberry fruit decoction. For red festering below the nose, gan worms of the mouth and teeth, and mouth sores, grind two pills in the milk the child drinks and apply to the affected place. For gan eyes and sparrow eyes (night blindness), take one white lamb's liver, slit it open with a bamboo knife, put two pills inside, bind it with hemp thread, boil it in rice-washing water until cooked and give it to eat on an empty stomach; also have the wet nurse constantly avoid toxic fish, garlic, chicken, duck, pork and the like.

xiongdan (bear bile; dissolved in warm water and added to the medicine)　luhui (aloe; ground)　nifen (calomel; ground with the mercury)　zhusha (cinnabar; ground and water-ground; 1 *fen* each)　shexiang (jako; musk; ground; half a *fen*)　chansu (toad venom; dried, soaked in wine overnight)　longnao (borneol; ground)　qianshuang (lead acetate; ground; 1 *zi* each)　xionghuang (yuo; realgar; ground and water-ground)　qingdai (indigo; ground)　hu huanglian (oren; picrorhiza; powdered; half a liang each)　bai fuzi (bushi; typhonium tuber; blast-fried; 2 *qian*)　shuiyin (suigin; mercury; 1 *qian*, ground with the calomel until not a speck the size of a rice grain is visible)

Powder the above, mix in the ground medicines evenly and form pills the size of glutinous millet with steamed cake soaked in boiled-down bile of a castrated boar. The vehicles are as above. This medicine drives off fright, treats wind, transforms worms, kills gan, removes the hundred illnesses and improves feeding. If one dose is given every two or three days, there will never be the hundred illnesses, and the child will not contract diseases of untimely death. Whenever it is given to a sick child, it is sure to show effect.

## Notes

Zhisheng dan: for fright wind, upward gaze and convulsions (given after dripping into the nose to induce sneezing); for the five gan disorders (distended abdomen, large head with thin limbs, eating earth, poor appetite, nail-biting, plucking eyebrows, sparse hair, visible abdominal veins); for chronic diarrhea; for abdominal pain from gan roundworms; for festering below the nose, gan worms of the mouth and teeth and mouth sores (applied with breast milk); and for gan eyes and night blindness (cooked inside lamb's liver). Given every few days it is said to prevent illness.
- Bear bile, aloe, picrorhiza and indigo clear gan heat; toad venom, realgar, mercury, calomel and lead acetate kill worms; borneol, musk, cinnabar and typhonium settle fright. It contains mercury and lead.
