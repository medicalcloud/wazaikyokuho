# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 054 腸風黒散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Changfeng hei san (Chofukokusan; Intestinal Wind Black Powder)

It treats all cases where the ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi are deficient and pathogenic wind and cold qi advance into and assail the zang-fu organs, or where eating raw and cold food, eating roasted and grilled food, or excessive drinking lead heat to accumulate in the intestines, making the intestines and stomach weak and deficient so that the dregs do not cohere, with fresh blood in the stools, pain of the navel and abdomen, tenesmus, or prolapse of the anus, or long-standing alcohol dysentery with very frequent stools.

baizong (old palm fiber; burned)　mumantou (climbing fig fruit; burned)　wutou (uzu; aconite [?]; stones removed)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; 2 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, decoct down to seven-tenths and take it warm on an empty stomach.

## Notes

Changfeng hei san (Baoqing-era additions): for bloody stools (intestinal wind), umbilical pain, tenesmus, rectal prolapse and alcohol dysentery when pathogenic wind and cold qi enter through deficient ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi, or heat accumulates in the intestines from raw and cold food, roasted food or drink.
- Old palm fiber and climbing fig fruit are burned black (hence "black") to stop bleeding.
- "Wutou (stones removed)": aconite has no stone, so this may be an error for wumei (ubai; mume), but all sources read "wutou" and it has been left as is.
