# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷中 009 論風湿脚気候 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the signs of wind-damp leg qi. It always arises from the toxic qi of wind-damp entering the legs and knees. The appearance is red swelling, or cold pain, or numbness without sensation, or soft, slack legs, or aversion to cold with vigorous fever and thirst and hypertonic sinews and vessels. Give Yu shanren jiangqi tang (Recluse Yu's Qi-Lowering Decoction), Paifeng tang, Xiao xuming tang (Shozokumeito) or Xiao jiangqi tang. For pain, give Shinan wan (Sekinangan), Zhuifeng yingtong wan or Ruxiang chentong san. For soft legs with inability to walk, give Huangqi wan (Ogigan) or Mugua wan (Mokkagan; Chaenomeles Pill). For rough urinary blockage, give Daochi wan (Dosekigan), Wulin san (Gorinsan) or Sanhe san (Sanwasan). For constipation, give Maren wan (Maningan); for severe constipation, Shengong wan. For surging into the heart with oppression, give Sanhe san. For pain thrusting into the lower back, give Da wuchen tang. Regardless of severity, wash by pouring with Wuxiang san (Gokosan) in all cases. If the legs are swollen with sores, [give] Toubing dan.

## Notes

On wind-damp leg qi (wind-damp toxin in the legs and knees: red swelling, cold pain, numbness, weak legs, chills and fever with thirst, hypertonicity): basically Yu shanren jiangqi tang, Paifeng tang, Xiao xuming tang and Xiao jiangqi tang; for pain Shinan wan, Zhuifeng yingtong wan and Ruxiang chentong san; for inability to walk Huangqi wan and Mugua wan; for rough urination Daochi wan, Wulin san and Sanhe san; for constipation Maren wan and Shengong wan; Sanhe san for surging into the heart; Da wuchen tang for lumbar pain; Wuxiang san externally; Toubing dan for swollen legs with sores.
