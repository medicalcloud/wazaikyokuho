# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 053 煨薑丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Weijiang wan (Roasted Ginger Pill)

It treats deficiency of the zang organ itself with undigested food, sometimes turning into strings and aggregations, sometimes bringing on heart pain; [and treats] cold accumulation and water spleen with binding, gathering and pain, and all cold-qi disorders.

fuzi (bushi; aconite)　naosha (sal ammoniac)　muxiang (mokko; costus root)　shengjiang (shokyo; fresh ginger)

Take fifty large aconite roots weighing half a liang each, remove the skin and navel, hollow out the core with a sharp small knife so that it holds about half a *qian* of sal ammoniac, and fill it. Then knead aconite powder with flour into cakes, wrap the aconite in them, and roast over a "civil and military" fire until yellow; add costus about half the weight of the aconite, powder together finely and form pills the size of a euryale seed with water. Then split a piece of fresh ginger in two, put the medicine inside, wrap it in wet paper and roast it; when the ginger is hot, chew it and take it with plain hot water, on an empty stomach.

## Notes

Weijiang wan (Shaoxing-era additions): for indigestion, strings and aggregations and heart pain from deficiency of the spleen, and for pain from bound cold accumulations and "water spleen", and all cold-qi disorders.
- An elaborate preparation: aconite roots are hollowed out and filled with sal ammoniac, wrapped in aconite-and-flour cakes and roasted, powdered with costus into pills, and then roasted again inside split ginger.
