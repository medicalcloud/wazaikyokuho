# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 108 木香檳榔丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Muxiang binglang wan (Mokkobinrogan; Costus and Areca Pill)

It disperses and guides the sanjiao, loosens and frees the chest and diaphragm, breaks up phlegm and drives out retained fluid, eases qi and disperses food, and frees and moistens the large intestine.

yuliren (ikurinin; bush cherry kernel; skin removed)　zaojiao (soap pod; skin removed, smeared with ghee and roasted)　banxia (hange) qu (pinellia leaven; 2 liang each)　binglang (areca)　zhiqiao (bitter orange; stir-fried with bran)　muxiang (mokko; costus root; not exposed to fire)　xingren (kyonin; apricot kernel; skin and tips removed, stir-fried with bran)　qingpi (green tangerine peel; white removed; 1 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder. Separately knead 4 liang of soap pod in a bowl of fermented rice water and simmer into a paste, add a little refined honey, knead [the powder with it] and form pills the size of wutong seeds. Take fifty pills each time after meals with warm ginger decoction.

## Notes

Muxiang binglang wan (bureau additions; the last formula of juan 3): disperses the sanjiao, loosens the chest, removes phlegm-fluid retention, moves qi, digests food and moistens and frees the large intestine.
- Costus, areca, bitter orange and green tangerine peel send qi down; bush cherry kernel and apricot kernel moisten the intestines; soap pod and pinellia leaven remove phlegm. Its composition differs from the later Muxiang binglang wan (which contains rhubarb and morning-glory seed).
