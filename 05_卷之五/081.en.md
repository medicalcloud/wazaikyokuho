# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 081 養正丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yangzheng dan (Upright-Nourishing Elixir)

(From the *Shanghan lun* of the Perfected Man of Baolin, Gu Boyang; also called Jiaotai dan, Interaction Elixir.) It repels pathogen and supports the healthy qi, assists yang and joins the true. It treats deficient, deficient original qi with yin pathogen sweeping back and forth, healthy qi out of its normal order, exuberance above and deficiency below, qi failing to rise and fall, insufficient breathing, dizziness and shortness of breath, a timid, weak heart-spirit, fright palpitations in dreams, night sweats over the whole body, and abdominal and lumbar pain; or deficiency vexation with manic speech, dry mouth and dyspnea upward, stomach reflux with vomiting of food, sudden turmoil with cramps, and unremitting cough with counterflow. It also treats wind stroke with tidal surging of drool and loss of consciousness, yang qi about to desert, and cold limbs. In cold damage with yin exuberance, spontaneous sweating, green lips and a deep pulse, it is most suitable. It can also treat women's postpartum body heat from blood and qi, uneven menses, vaginal discharge and abdominal pain. Taken regularly it relieves heart fire, strengthens kidney water and improves eating.

shuiyin (suigin; mercury)　liuhuang (sulfur; finely ground)　zhusha (cinnabar; finely ground)　heixi (black tin, i.e. lead; dross removed, weighed, made into granules with the mercury; 1 liang each)

Using one black cup, melt the black tin over the fire into liquid, then add the mercury and stir evenly with a willow twig, then add the cinnabar and stir until no specks are visible; set it down for a while and only then add the sulfur powder, stirring rapidly into a uniform liquid. If flames arise, sprinkle with vinegar; when cool take it out, grind extremely fine like powder and form pills the size of mung beans with paste boiled from glutinous rice flour. Take twenty pills each time, increasing to thirty, with salt water. This medicine makes yin and yang rise and fall and brings the heart and kidneys into balance. Take on an empty stomach before meals with jujube decoction. Its divine efficacy cannot be fully described.

## Notes

Yangzheng dan (Jiaotai dan; Wu Zhige's additions): for dizziness, breathlessness, fright palpitations, night sweats, abdominal and lumbar pain, deficiency vexation, manic speech, dyspnea, stomach reflux, sudden turmoil and cough with exuberance above and deficiency below from deficient original qi and yin pathogen; for wind stroke with drool, unconsciousness, yang desertion and cold limbs; for cold damage with yin exuberance; and for women's postpartum fever, irregular menses and discharge.
- An elixir of mercury, black tin (lead), cinnabar and sulfur melted together (of the Heixi dan [Kokushakutan] type). It contains mercury and lead, is highly toxic and is not used today.
