# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 031 十華散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Shihua san (Ten Flowers Powder)

It treats men's five taxations and seven damages with pain all over the body, cramping of the four limbs, weakness of the lower back and knees, deficiency of the original qi of the spleen with no desire for food, vomiting and diarrhoea of sudden turmoil, and cold, numb limbs. It also resolves cold damage of the two toxins and treats leg qi flowing and pouring with swelling, pain and inability to walk, as well as deficiency taxation and similar illnesses.

wujiapi (acanthopanax bark)　chenpi (aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried)　gancao (licorice; 6 liang each)　jiegeng (platycodon)　qianghuo (notopterygium)　huangqi (astragalus)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; skin removed, stir-fried; 8 liang 8 *qian* each)　fuzi (aconite; 6 liang)　large chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; 3 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, two slices of fresh ginger and one jujube, decoct down to six-tenths; at any time. It may also be mixed in hot salted wine.

## Notes

Shihua san (Shaoxing-era additions): for general pain, limb cramping and weak lower back and knees in the five taxations and seven damages, poor appetite from spleen deficiency, vomiting, diarrhoea and cold limbs in sudden turmoil, cold damage of the two toxins, leg qi and deficiency taxation.
- Aconite, Sichuan aconite, dried ginger and cinnamon warm; acanthopanax, notopterygium and black atractylodes dispel wind-damp; astragalus and licorice supplement.
- The base text reads 四技 for 四肢 ("four limbs"); since the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions both read 四肢, it has been emended (see the emendation).
