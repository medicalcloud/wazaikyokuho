# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 049 肥児丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Fei'er wan (Child-Fattening Pill)

Gan disorders in children mostly arise from insufficient milk and eating food too early; or from long-standing illness of the viscera, a deficient stomach and stirring worms, with daily wasting, a large abdomen and bristling hair, inability to walk, a yellow face, bad breath and fever, and a face lacking vitality. This medicine kills worms and improves eating.

shenqu (medicated leaven; stir-fried)　huanglian (coptis; fibrous roots removed; 10 liang each)　roudoukou (nutmeg; wrapped in dough and roasted in embers)　shijunzi (quisqualis fruit; husk removed)　mainie (barley sprout; stir-fried; 5 liang each)　binglang (areca; not exposed to fire, finely chopped, sun-dried; 20)　muxiang (costus root; 2 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of millet grains with pig's bile. Take thirty pills each time, adjusted for age, with boiled water on an empty stomach. (One formula uses 1 liang each of coptis, medicated leaven and quisqualis, half a liang each of areca and nutmeg and 2 *qian* of costus, made into pills the size of radish seeds with flour paste and swallowed with boiled water.)

## Notes

Fei'er wan (from here the layer of Baoqing-era additions): for gan disorders (wasting, large abdomen, bristling hair, inability to walk, sallowness, bad breath, fever, listlessness) arising from insufficient milk, premature solid food, or chronic visceral illness with a deficient stomach and stirring worms; kills worms and improves appetite. Later known as a representative formula for children's gan.
- Quisqualis and areca kill worms; coptis clears gan heat; medicated leaven and barley sprout aid digestion; nutmeg and costus move qi; made into pills with pig's bile.
