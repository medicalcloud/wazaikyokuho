# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 011 竜虎丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Longhu dan (Dragon and Tiger Elixir)

It treats men and women with acute or slow wind, newly contracted or long-standing, with loss of control of half the body and weakness of the sinews of the hands and feet; and wind toxin attacking and pouring, with sores and scabies all over the body; head wind with much white dandruff; toxic wind with sores on the face; stabbing wind feeling like needle pricks; epileptic wind with sudden falling and crying out; stubborn wind with no sense of pain or itch; scrofulous wind with mottling on the neck; dark wind with spinning head and blackness before the eyes; *ci* wind with red spots on the face; liver wind with stuffy nose and twitching eyes; one-sided wind with deviation of the mouth and eyes; joint wind with the limbs feeling severed and rejoined; spleen wind with frequent nausea; wine wind with inability to walk; lung wind with nasal congestion and neck pain; gallbladder wind causing sleeplessness; qi wind with a feeling of insects crawling in the flesh; kidney wind with cicada-like ringing in the ears; and damp itching in the genital region.

hei qianniu (black morning-glory seed; scorched)　huoxiang leaves (agastache; raw)　tianma (gastrodia; sprouts removed)　niuxi (achyranthes; sprouts removed, soaked in wine, sliced, dried over a fire and lightly stir-fried)　liuhuang (sulphur; fused [with mercury] into "sand")　tianzhuhuang (tabasheer; ground raw)　xixin (asarum; sprouts removed, washed)　banxia (pinellia; washed seven times in hot water and processed with ginger juice)　fuzi (aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　heshouwu (fleeceflower root; coarse skin removed)　qianghuo (notopterygium; sprouts removed, washed and dried over a fire)　duhuo (angelica pubescens root; sprouts removed)　chaihu (bupleurum; sprouts removed)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage; washed)　jiegeng (platycodon; raw; 2 liang each)　hanshuishi (calcite; burned red-hot, ground and water-ground) 1 jin　huixiang (fennel; rinsed, earth removed, dried over a fire)　gansong (nardostachys; washed free of earth, dried over a fire)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　wulingzhi (flying-squirrel droppings; raw)　baizhi (angelica dahurica; raw)　juhua (chrysanthemum; earth removed)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　bai jiangcan (silkworm; silk and mouth removed, stir-fried)　suoshaner (amomum; raw; 5 liang each)　yaxiao (saltpetre; ground)　muxiang (costus root; raw)　shuiyin (mercury; fused with the sulphur over a gentle fire into "sand")　xionghuang (realgar; ground and water-ground)　shexiang (musk; ground; 1 liang each)　dilong (earthworm; earth removed, scorched)　bai ganjiang (white dried ginger; blast-fried)　zhusha (cinnabar; ground and water-ground)　bai jili (tribulus; scorched)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; sprouts removed; 3 liang each)　wushe (black-striped snake; soaked in wine, roasted, skin and bones removed; 8 liang)　longnao (borneol; ground; half a liang)

Make the above into a fine powder and form a mass with refined honey. Each dose is one pill the size of a euryale seed, chewed and taken with mint wine; one dose a day, two if severe. For postpartum fright wind with raving and seeing things, grind it in cinnabar wine. For postpartum deficiency swelling of the body and blood wind, with increasing torpor, body feeling as if pricked by needles, hair falling out with combing, a yellow face and counterflow of the heart, decoct angelica wine and chew it with that, two doses a day. To treat cold damage, stir-fry scallion and fermented soybeans and chew one or two doses with wine, then cover up to sweat; it is cured at once. For children's fright wind, dissolve a little in mint wine. For adults with acute wind, clenched jaws and loss of voice, dissolve it in mint wine and pour it in. For regular use take with tea or wine as preferred, at any time.

## Notes

Longhu dan: said to treat hemiplegia from acute or slow wind, sores and scabies from wind toxin, and nearly twenty named kinds of "wind" (head wind, toxic wind, stabbing wind, epileptic wind, etc.).
- A large formula combining wind-dispelling and yang-warming drugs (morning-glory seed, gastrodia, notopterygium, duhuo, aconite, snake) with sulphur fused with mercury, calcite and saltpetre.
- It is taken with different vehicles for postpartum fright wind and blood wind, for inducing sweat in cold damage, and for children's fright wind.
- The Siku quanshu edition differs in doses and processing (e.g. "1 liang each" for "2 liang each"; see the variants).
