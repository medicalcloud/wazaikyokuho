# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 042 感応丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Ganying wan (Responsive Pill)

It treats accumulated cold in a deficient middle with weak qi and some damage, so that [food] lodges and accumulates in the stomach duct and cannot be transformed; or cold damage through qi, overeating after hunger, or excessive drunkenness, with hard fullness below the heart, distending pain of both flanks, great pain of the heart and abdomen, vomiting and diarrhea of sudden turmoil, frequent continuous stools with heavy, slow, rough tenesmus, long-standing red and white dysentery mixed with pus and blood, undigested grain, recurring after recovery. It also treats drunkenness with vomiting, phlegm counterflow and nausea, sleepiness and spinning head, chest stuffiness and oppression, weary limbs and no desire for food. It also treats cold damage in pregnancy, injury soon after childbirth, and long-standing accumulated cold unresponsive to hot medicines. It also treats long illness with emaciation dragging on for years, gradually becoming weak, with a yellow face and wasted flesh, eating sometimes more and sometimes less, and stools sometimes constipated and sometimes loose. Whether the accumulated cold is old or new, it treats it all. Even a major illness shows complete recovery within three doses. This medicine is warm and non-toxic, neither drying nor heating; it does not harm the stomach qi nor cause vomiting or diarrhea, but merely grinds down and transforms accumulations, disperses and drives out warmth and cold, treats damage from food and drink, and eases stagnant qi of the sanjiao. Roll pills the size of mung beans as needed; take three to five pills each time, adjusted according to deficiency or excess, swallowed with warm water, at any time. Taken regularly it improves eating, disperses wine toxin and prevents drunkenness. It also treats children with weak spleen and stomach repeatedly injured and stagnant, with white, fishy-smelling stools and diarrhea of water and grain: give five pills the size of millet grains with dried-ginger decoction, at any time. [It is for] the above illnesses dragging on for months when hot medicines and purging have both failed.

baicaoshuang (pot soot; scraped from the bottom of a village household's cooking pot, finely ground, weighed at 2 liang)　xingren (kyonin; apricot kernels; picked clean, double kernels removed, 140, tips removed, soaked overnight in hot water, skins removed, ground separately to a soft paste)　southern muxiang (mokko; costus root; rhizome head removed; 2½ liang)　dingxiang (clove; freshly picked; 1½ liang)　chuan ganjiang (kankyo; Sichuan dried ginger; processed by blast-frying; 1 liang)　roudoukou (nutmeg; coarse skin removed, using the smooth-skinned kernels; 20)　badou (hazu; croton seeds; 70, skin, core and membranes removed, finely ground, all oil pressed out until like powder)

Except for the croton powder, pot soot and apricot kernels, pound the remaining four ingredients into a fine powder, mix with the three, grind fine, and knead it encased in good wax. First melt 6 liang of wax into liquid and strain through a double layer of floss to remove the dregs; boil the wax in 1 *sheng* of good wine in a silver or stone vessel until melted, bring it to the boil several times and pour it out; when the wine has cooled the wax floats up by itself; take the wax and weigh it for use. When compounding in spring or summer, heat 1 liang of clear oil in a pot until the powder disperses fragrant and cooked, then add 4 liang of the wine-boiled wax, melt them together into liquid and, while hot in the pot, mix in the powdered medicines; when compounding in autumn or winter, use 1½ liang of clear oil, heat them together into a hot liquid, encase and knead the powder into a mass, divide it into small sticks, wrap them in oiled paper and roll pills as needed. This is the formula of the Gao family of the Palace Command.

## Notes

Ganying wan (Shaoxing-era additions): for old or new accumulated cold—hard fullness, flank and abdominal pain, sudden turmoil, diarrhea and chronic dysentery after damage from food or wine, hangover, cold in pregnancy and after childbirth, weakness from chronic illness, and children's indigestive diarrhea.
- Croton frost grinds down accumulations; pot soot, apricot kernel, costus, clove, dried ginger and nutmeg are encased in wax and made into pills; it is said to be warm, non-toxic and not to cause vomiting or diarrhea. Attributed to the Gao family of the Palace Command.
