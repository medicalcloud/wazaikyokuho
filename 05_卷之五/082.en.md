# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 082 黒錫丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Heixi dan (Black Tin Elixir)

(A formula received by the Great Master Ciji of Danyang from the divine immortal Lord Sang.) It treats long-standing cold of the spleen origin with repletion above and deficiency below and phlegm-rheum in the chest, sometimes attacking the head and eyes upward with piercing pain, staring eyes and dizziness; running-piglet qi surging upward, the chest and abdomen together with both flanks distended with unbearable stabbing pain and the breath about to expire; yin and yang qi failing to rise and fall above and below, inability to eat, a yellow face and emaciation, swollen limbs, the five kinds of water qi, and leg qi attacking upward; swollen, painful gums, sores filling the mouth and teeth about to fall out; and also spleen-cold heart pain with unceasing cold sweat. Or in sudden wind-strike with phlegm surging up above the diaphragm, difficult speech, a clouded spirit and disordered qi, phlegm rattling in the throat, an appearance like paralysis, where wind medicines have already been used to induce vomiting without result, one should use a hundred pills of this medicine, pour them in with decocted ginger and jujube decoction, pressing down the wind-drool; consciousness returns at once and the wind-drool descends by itself. Or for exposure to cold evil with sudden turmoil, vomiting and diarrhoea, counterflow cold of the hands and feet and green-black lips and mouth; men with impotence, aching, soft legs and knees, lack of strength in walking, deficiency rumbling of the navel and abdomen and long-standing slippery stools; and women with long-standing cold of the sea of blood, spontaneous white discharge, childlessness for years, and blood and qi attacking and pouring into the head, face and limbs: all should take it. It also treats vexing blockage of the diaphragm and stomach and phlegm-rheum with deficiency panting that a hundred medicines have failed to cure. Taken regularly it digests food, nourishes essence and spirit, generates yang and expels yin, dissolves and grinds away cold stagnation, removes damp and breaks aggregations without disturbing the true qi, so that the five viscera are calm, the six bowels regulated and free, and the hundred illnesses do not invade.

A song says: "When yin is damaged and yang declines, one can truly be harmed; even regulating the nutritive and defensive is hard to manage. For those with weak qi, moving blood and phlegm arising, try listening to what Lord Sang sets forth." Another song: "Husband and wife (black tin and sulphur) unite, the work is done in four; toosendan and aquilaria borrow one liang. Costus, aconite, fenugreek, actinolite and psoralea, cinnamon, fennel and nutmeg are equal without difference. Wutong-sized with wine paste, carefully refined, reversing age and returning to youth is a thing to be praised."

chenxiang (aquilaria; shaved)　fuzi (aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　huluba (fenugreek; soaked in wine, stir-fried)　yangqishi (actinolite; finely ground and water-ground)　huixiang (fennel; imported by ship, stir-fried)　poguzhi (psoralea; soaked in wine, stir-fried)　roudoukou (nutmeg; wrapped in dough and roasted in embers)　jinlingzi (toosendan fruit; steamed, skin and stones removed)　muxiang (costus root; 1 liang each)　rougui (cinnamon bark; bark removed; only half a liang)　heixi (black tin, i.e. lead; dross removed, weighed)　liuhuang (sulphur; clear pieces, made into granules; 2 liang each)

In a black cup or a new iron pot, make the black tin and sulphur into granules in the usual way, draw out the fire toxin on the ground, and grind extremely fine. Pound and sift the remaining medicines into a fine powder, combine everything and grind it evenly from morning to evening until it is black and shiny, and form pills the size of wutong seeds with wine paste. Dry them in the shade, put them in a cloth bag and rub them until glossy. Take thirty or forty pills each time on an empty stomach with ginger-salt decoction or jujube decoction; women take them with mugwort-vinegar decoction.

## Notes

Heixi dan (Wu Zhige's additions): for phlegm-rheum, headache and dizziness from long-standing cold of the spleen origin with repletion above and deficiency below; for running piglet surging upward; for poor appetite, oedema, water qi and leg qi when yin and yang fail to rise and fall; for swollen gums and mouth sores; for spleen-cold heart pain; for phlegm surging up in wind-strike (pour in a hundred pills); for sudden turmoil from cold evil; for impotence and diarrhoea in men; for cold sea of blood, discharge and infertility in women; and for deficiency panting. Later regarded as a representative formula for panting with exuberance above and deficiency below (kidneys failing to grasp qi).
- Black tin (lead) and sulphur made into granules, plus aquilaria, aconite, fenugreek, actinolite, fennel, psoralea, nutmeg, toosendan, costus and cinnamon. Two mnemonic songs for the ingredients are included. It contains lead, is toxic and is hardly used today.
