# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 072 乳香宣経丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Ruxiang xuanjing wan (Frankincense Channel-Freeing Pill)

It treats bodily deficiency invaded in turn by wind, damp, cold and summer heat, the four qi contending with one another, with hemiplegia, stubborn numbness of the hands and feet, vexing pain of the joints, swelling of the shins, aversion to cold and fever, gradually becoming leg qi; insufficiency of the liver and kidneys with cramping of the four limbs and [pain] attacking and pouring through the whole body; or sprains and blows injuring the sinews and bones within; men's mounting qi (shan) and women's irregular menstruation. Taken regularly it quickens the blood and stops pain, supplements deficiency and strengthens the sinews and bones.

chuanlianzi (toosendan fruit; chopped and stir-fried)　qianniuzi (morning-glory seed; stir-fried)　wuyao (lindera; wood removed)　huixiang (fennel; rinsed free of sand and earth, stir-fried)　jupi (kippi; tangerine peel; white pith removed)　bixie (dioscorea hypoglauca; lightly roasted)　fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia; 2 liang each)　ruxiang (frankincense; ground)　caowu (wild aconite; boiled with 1 *ge* of black soybeans until, cut with a bamboo knife, it is black throughout; skin and tips removed, dried over a fire)　wulingzhi (flying-squirrel droppings; soaked in wine, rinsed free of sand and grit, dried in the sun and ground; half a liang each)　weilingxian (clematis root; rhizome head removed, washed; 2 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with wine paste. Take fifty pills each time with salt water or salted wine as preferred; women take them with vinegar decoction.

## Notes

Ruxiang xuanjing wan (Wu Zhige's additions): for paralysis, numbness, joint pain and swollen shins (incipient leg qi) from combined wind, damp, cold and summer heat, for limb cramping from liver-kidney insufficiency, for sprains and blows, men's mounting qi and women's irregular menses.
- Toosendan, lindera, fennel and tangerine peel move qi; dioscorea, saposhnikovia, clematis and wild aconite dispel wind-damp; frankincense and flying-squirrel droppings move the blood.
