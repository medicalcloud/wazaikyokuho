# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷中 001 中風総論 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the signs of wind stroke.

General discussion of wind stroke. Wind is the vast qi of heaven and earth; when it is correct and smooth it can generate and grow all things, but when it is biased and evil it injures all kinds of beings. When a person is struck by this pathogen, few fail to die; therefore when it enters the zang organs it is hard to cure. If it strikes and injures when the meridians and collaterals are empty, the result is hemiplegia, paralysis of the hands and feet, tidal surging of drool with clouded, blocked consciousness, deviation of the mouth and eyes, numbness of the skin, and withering, hypertonicity and distortion. It differs according to the qi of the zang organ affected, on the left or on the right; the side with pathogenic qi becomes slack and the side with healthy qi becomes taut, the healthy qi pulling against the pathogen, producing deviation and loss of use. The nature of wind is violent and fierce, it moves readily and changes often; it strikes people suddenly, makes them dizzy, provokes drool, clouds them and confuses the spirit. Therefore it is regarded as the chief of the hundred illnesses; the sages put it first in their teaching, and the imperial physicians in compiling this work discussed wind stroke first.

## Notes

The opening general discussion of "On the signs of wind stroke" in the General Guide, middle volume: wind nurtures all things when correct and harms when evil; entering the zang organs it is hard to cure; striking empty meridians it causes hemiplegia, paralysis, drool, facial deviation and numbness, the affected side slackening and the sound side pulling taut; wind is the chief of the hundred illnesses and is therefore discussed first.
- Under the heading "On the signs of wind stroke" the middle volume contains not only the discussions of wind stroke that follow but also those on cold damage (from no. 020 on), as in the base text's heading structure.
