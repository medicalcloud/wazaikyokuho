# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 070 纏金丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chanjin dan (Gold-Wrapped Elixir)

It is suitable for adults and children with all kinds of diarrhoea and dysentery, whether cold or hot, red or white, dragging on without healing or recurring after healing, with pain in the abdomen.

naosha (sal ammoniac)　ruxiang (frankincense; 2½ *qian* each)　xingren (apricot kernel; skin and tips removed)　badou (croton seed; skin, germ and membrane removed, oil expressed; 8½ *qian* each)　huangla (yellow beeswax)　zhusha (cinnabar; 1 liang each)　mubie (momordica seed; half a liang)　baijiaoxiang (liquidambar resin; 1 *qian*)　huangdan (red lead; 2½ liang)　pishuang (arsenic trioxide; boiled in vinegar and calcined; 3½ *qian*)

Grind the above into a fine powder, knead with melted wax and form pills the size of hemp seeds. Take one pill each time; children half a pill. For watery diarrhoea give it with freshly drawn water; for red dysentery with licorice decoction; for white dysentery with dried-ginger decoction; for red and white dysentery with licorice and dried-ginger decoction. In all cases give it cooled at bedtime. Pregnant women must not take it. Avoid hot things for one or two *shichen*.

## Notes

Chanjin dan (Wu Zhige's additions): nearly the same indications and vehicles as the earlier Bu'er wan (juan 6, no. 035); for protracted or recurrent diarrhoea and dysentery, cold or hot, red or white.
- A highly toxic formula of croton seed, arsenic trioxide, red lead, sal ammoniac, momordica seed and others made into wax pills. Contraindicated in pregnancy. Not used today.
