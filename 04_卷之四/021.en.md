# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之四 021 人参養肺丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Renshen yangfei wan (Ninjinyohaigan; Ginseng Lung-Nourishing Pill)

It treats injury to both lungs and stomach with qi rushing upward, visiting heat fuming the lungs, cough with urgent breathing, vexing palpitation in the chest, thick sticky mucus and spittle, sometimes with fresh blood, rising qi with urgent dyspnea, inability to lie in peace, weary aching limbs, dry throat and mouth, reduced eating, gradual emaciation and weakness with dyspnea exhaustion; or falls with fright, or tumbles while crossing water; or injury to the lungs and stomach from shouting in anger or from sexual exhaustion while drunk and overfed, with spitting and vomiting of blood.

huangqi (ogi; astragalus; rhizome head removed, smeared with honey and roasted)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; 1 liang 8 *qian* each)　bai fuling (bukuryo; white poria; skin removed)　gualou root (trichosanthes root; 6 liang each)　xingren (kyonin; apricot kernel; skin and tips removed, stir-fried with bran; 2 liang 4 *qian*)　zaojiaozi (soap-pod seeds; stir-fried; 300)　banxia (hange; pinellia; washed and powdered, made into leaven with ginger juice; 4 liang, stir-fried)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of a pellet with refined honey. Chew one pill thoroughly each time after meals and wash it down with perilla decoction; for urgent dyspnea, take it with mulberry-bark decoction.

## Notes

Renshen yangfei wan (Baoqing-era additions): for cough, shortness of breath, palpitation, sticky and blood-streaked sputum, dyspnea, dry mouth and emaciation when the lungs and stomach are injured, qi rushes upward and visiting heat fumes the lungs, and for spitting and vomiting of blood after falls, fright, anger or sexual exhaustion while drunk.
- Ginseng, astragalus and poria supplement lung qi; trichosanthes root moistens; apricot kernel, soap-pod seed and pinellia leaven treat phlegm and cough.
- The base text reads 咳熱熏肺 for 客熱熏肺 ("visiting heat fumes the lungs"); since the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions both read 客熱, it has been emended (see the emendation).
