# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 076 薑合丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jianghe wan (Ginger-Box Pill)

It treats men and women with weak qi and blood and long-accumulated yin cold lingering and stagnating without transforming, binding and gathering into a form, with distension of the heart and abdomen and labor-like stabbing pain reaching up into the chest and flanks; or long-standing deficiency of the spleen and stomach with internal damage from cold foods, pouring diarrhea, abdominal pain and borborygmus; or long-standing dysentery that is entirely white, at times passing blue-black matter, with uncontrollable slippery intestines. It also treats lodged phlegm in the stomach duct with vomiting and acid swallowing, blockage, no desire for food, a heavy body and a withered yellow complexion; and long-standing pain of the heart and spleen—taking it removes the root for good.

dingxiang (clove; not exposed to fire)　muxiang (mokko; costus root; not exposed to fire)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; 1 liang each)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes; dried over a fire)　qingpi (green tangerine peel; white removed)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed; 2 liang each)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; 2½ liang)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, roasted with ginger juice)　roudoukou (nutmeg; blast-fried; 2 liang each)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried; 3 liang)

Make the above medicines into a fine powder, add 8 *qian* of sal ammoniac, and form pills with a paste of ginger juice and flour, twenty pills to each liang. For each dose take one pill: cut open a piece of old ginger the size of the top of a thumb to make a little lidded box, put the pill inside, wrap it in wet paper and roast over a gentle fire for about the time of a meal; take it out, remove the paper, chew it thoroughly together with the ginger and wash it down with plain hot water. Pregnant women must not take it. Children divide one pill into four doses. For old people and children with internal damage and accumulations, it never fails to work divinely. This medicine does not harm the zang-fu organs.

## Notes

Jianghe wan (Wu Zhige's additions): for accumulations, abdominal distension and stabbing pain from long-accumulated yin cold with weak qi and blood, for diarrhea and abdominal pain from chronic spleen-stomach deficiency, chronic dysentery, vomiting, acid reflux, stuffiness and poor appetite from lodged phlegm, and chronic heart and spleen pain.
- Aconite, dried ginger, clove and nutmeg warm; ginseng and atractylodes supplement; costus, tangerine peels and magnolia bark move qi; sal ammoniac transforms accumulations. The pill is roasted inside a piece of ginger and chewed with it (hence "ginger box"). Contraindicated in pregnancy.
