# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷中 023 論傷寒表証 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On exterior patterns of cold damage. In cold damage, one or two days after falling ill, with headache, body pain, aversion to cold, or slight dyspnea: for the constitutionally deficient and the elderly, Wuji san (Goshakusan; Five Accumulations Powder) or Sheng sanzi (Sage Powder) may be given. These two medicines must not be given when the illness has lasted many days or when there is no aversion to wind, nor taken lightly in summer and autumn. Because summer heat stroke resembles cold damage, if a person with summer heat stroke takes these two medicines by mistake it is like carrying firewood to save [a house] from fire, and the harm is not slight; one must examine carefully and use medicines with care. For the young and strong, in summer and autumn, medicines such as Jinfeicao san (Kinfutsusosan; Inula Powder), Laisu san, Renshen qinggu san, Congbai san (Sohakusan; Scallion Powder), Hejie san (Harmonizing and Resolving Powder) and Shenzhu san may be taken. One or two days after falling ill, with headache, fever, body pain, aversion to cold and no sweating, give Congbai san, Bajie san, Jinfeicao san or Renshen qinggu san. One, two or three days after falling ill, with spontaneous sweating, headache, aversion to wind or vomiting, Shengma gegen tang (Shomakakkonto; Cimicifuga and Pueraria Decoction), Baidu san (Toxin-Vanquishing Powder), Xiangsu san (Kososan; Cyperus and Perilla Powder), Congbai san, Renshen qinggu san, Hejie san, Shenzhu san and the like may all be used. If the fever has already subsided after sweating, give qi-harmonizing medicines such as Jiahe san or Zhengqi san.

## Notes

The General Guide's discussion of exterior patterns of cold damage: which formulas of this book to use in the early, exterior stage according to constitution (weak or elderly versus strong), season and the presence of sweating.
- Wuji san and Sheng sanzi are strongly warming, hence the warning against misusing them for summer heat.
- All the formulas named are found in this book, mainly in juan 2 ("treating cold damage").
- The Siku quanshu edition reads 投火 for 救火, 神禾散 for 神朮散, and 汗已退 for 熱已退 (see the variants).
