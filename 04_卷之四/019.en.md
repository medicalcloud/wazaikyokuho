# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之四 019 温肺湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wenfei tang (Lung-Warming Decoction)

It treats lung deficiency with cold fluid retention lodged for a long time, which in attacks causes dyspnea and cough so that one can neither sit nor lie, vomiting of phlegm and froth, and no desire for food.

bai shaoyao (shakuyaku; white peony root; 6 liang)　wuweizi (gomishi; schisandra; stalks removed, stir-fried)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　banxia (hange; pinellia; boiled until cooked, dried over a fire)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　xingren (kyonin; apricot kernel)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; stir-fried; 3 liang each)　xixin (saishin; asarum; rhizome head removed, washed; 2 liang)

Chop the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take three large *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, decoct down to eight-tenths, wring the liquid out through silk and take it after meals. The dregs of two doses are decocted again as one dose. One version omits white peony and asarum; it may be modified in use.

## Notes

Wenfei tang (Shaoxing-era additions): for long-standing cold fluid retention lodged in deficient lungs, with attacks of dyspnea and cough that prevent sitting or lying, vomiting of frothy phlegm and poor appetite.
- Dried ginger, asarum, schisandra, cinnamon, pinellia, peony and licorice are close to Xiao qinglong tang (Shoseiryuto) without ephedra, with tangerine peel and apricot kernel added.
- The base text reads 久咳寒飲 for 久客寒飲 ("cold fluid retention lodged long"); since the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions both read 久客寒飲, it has been emended (see the emendation).
