# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之七 021 湯泡散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Tangpao san (Hot-Steep Powder)

It treats all cases of insufficiency of the liver meridian with visiting heat and wind congestion attacking upward, red, rough eyes, painful pupils and festering lids, fear of the sun and aversion to light, much tearing at night when lying down, seasonal sudden red eyes with pain at both temples (Taiyang points), dizziness, unclear vision and gradual formation of nebulae and membranes.

chi shaoyao (shakuyaku; red peony root)　danggui (toki; Chinese angelica; washed, dried over a fire)　huanglian (oren; coptis; fibrous roots removed)

Take equal parts of the above, pound and sift into a fine powder. Each time steep 2 *qian* in rolling boiling water and, while hot, steam and wash the eyes; when it cools, warm it again and wash; wash three to five times a day until healed. Avoid salted and preserved foods and toxic things. (Its explanation says: All diseases of the eyes are caused by congealed, stagnant blood; therefore they are treated with blood-moving medicines combined with coptis. Blood moves when it obtains heat, so when washing is done while hot, it never fails to work.)

## Notes

Tangpao san (bureau additions): for red, rough eyes, painful pupils, festering lids, photophobia and night tearing from an insufficient liver meridian with visiting heat and wind congestion, and for epidemic sudden red eyes with temple pain, dizziness, poor sight and nebulae.
- An external wash of red peony, angelica and coptis steeped in boiling water, used with the steam while hot. A note explains that eye diseases arise from congealed blood, so blood-moving drugs are applied hot.
