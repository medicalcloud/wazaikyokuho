# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 006 椒紅丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jiaohong wan (Sichuan Pepper Husk Pill)

It treats women with disharmony of blood and qi, timid, weak bowels and zang organs, wind-cold pathogenic qi taking advantage of deficiency to lodge and contend, cold pain of the navel and abdomen, occasional distension of the flanks and ribs, a withered yellow complexion, an emaciated body, lassitude with a liking for lying down, and no desire for food. Taken regularly it supplements deficiency and damage, warms the lower zang organs, expels chronic cold and improves eating.

chenxiang (aquilaria)　ezhu (zedoary)　helile (kariroku; chebule; roasted in embers, stone removed)　jiaohong (Sichuan pepper husk; lightly stir-fried to sweat out the oil)　danggui (toki) (Chinese angelica; rhizome head removed, soaked in wine, lightly stir-fried)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes; 1 liang each)　shexiang (jako; musk; 1 *fen*, ground separately)　dingxiang (clove)　roudoukou (nutmeg; blast-fried)　gao liangjiang (lesser galangal; rhizome head removed, stir-fried with sesame oil; half a liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder, mix in the musk evenly and form pills the size of wutong seeds with flour paste boiled in wine. Take thirty pills each time with warm wine, on an empty stomach before meals.

## Notes

Jiaohong wan: for cold umbilical pain, flank distension, sallow complexion, wasting, lassitude and poor appetite when wind-cold rides on disharmonious blood and qi and weak zang organs in women.
- Sichuan pepper husk, aconite, lesser galangal and clove warm; aquilaria, zedoary and musk move qi and blood; nutmeg, chebule, atractylodes and angelica are added.
