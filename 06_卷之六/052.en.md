# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 052 金粟湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jinsu tang (Golden Millet Decoction)

It treats all diarrhea and dysentery in men, women, unmarried girls and children, recent or long-standing, with disharmony of cold and heat, without limit day and night, diarrhea as soon as gripping pain of the navel and abdomen occurs, weary limbs, blocked, rough urination, no desire for food, and gradual emaciation. It is also suitable for damage by raw and cold food with timid, weak spleen and stomach, undigested food, abdominal distension with thunderous rumbling, and unceasing diarrhea that has not healed for months.

chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed; 1 liang 1 *fen*)　cheqianzi (shazenshi; plantago seed; stir-fried; 4 liang)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried; 2 liang)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; stir-fried)　yingsuqiao (poppy husk; pulp and stalk removed, stir-fried with honey; half a jin each)

Powder the above. For each dose take two large *qian* with one *zhan* of water, one jujube and two slices of fresh ginger, decoct down to seven-tenths and take it fairly hot on an empty stomach before meals; it may also be taken mixed in rice water. Avoid raw and cold, greasy and fishy food, pickled fish, sauces and the like.

## Notes

Jinsu tang (Baoqing-era additions): for all diarrhea and dysentery, recent or long-standing, in people of any age or sex, with gripping umbilical pain, fatigue, rough urination, poor appetite and wasting; and for indigestion, abdominal distension, borborygmus and protracted diarrhea after damage by raw and cold food.
- Poppy husk astringes; dried ginger warms; plantago seed promotes urination to firm the stools; tangerine peel and licorice are added.
