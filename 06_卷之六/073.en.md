# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 073 三神丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Sanshen wan (Three Spirits Pill)

It is suitable for failure of the clear and turbid to separate, with pouring diarrhoea, red or white, gripping pain of the navel and abdomen and tenesmus.

caowu (wild aconite; three pieces, each with skin and tip removed: one raw, one blast-fried, one burned to ash)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of radish seeds with vinegar paste. Adults take five to seven pills, children three. For watery diarrhoea give them with back-flowing water; for red dysentery with licorice decoction; for white dysentery with dried-ginger decoction.

## Notes

Sanshen wan (Wu Zhige's additions): for pouring diarrhoea with failure of the clear and turbid to separate, red or white dysentery, gripping umbilical pain and tenesmus.
- Three wild aconite roots processed three ways—raw, blast-fried and burned to ash (hence "three"). Highly toxic.
