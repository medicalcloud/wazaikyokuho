# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 042 七宣丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qixuan wan (Seven Dispersals Pill)

It treats bound, gathered wind qi, undigested lodged food together with sand, stones, skin or hair in the abdomen; long-standing pain of the lower back and legs, cold as ice or stone; leg qi surging into the heart with vexation, oppression and confusion; dizziness with blackout and collapse; heavy pain of the shoulders and back; distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen; blockage of the chest and diaphragm; swollen wind-toxin qi extending to the head and face; stools sometimes constipated and urine at times rough; stuffiness of spleen and stomach qi with inability to eat; cramps from leg qi with pulling pain and hypertonicity; an abstracted heart-spirit and unquiet sleep; and similar disorders.

chaihu (saiko; bupleurum; shoots removed, washed)　zhishi (kijitsu; unripe bitter orange; scorched)　muxiang (mokko; costus root)　helile pi (chebule fruit rind; 5 liang each)　taoren (tonin; peach kernel; skin and tips removed, scorched)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched; 6 liang each)　dahuang (daio; rhubarb; wrapped in dough and roasted in embers; 15 liang)

Powder the above and form pills the size of wutong seeds with refined honey. Take twenty pills each time with rice water, after meals and at bedtime, gradually increasing to forty or fifty until there is a free bowel movement. When the illness is felt to recede, take Wubu wan. Regardless of sex or age all may take it, adjusting for deficiency or excess.

## Notes

Qixuan wan: for bound wind qi and lodged food, sand, stones, skin or hair in the abdomen, long-standing cold pain of the back and legs, leg qi surging into the heart, dizziness and collapse, shoulder and back pain, abdominal distension, chest blockage, wind-toxin swelling of the head and face, constipation and rough urination, spleen-stomach stuffiness, cramps from leg qi, and abstraction with insomnia.
- Mainly rhubarb, purging with seven ingredients (bitter orange, costus, chebule, peach kernel, bupleurum, licorice). After the illness recedes, Wubu wan (perhaps in juan 5; not verified) is used to supplement.
