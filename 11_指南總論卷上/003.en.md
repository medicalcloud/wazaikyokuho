# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷上 003 論服餌法 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the method of taking medicines.

Medicines have sovereign, minister, assistant and envoy; people are strong or weak, deficient or excess. The method of taking them varies in weight; young and old follow different paths, the strong and the emaciated differ; some should be supplemented, some drained; some suit decoctions, some pills. If adjustments do not lose what is fitting, and medicine and illness match, cure is certain. If the illness is above the chest and diaphragm, eat first and then take the medicine. If the illness is below the heart and abdomen, take the medicine first and then eat. If the illness is in the limbs and blood vessels, [take it] on an empty stomach in the morning. If the illness is in the bones and marrow, [take it] on a full stomach at night. In general, the force of the medicine and the qi of food should not meet: when the food qi is dispersed, take the medicine; when the medicine qi has dispersed, take food. Adjusting thus, the five zang organs are calm and harmonious. Not only are medicinal properties manifold, but the timing, early or late, must also be regulated; what is now called "eating first" and "eating after" means this.

Decoctions should be taken fairly hot, for then they are easily digested and go down. If cold, they are vomited and do not go down; if too hot, they injure the throat. Care must be taken. Decoctions must be clarified; if turbid, they cause oppression in the chest that does not resolve. Allow about as long as it takes to walk ten *li* before taking the next dose; if too soon, the previous decoction has not dispersed when the next arrives and collides with it, and vomiting will certainly follow. Ask the patient whether the medicine in the belly has dispersed before giving more.

Supplementing pills are all the size of wutong seeds, starting with twenty and gradually increasing per dose to a limit of forty; more than this harms the person. "Twice a day" means wishing to prolong the days and time without lapse, so that the medicine's force gradually accumulates and fumigates and steams the five zang organs; the longer the better; taking it all at once is not good, and merely swallowing famous medicines brings very little benefit.

In taking medicinal wine, keep the qi of the wine continuous without interruption; if interrupted, the medicine's force is lost. Drink as much as one's nature allows, until an effect is felt; do not drink to the point of drunken vomiting, which greatly harms a person.

In treating illness with toxic medicines, begin with the size of a millet grain; stop when the illness is gone; if it is not gone, double the dose; if still not gone, increase tenfold, until it is gone. Where one or two toxic drugs such as croton seed or kansui are used alone in a formula, one must not take the full prescription. As the classic says: with one toxic drug among one ingredient, take one pill the size of a fine hemp seed; one toxic among two ingredients, two pills the size of large hemp seeds; one toxic among three, three pills the size of *hu* beans; among four, four pills the size of adzuki beans; among five, five pills the size of soybeans; among six, six pills the size of wutong seeds. Pills are made by these numbers, but among toxins some are light and some heavy: how could wolfsbane and gelsemium be the same as aconite and genkwa? All such must be measured in use.

After taking decoctions, gruel, food, meat and vegetables must all be thoroughly cooked; thoroughly cooked food is easily digested and suits the medicine. If raw, it is hard to digest and also damages the medicine's force. It is also better to eat few vegetables, and to take little salt and vinegar. One must not exhaust the mind or exert strength, nor indulge in joy and anger. Thus in treating illness the force of medicine comes first, but as regards treatment by diet, good care of oneself accounts for more than half, surpassing medicine. Therefore the patient must strive to take care of himself; perfect self-cultivation can bring long life—how much more than merely curing illness!

## Notes

On the method of taking medicines: timing relative to meals by the site of illness (after meals for illness above the diaphragm, before meals below the heart and abdomen, on an empty stomach in the morning for the limbs and vessels, at night on a full stomach for bones and marrow); taking decoctions warm and clear at intervals of about ten *li*'s walk; supplementing pills from twenty up to forty; keeping up medicinal wine without drunkenness; starting toxic drugs small and increasing until the illness goes, with the rule of pill sizes by number of ingredients per toxin; and the importance of diet and self-care after medication.
- Largely based on older accounts such as the *Qianjin fang* and the preface of the *Shennong bencao jing*.
