# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 023 川楝散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chuanlian san (Toosendan Powder)

It treats qi pain of the bladder and small intestine with griping pain below the navel surging up into the heart and abdomen, a withered yellow complexion, dull pain under the feet, weary limbs, no desire for food, frequent urination at night and itching of the outer kidneys (scrotum).

chuanlianzi (toosendan fruit; steamed, skin and stones removed)　poguzhi (psoralea; stir-fried)　huixiang (fennel; stir-fried; 4 liang each)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried; 1 liang)　huluba (fenugreek; soaked in wine, stir-fried; 3 liang)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; 1½ liang)

Make the above into a fine powder. Take 2 *qian* each time on an empty stomach before meals, mixed in hot wine.

## Notes

Chuanlian san (Baoqing-era additions): for griping pain below the navel rising upward, sallow complexion, pain in the soles, lassitude, poor appetite, nocturnal frequency and scrotal itching from bladder and small-intestine qi pain (mounting).
- Toosendan and fennel stop mounting pain; psoralea, fenugreek, aconite and dried ginger warm the kidneys.
