# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷上 001 論処方法 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On the method of composing formulas.

In composing a formula to treat illness, one should first diagnose and know the source of the illness, examine its fullness or emptiness, and then supplement or drain. Distinguish the cold and heat of the locality, observe the strength or decline of the man or woman, understand deeply the fine points of the sweetness and pungency of herbs and minerals, sovereign and minister, cold and heat; [discern] whether the proper channel itself is ill or damaged by external evil, whether it is in yin or in yang, in the exterior or the interior. One must examine how the forms and signs differ and how deficiency and repletion are not alike, trace where the evil came from and know where the illness arose. When the exterior is replete, drain the exterior; when the interior is replete, drain the interior; when it is in yang, treat yang; when in yin, treat yin. Using medicines received by the five viscera, as suits the four seasons, adjusting them to the proper balance, without error in purging or sweating, there is no illness that will not heal. If one does not clearly understand what to add and subtract, decides by one's own inclinations, does not distinguish what is to be feared and avoided, uses mutually opposing and mutually averse drugs together, or drains the interior when the illness is in the exterior, disperses the exterior when it is in the interior, drains yang when it is in yin, drains yin when it is in yang, failing to understand and being blind to the causes, then not only does the illness not heal but the patient is harmed. This must be deeply guarded against. Therefore the physician must clear his mind and apply his intention, penetrate the obscure and reach the subtle, examine the depth of the illness, understand the strength or mildness of the medicines, and compose formulas on a sound basis that work together with the illness. The key to the marvel lies here.

In treating the various illnesses, one should first use decoctions to wash and clear the five viscera and six bowels, open and free the vessels, regulate and smooth yin and yang, break evil within, moisten what is withered, gladden the skin and augment strength; water can cleanse all things, and so decoctions are used. If an illness of the limbs is long-standing with wind-cold stirring, powders should be used next; powders can expel evil, and wind qi and damp impediment moving between exterior and interior without fixed abode should be calmed by powders. Next pills should be used; pills can expel wind-cold, break accumulations and gatherings, disperse hard conglomerations, improve eating and harmonize the nutritive and defensive qi. One who can combine and apply these may be called a superior practitioner. Hence it is said, "Medicine is intention." In general, medicines for nourishing life contain many sovereigns, medicines for nourishing the nature contain many ministers, and medicines for treating illness contain many envoys. Use them with discernment, and not one in a hundred will fail.

Treat cold with hot medicines and heat with cold medicines; undigested food with emetic and purgative medicines; ghost infixation and gu toxin with gu-toxin medicines; abscesses, swellings, sores and tumours with medicines for sores and tumours; wind-damp with wind-damp medicines—each according to what is fitting. Lord Lei says: "Medicines have three grades, illnesses have three stages. Medicines differ in sweetness and bitterness, lightness and heaviness; illnesses are new or old, and differ in cold and warmth. Heavy, hot, greasy, sour and salty medicines, minerals and foods treat wind illnesses but do not suit other illnesses. Light, cold, sweet, bitter and astringent herbal and mineral medicines and foods treat heat illnesses but do not suit other illnesses. Light, hot, pungent, bitter and bland medicines and foods treat cold illnesses but do not suit other illnesses. These general principles roughly show the source and course; the rest can be known by observing the signs of the illness. In practice, devise what is fitting; one should understand these essentials."

## Notes

The first discussion of the General Guide, upper volume: principles of prescribing—discerning the source of illness, deficiency and repletion, locality, season, sex and drug properties; not erring in supplementing, draining, sweating and purging according to exterior, interior, yin and yang; the uses of decoctions (washing and opening the viscera), powders (expelling wandering evil) and pills (breaking accumulations, regulating the nutritive and defensive qi); the proportions of sovereign, minister and envoy; and choosing drugs by cold and heat (quoting Lord Lei).
- The saying "medicine is intention" and the discussion of decoctions, powders and pills follow older accounts found in the *Qianjin fang* and elsewhere.
