# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 025 丁香丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Dingxiang wan (Chokogan; Clove Pill)

It treats undispersed accumulated stagnation with hard distension of the heart and abdomen, phlegm counterflow and retching, belching of sourness and acid swallowing, stabbing pain of the flanks and ribs and stuffiness and oppression of the chest and diaphragm; or stomach reflux with nausea, inability to get food down, qi rising to surge into the chest, and choking stuffiness; and food concretions, wine aggregations, blood conglomerations and qi lumps that flare up with stabbing pain, with complete lack of desire for food. Taken regularly it digests food and moves stagnant qi.

zhuya zaojiao (small soap pods; skin removed, roasted until scorched black, finely powdered)　good ink (burned and quenched in vinegar)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried)　dingxiang (clove)　muxiang (mokko; costus root; 1 liang each)　ganqi (kanshitsu; dried lacquer; crushed and stir-fried until no more smoke, finely powdered)　hei qianniu (black morning-glory seed; stir-fried and finely powdered)　chuan dahuang (daio; Sichuan rhubarb; finely powdered separately)　peng'ezhu (zedoary; blast-fried and crushed)　jing sanleng (sparganium; blast-fried and crushed)　naosha (sal ammoniac; ground separately)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; 2 liang each)　qingpi (green tangerine peel; white removed; 3 liang)　badou shuang (croton frost; first heat the sal ammoniac in vinegar, add the croton frost and bring to the boil two or three times, then add the rhubarb powder and simmer into a paste; 1½ *qian*)

Knead the above with the rhubarb, sal ammoniac and croton paste into pills the size of mung beans. Take one or two pills each time with tea or wine as preferred. To dissolve concretions, conglomerations and aggregation lumps, take seven pills with ginger decoction. In all cases take after meals and at bedtime.

## Notes

Dingxiang wan: for hard distension, vomiting, acid reflux, flank pain and chest stuffiness from accumulated stagnation, for stomach reflux, and for attacks of stabbing pain from food concretions, wine aggregations, blood conglomerations and qi lumps.
- Croton frost, rhubarb, morning-glory seed and soap pod purge; sparganium, zedoary, dried lacquer and sal ammoniac break up accumulations; clove, costus, cinnamon, dried ginger and aconite warm and move qi.
- The base text writes 丁香木香 without a space, but these are two ingredients.
