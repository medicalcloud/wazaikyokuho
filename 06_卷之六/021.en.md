# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 021 導赤丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Daochi wan (Dosekigan; Red-Guiding Pill)

It treats congealed stagnation of the heart and kidneys with heat in the bladder, urinary blockage, and wind and heat contending with dribbling strangury that does not flow; or excessive intake of supplementing medicines with obstructed, rough waterways, passing little but getting up often, cramping pain of the navel and abdomen attacking and pouring into the genital region; or congested heat of the heart and lungs with red face and palpitation, dry mouth and vexing thirst; as well as abscess swellings and effusions of the back with static, blocked blood vessels. Taking it expels pus, disperses swollen toxin internally, and frees and guides pathogenic heat out of the heart meridian. It treats all pent-up wind-heat and the five kinds of strangury.

chi shaoyao (shakuyaku; red peony root)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed)　huashi (kasseki; talc; 4 liang each)　sheng gan dihuang (kanjio; dried raw rehmannia; dried over a fire)　mutong (akebia; nodes removed; half a jin each)　dahuang (daio; rhubarb; stir-fried; 15 liang)　shanzhizi ren (gardenia kernel; stir-fried; 12 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with refined honey. Take twenty to thirty pills each time after meals, swallowed with warm water.

## Notes

Daochi wan (bureau additions): for urinary blockage and strangury from bladder heat; for rough urination and abdominal pain after excessive supplementing medicines; for red face, palpitation and thirst from heat in the heart and lungs; and for abscesses and effusions of the back.
- The raw rehmannia and akebia of the earlier Daochi san (Dosekisan) (juan 6, no. 016) plus rhubarb, gardenia, red peony, poria and talc, made into pills.
