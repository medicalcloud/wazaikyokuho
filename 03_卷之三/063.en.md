# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 063 鉄刷湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Tieshua tang (Iron Brush Decoction)

It treats men's spleen accumulation and heart-qi pain and women's stabbing blood-qi pain; and also nausea from drunkenness, all malaria, dysentery and qi disorders, rectal bleeding from intestinal wind and visceral toxin, and slippery diarrhea.

liangjiang (galangal; stir-fried in oil; 6 liang)　huixiang (fennel; stir-fried; 2 liang)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; 8½ liang)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked overnight in rice-washing water; 8 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with three slices of ginger, a pinch of salt and one *zhan* of water, decoct down to seven-tenths and take it warm; it may also be mixed in hot wine. For spleen-cold [malaria], decoct it in one *zhan* of wine and give three doses in succession as the attack approaches. It also treats people from elsewhere who do not adapt to the local water and soil, and children's prolapse of the rectum from visceral cold; in both cases decoct it with three slices of ginger and one jujube. For summer heat with latent heat, grate fresh ginger and take it mixed in cold water. When rising early to travel, wrap a little of the medicine in a stoned jujube and chew it with three slices of ginger. It can ward off unseasonable epidemics, sha and miasmic diseases in all seasons.

## Notes

Tieshua tang (Baoqing-era additions): for men's spleen accumulation and heart-qi pain, women's blood-qi pain, hangover, malaria, dysentery, qi disorders, rectal bleeding, diarrhea, failure to adapt to local water and soil, children's rectal prolapse, summer heat, and as a preventive for early-morning travelers.
- Galangal, fennel, black atractylodes and licorice warm and remove damp.
- The base text reads 陽風 for 腸風 (intestinal wind); since the Siku quanshu and Executive Yuan editions both read 腸風, it has been emended (see the emendation).
