# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷下 012 論宿患心腹痛 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On long-standing heart and abdominal pain. For accumulated lumps, qi lumps, conglomerations and aggregations of long duration, with attacks and remissions at irregular times, do not seize and purge them [all at once]; they should be dissolved and ground away gradually. Have the patient take a few pills at a time of Ganying wan, Wenbai wan, Aiji wan, Pengjian wan or Xiao lizhong wan, taken regularly to grind them away gradually, and further give Hepi san, Jiahe san, Shen ling baizhu san, Si junzi tang, Sishi wan, Jianpi tang, Jianzhong san, Pingwei san and the like to assist the spleen and stomach; in time the root can be removed. If the pain has intervals of rest, or moves up and down, with confused oppression in the chest, occasional vomiting of cold froth, discomfort of the central stomach duct, vomiting counterflow and nausea, it may be worm pain; give Jixiao wan or Jiutong wan. Jiutong wan is purgative in nature and must not be taken much. If there is sudden violent pain of the heart and abdomen, distended and taut so that one cannot breathe, attacking back and forth, with fainting and nausea, it may be infixation, hostility or ghost qi; give Suhexiang wan. For a weak person with cold viscera, pinch Suhexiang wan into a cake, cover it with a flat-iron, set the cake on the iron and roast it until extremely hot to remove the nature of the borneol, then take it according to method. If the pain does not cease, the chest and diaphragm are blocked and oppressed, vomiting does not settle and neither gruel nor medicine goes down, give Qing muxiang wan swallowed with Shunqi san; if there is no Shunqi san, Wuji san will do; also Jiutong wan, Sanleng san and Da chenxiang wan.

## Notes

On long-standing heart and abdominal pain: accumulated lumps and conglomerations should not be purged at once but ground away gradually with small regular doses of Ganying wan, Wenbai wan, Aiji wan, Pengjian wan or Xiao lizhong wan, with Hepi san, Jiahe san and others to support the spleen and stomach; worm pain (intermittent, moving, cold froth) with Jixiao wan or Jiutong wan (purgative, not to be overused); sudden violent pain from infixation or ghost qi with Suhexiang wan (for the cold and weak, roasted on a flat-iron to drive off the borneol); unremitting pain with vomiting with Qing muxiang wan in Shunqi san (or Wuji san), Jiutong wan, Sanleng san and Da chenxiang wan.
