# 太平惠民和劑局方 指南總論 卷中 024 論傷寒重証 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

On severe patterns of cold damage. When the patient, from the fourth or fifth to the sixth or seventh day, has no aversion to wind and cold, fever with vexing thirst and vomiting counterflow, slight sweating of the palms and armpits, constipation and red urine, and after three or four days abdominal fullness, slight panting and manic, delirious speech, one must on no account give hot medicines recklessly; simply give one packet of Xiao chaihu tang (Minor Bupleurum Decoction), first taking one dose. If the bowels have not moved after the time it takes a person to walk ten-odd *li*, wait about half a day and give another dose; when the bowels move and dry stools come out, the patient recovers. This is an interior pattern. For vexing thirst, give Wuling san. If the hands and feet are cold with vomiting and diarrhoea, Xiao chaihu tang must not be given; give only Shen ling baizhu san (Ginseng, Poria and White Atractylodes Powder), Si junzi tang and the like. If after regulating and moving the bowels one fears that the patient is weak or elderly, balanced supplementing medicines should be used: give Jiahe san, Si junzi tang or Shen ling baizhu san.

## Notes

On severe patterns of cold damage: on days four to seven, without aversion to cold but with fever, vexing thirst, vomiting, slight sweating of the palms and armpits, constipation, dark urine, abdominal fullness, panting and delirium (an interior pattern), hot medicines are forbidden; Xiao chaihu tang is given to move the bowels; Wuling san for thirst; Shen ling baizhu san or Si junzi tang if the extremities are cold with vomiting and diarrhoea; afterward gentle supplementation (Jiahe san, etc.) for the weak or elderly.
