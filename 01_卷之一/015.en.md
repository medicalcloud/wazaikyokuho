# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 015 和太師牛黄丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

He taishi niuhuang wan (Grand Preceptor He's Bezoar Pill)

It treats sudden wind stroke with dizziness and falling, a clouded and obstructed spirit, loss of consciousness, clenched jaws, a fixed staring gaze, and phlegm and drool congesting the chest, diaphragm and throat; as well as paroxysmal attacks of the various epilepsies, with convulsions of the hands and feet, the mouth and eyes pulling against each other, and rigidity of the neck and back.

shiyan (fossil brachiopod)　shehuang (limonite nodule)　cishi (magnetite; these three burned together and quenched in vinegar nine times, finely ground)　xionghuang (yuo; realgar; ground and water-ground)　chensha (cinnabar; ground and water-ground)　shilü (malachite; ground and water-ground; 1 liang each)　niuhuang (goo; bezoar)　fenshuang (mercurous chloride sublimate; ground)　qingfen (calomel; finely ground)　shexiang (jako; musk; finely ground; half a liang each)　silver leaf (ground; 100 leaves)　gold leaf (100 leaves, for coating)

Grind all the above evenly and finely, knead with flour paste boiled in wine and form pills the size of a euryale seed. Take one pill each time, ground in decocted mint wine. The elderly may take half a pill. Children under ten divide [a pill] into four doses, ground in honey water; under four, into five doses; under one year, into seven doses. If the jaws are clenched, pry them open with something and pour it in.

## Notes

He taishi niuhuang wan: for sudden wind stroke with collapse, unconsciousness and clenched jaws, and for epileptic attacks.
- Fossil brachiopod, limonite and magnetite, burned and quenched in vinegar, are combined with realgar, cinnabar, malachite, mercury sublimate, calomel, bezoar and musk; children take divided doses by age.
- The name seems to refer to a Grand Preceptor named He, but this has not been verified.
