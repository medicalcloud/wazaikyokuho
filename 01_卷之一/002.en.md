# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 002 霊宝丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Lingbao dan (Numinous Treasure Elixir)

(It has three names: also called Guiming dan (Life-Restoring Elixir) or Fanhun dan (Soul-Returning Elixir); with mirabilite added it is called Poguan dan (Coffin-Breaking Elixir).) It treats wind stroke with numbness of the hands and feet and halting, sluggish speech; or pain reaching to the bone marrow; or numbness invading the skin, with itching like crawling insects or numbness as stubborn as iron or stone; or much phlegm and somnolence; or forgetfulness and irritability, with blood and vessels not moving and flesh dry and emaciated; or long confinement to bed, needing help to rise, with sluggish speech and a puffy face, feeling simply unwell; or those who, entangled in illness, die suddenly. It treats all of these.

liuhuang (sulfur; broken into pieces the size of soap-pod seeds, put in a silk bag, boiled in ash-free wine for three days and nights, taken out and ground to powder; 1 liang)　zirantong (pyrite; broken up and ground to fine powder; 1 liang)　xionghuang (yuo; realgar; broken into pieces the size of soap-pod seeds, put in a silk bag, boiled in rice vinegar for three days and nights, taken out and ground to powder; 1 liang)　guangmingsha (bright cinnabar; broken into pieces the size of soap-pod seeds, put in a silk bag, boiled in buckwheat-ash lye for three days and nights, taken out and ground to powder; 1½ liang)

For the above four ingredients, use a lidded porcelain jar. First lay three gold leaves on the bottom of the jar, put in the sulfur and cover it with two gold leaves. Next put in the realgar and cover it with two gold leaves; next the cinnabar, covered with two gold leaves; next the pyrite, covered with three gold leaves. Put the lid on the jar without sealing it with clay, set the jar firmly in an ash pit and nourish it with fire for three days and three nights. On the first day surround the jar at a distance of 3 *cun* with half a jin of glowing charcoal; on the second day use 10 liang of glowing fire 2½ *cun* from the jar; on the third day use 1 jin of fire 2 *cun* from the jar, until the fire burns out. When cool, take the medicine out of the jar, wrap it in three layers of paper, bury it in clean moist earth until the next morning, take it out and grind it finer.

cishi (magnetite; burned and quenched in vinegar twenty times, pounded, sieved and ground to powder)　zishiying (murasakisekiei; fluorite; ground to powder)　yangqishi (yokiseki; actinolite; ground to powder)　changlishi (fibrous gypsum; ground to powder)

The above four ingredients, 3 *fen* each. Use a lidded porcelain jar; put in first the magnetite, then the actinolite, then the gypsum, then the fluorite. Put in gold leaf layer by layer exactly as before, close the mouth with the lid without sealing it, and nourish with fire for three days and three nights: on the first day 1 jin of glowing charcoal 3 *cun* from the jar; on the second day half a *cheng* of fire 2½ *cun* from the jar; on the third day half a *cheng* of fire 2 *cun* from the jar. From day to night let the fire die out by itself. When cool, take the medicine out, wrap it in paper, bury it in moist earth until the next morning, take it out and grind it extremely fine.

tiger shin bone (smeared with wine and roasted yellow)　seal genitals (brushed with wine and lightly roasted)　longchi (dragon teeth)　longnao (borneol)　shexiang (jako; musk)　niuhuang (goo; bezoar)

The above six ingredients, 1 liang each, pounded and sieved to powder and then ground finely to a powder.

zhongru (stalactite; 10 liang, in a silk bag. First boil it in long-flowing water for half a day and discard the water; then use another 5 *dou* [of water], decoct down to 1 *dou*, and use it to boil the herbal medicines. Keep 3 *ge* of the stalactite water and grind 3 *fen* of raw rhinoceros horn in it)　tianma (gastrodia; sprouts removed)　yuanzhi (onji; polygala; core removed)　xianlingpi (epimedium)　baji (morinda)　wushe (black-striped snake; soaked in wine, lightly roasted, skin and bones removed, flesh used)　kushen (kujin; sophora root; 1 liang 1 *fen* each)

The above seven ingredients are pounded into a coarse powder, decocted in the 1 *dou* of stalactite water down to 7 *sheng*, strained through raw silk to remove the dregs, and allowed to settle.

rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　lurong (rokujo; deer antler velvet; hair removed, roasted with ghee until slightly yellow)　muxiang (mokko; costus root)　roudoukou (nutmeg; 1½ liang each)　yanhusuo (corydalis)　hutonglü (populus resin; 3 *fen* each)

The above six ingredients are pounded and coarsely sieved, decocted in the 7 *sheng* of stalactite liquid down to 4 *sheng*, strained through raw silk and allowed to settle.

banxia (hange; pinellia; washed seven times in hot water to remove the slipperiness)　danggui (toki) (Chinese angelica; sprouts removed; 1 liang each)

The above two ingredients are pounded and coarsely sieved, decocted in the 4 *sheng* of stalactite liquid down to 3 *sheng*, strained through raw silk and allowed to settle.

fresh rehmannia juice　boy's urine　ash-free wine (1 *sheng* each)　zaojia (sokyo) seeds (soap-pod seeds; pounded and sieved to powder; 1½ liang)

Combine the rehmannia juice etc. with the previous medicinal liquids, 6 *sheng* in all, put them into a silver pot and, in a quiet room, nourish over a gentle "civil and military" fire down to 1 *sheng*. Add the powdered metal and mineral medicines, stir with a willow spatula without stopping, and when the consistency is right remove from the fire. Then add the six ingredients such as bezoar and stir until perfectly even; immediately add the soap-pod seed powder and the water in which the rhinoceros horn was ground, strained through floss, into the medicine. Then have a strong man grind it with a pestle in a mortar three to five thousand times. When finished, divide it into three parts; to one part add 1 liang of mirabilite and grind evenly again (this is called Poguan dan). Make pills the size of mung beans. To treat any wind disease or injured limbs from a blow, regardless of severity or duration, first give 1–2 *qian* of Hongxue tongzhong san (Red Snow Powder for Freeing the Center) mixed in clear tea (the formula is in juan 6). After a while give hot tea to bring on one or two bowel movements, then decoct ginger and black soybean decoction according to the method and take three pills with it. Have another person rub the affected place with warm hands; soon the heat will penetrate, and one should feel something like fire inside the flesh reaching the site of the illness. Those who for one or two hundred days or up to a year have been unable to leave their beds from wind disease can walk after ten doses. For very severe cases, after each purging wait a day and take five pills, then rest three to five days and purge again; within thirty pills they recover as before. For injuries from blows of many years that ache and cannot move whenever the weather turns cloudy, five to seven doses are enough. For wind disease or injured limbs unable to move for ten or five years, simply take it according to the method; ten pills are effective, and even severe cases need no more than thirty. If someone suddenly dies from malign-qi strike, as long as the region of the heart is not yet cold, take five pills, mix them with vinegar and rub the navel more than a thousand times; warmth should gradually spread from all sides of the navel. When the eyes open, grind ten pills in hot vinegar and give them; once in the mouth the person revives. For any wind stroke with clenched jaws, or sudden death from sudden syncope (shijue), grind two or three pills in hot vinegar and pour them into the mouth; once they pass the throat the person revives. For regular use, take two pills with warm wine on an empty stomach; after about ten pills one will never have wind disease for the rest of one's natural life. This medicine is divinely effective; its power cannot be measured by human wisdom.

## Notes

Lingbao dan (also Guiming dan, Fanhun dan; with mirabilite, Poguan dan): said to treat everything from paralysis and speech disorders after wind stroke to revival from sudden death (malign-qi strike, sudden syncope).
- Its preparation is strongly alchemical: minerals such as sulfur, pyrite, realgar and cinnabar, and magnetite, fluorite etc., are sealed with gold leaf in jars and "nourished" with fire for three days and nights; the herbs are decocted in stages in stalactite water.
- In use, the patient is first purged with Hongxue tongzhong san.
- The Siku quanshu edition assigns the liquids for boiling the four minerals (ash-free wine, rice vinegar, buckwheat lye) differently from the base text (see the variants).
- It contains many highly toxic minerals and is not used today.
