# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 027 思仙続断丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Sixian xuduan wan (Eucommia and Dipsacus Pill)

It treats wind deficiency of the spleen and kidneys with toxic qi flowing and pouring, aching pain of the thighs and knees, difficulty walking, dribbling urination and heaviness after defecation. This medicine supplements internal damage of the five viscera, regulates the middle, augments essence and cools the blood, hardens and strengthens the sinews and bones, augments wisdom, lightens the body and enables one to endure old age.

mugua (chaenomeles; pulp removed; 3 liang)　xuduan (dipsacus)　bixie (dioscorea hypoglauca; 6 liang each)　niuxi (achyranthes; washed, rhizome head removed, soaked overnight in wine, dried over a fire)　yiyiren (coix seed; stir-fried; 4 liang each)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; rhizome head and forks removed)　duzhong (eucommia; bark removed, stir-fried with ginger until the threads break; 2 liang each)

Powder the above and form pills with vinegar paste. Take thirty to fifty pills each time on an empty stomach before meals, with warm wine or salt water as preferred.

## Notes

Sixian xuduan wan (Baoqing-era additions): for aching thighs and knees, difficulty walking, dribbling urine and heaviness after defecation from wind deficiency of the spleen and kidneys with flowing toxic qi.
- Dipsacus, eucommia and achyranthes strengthen the lower back and legs; chaenomeles, coix and dioscorea remove damp; aconite and saposhnikovia dispel wind. "Sixian" probably refers to eucommia, whose other name is sixianmu.
