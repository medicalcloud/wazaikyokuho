# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 040 烏荊丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wujing wan (Aconite and Schizonepeta Pill)

It treats all kinds of wind with slackness [of the limbs], loss of control, deviation of the mouth and eyes, halting and sluggish speech, twitching of the eyebrows and eyes, a clouded and oppressed head, cramping of the sinews and vessels with inability to bend and stretch, numbness of the whole body, pain in all the joints, and itching skin that turns into sores when scratched. It also treats women's blood wind with pain and itching all over the body, headache and dizziness, and is especially effective for intestinal wind and visceral toxin with unceasing bleeding from the bowel. Taken for a long time, it makes the complexion pleasant, the strength vigorous and the body light and healthy, and keeps the beard and hair from turning white.

chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; 1 liang)　jingjie spikes (schizonepeta; 2 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with a vinegar and flour paste. Take twenty pills each time with wine or hot water. When ill, take three or four doses a day on an empty stomach; when well, one dose in the early morning. (There was a Mr Guo, Assistant Director in the Directorate for Imperial Manufactories, who when young suffered from wind cramps and convulsions: his head and jaw hung slack and would not close, and he could eat only by supporting his jaw with his hand. After six or seven doses of this he recovered at once. He then took it constantly for more than fifty years; at over seventy he was strong and healthy, with not a white hair in his beard or hair. This medicine is especially good for bleeding from intestinal wind, and many have found it effective. Of those I have seen with my own eyes who recovered from rectal bleeding by taking it, there have been several within a single year.)

## Notes

Wujing wan (the following formulas belong to the layer "formulas added in the Shaoxing era"): for paralysis, facial deviation, numbness and itching from wind, women's blood wind, and rectal bleeding from intestinal wind and visceral toxin.
- A simple two-ingredient formula of Sichuan aconite and schizonepeta spikes. The bracketed passage at the end is an original note recording Mr Guo's case and the compiler's own observations.
