# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 074 独活寄生湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Duhuo jisheng tang (Dokukatsukiseito; Pubescent Angelica and Mistletoe Decoction)

It treats weak, deficient kidney qi with pain of the lower back and spine. This illness is contracted by lying on cold, damp ground exposed to wind; if not treated promptly it flows into the legs and knees and becomes hemilateral withering and cold impediment, slack, weak, painful and heavy. For lumbar pain with heavy legs, hypertonicity and impediment, this should be taken urgently.

duhuo (dokukatsu; pubescent angelica; 3 liang)　sangjisheng (mulberry mistletoe; the *Gujin lu yan* uses xuduan (zokudan), which is another name for the mistletoe and not true dipsacus)　danggui (toki) (Chinese angelica; soaked in wine, dried over a fire)　bai shaoyao (shakuyaku; white peony root)　shu dihuang (prepared rehmannia; soaked in wine and steamed)　niuxi (goshitsu; achyranthes; rhizome head removed, soaked in wine)　xixin (saishin; asarum; shoots removed)　bai fuling (bukuryo; white poria; skin removed)　fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia; rhizome head removed)　qinjiao (large-leaf gentian; earth removed)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng)　guixin (cinnamon heart; not exposed to fire)　xiongqiong (Sichuan lovage)　duzhong (tochu; eucommia; processed and stir-fried until the threads break)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; 2 liang each)

Cut the above into a powder. For each dose take four large *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, decoct down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it on an empty stomach. For qi deficiency with diarrhea, omit the rehmannia. It also treats abdominal pain just after childbirth with inability to turn or move, and hypertonic pain, impediment and weakness of the lower back and legs with inability to bend and stretch. This decoction is best at removing wind and dispersing blood. The *Zhouhou fang* version has one piece of aconite and lacks mistletoe, ginseng, licorice and angelica. People nowadays use it for joint wind wind and the flowing pour of leg qi with great effect.

## Notes

Duhuo jisheng tang (Baoqing-era additions): for lumbar pain from kidney deficiency contracted by lying on cold, damp ground in the wind, and for hemilateral withering, cold impediment, pain, heaviness and hypertonicity when it flows into the legs and knees; also for postpartum abdominal pain, cramping pain of the back and legs, joint wind wind and leg qi. Still widely used today for chronic lumbar, knee and joint pain (impediment patterns with liver-kidney insufficiency).
- Pubescent angelica, mistletoe, gentian, saposhnikovia and asarum dispel wind-damp; the Siwu tang (Shimotsuto) group (angelica, peony, rehmannia, lovage) with ginseng, poria and licorice supplement qi and blood; achyranthes, eucommia and cinnamon heart warm and supplement the kidneys. Originally a formula of the *Qianjin fang*.
- The note on mistletoe says that the "xuduan" of the *Gujin lu yan* is another name for mistletoe.
