# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之八 001 耆婆万病丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Qipo wanbing wan (Jīvaka's Myriad Illnesses Pill)

It treats the seven kinds of aggregation lumps, five kinds of epilepsy, ten kinds of infixation and hostility, seven kinds of flying corpse, twelve kinds of gu toxin, five kinds of jaundice, twelve kinds of malaria, ten kinds of water disease, eight kinds of great wind, and twelve kinds of wilting impediment; wind entering the head with dim, hazy vision; qi ascent and cough with a sound in the throat like a water rail, unable to lie down; food that does not turn into flesh; stagnant qi of the five viscera, undispersed accumulations and gatherings, blockage without passage, distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen extending to the chest and back, drum distension with hard bound qi flowing into the limbs, or the abdomen pressing on the heart and diaphragm with qi fullness, now quiet and now flaring, unhealed for ten or twenty years. The five kinds of diarrhoea; gan worms, roundworms, tapeworms and the various worms. Cold and heat above and below, long-accumulated phlegm-rheum making one sleep much, wasting without strength, seeping darkly into the bone marrow and becoming a stagnant illness; qi swelling of the body; vomiting counterflow on eating; aching lower back and legs, heavy limbs and inability to walk or stand for long. Women in whom, through childbirth, cold has entered the womb, the womb is unclean, or blocked without passage, static blood in the uterus is cold and stagnant and not fully discharged, causing intermittent pain, or who as a result have ceased to bear children; children's red and white dysentery; and body odour, deafness, nasal congestion and similar illnesses. In taking this medicine, three pills make one prescription; within three prescriptions the myriad illnesses are all removed. Its virtues are inexhaustible, hence the name "myriad illnesses pill". For malaria, take one pill before the attack; if not healed, take again as before.

shaoyao (peony root)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　xiongqiong (Sichuan lovage; not exposed to fire)　chuanjiao (Sichuan pepper; seeds and closed fruits removed, lightly stir-fried to remove the oil)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; rhizome head removed)　badou (croton seed; germ and membrane removed, stir-fried)　danggui (Chinese angelica; rhizome head removed)　sheng xijiao (raw rhinoceros horn; shaved)　jiegeng (platycodon)　yuanhua (genkwa; stir-fried red with vinegar)　fuling (poria; skin removed)　sangbaipi (mulberry root bark; stir-fried)　renshen (ginseng; rhizome head removed)　huangqin (scutellaria)　huanglian (coptis; fibrous roots removed)　yuyuliang (limonite; quenched in vinegar, ground and water-ground)　puhuang (typha pollen; lightly stir-fried)　qianhu (peucedanum; rhizome head removed)　daji (euphorbia root; chopped, stir-fried)　tingli (lepidium seed; stir-fried)　shexiang (musk; ground)　xixin (asarum; shoots removed)　xionghuang (realgar; ground and water-ground)　zhusha (cinnabar; ground and water-ground)　ziwan (aster; rhizome head removed)　gansui (kansui)　niuhuang (bovine bezoar; ground; 1 liang each)　wugong (centipede; twelve segments, head and legs removed, roasted)　yuanqing (blister beetle; twenty-eight, stir-fried with glutinous rice until the rice turns yellow-black, head, legs and wings removed)　shi xiyi (lizard; head, tail and legs removed, roasted; 4 *cun*)

Make the above into a fine powder, mix in the ground medicines evenly and form pills the size of adzuki beans with refined honey. If a child under one year old is ill, have the wet nurse take two pills the size of adzuki beans, again aiming for vomiting or purging. For recent and sudden illnesses take more; for long-accumulated illnesses take less; for regular use, aim for slightly loose stools. For a sudden illness near death, take one or two pills; with vomiting or purging it heals. For sudden malign strike with clenched jaw, take two pills with 1 *ge* of sour rice water; with purging it heals. For the five infixations, ghost stabbing and visiting hostility, take two pills. For men and women with evil illness, singing and weeping, the abdomen swollen as if pregnant, take two pills three times a day and once at night, between meals. For gu toxin with vomiting of blood and stabbing abdominal pain, take two pills; if not healed, take again. For malaria, take one pill before the attack; if not healed, take again. For all with phlegm-rheum, take three pills. For cold aggregations, take three pills three times a day, all between meals, constantly keeping the stools slightly loose. For lodged food not dispersing, take two pills to purge. For conglomerations, accumulations and gatherings, take two pills three times a day. For hypertonicity, distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen, and heart pain, take three pills. For qi ascent, vomiting counterflow and chest fullness with inability to lie down, take two pills; if not healed, take again. For severe dysentery, take two pills three times a day. For gan-damp, take two pills, and mix one pill the size of an apricot kernel with 2 *ge* of vinegar and pour it into the lower orifice (anus). For water disease, take three pills twice a day between meals, stopping when healed; if the person is weak, take every other day. For headache with aversion to cold, take two pills and induce sweating again. For cold damage and epidemic illness, take two pills three times a day between meals. For urinary blockage, take two pills; if not healed, take again the next day. For constipation, take three pills and also insert one pill into the lower orifice, and the bowels move at once. For deafness and purulent ear, wrap it in silk floss to the size of a jujube stone and plug the ear. For nosebleed, take two pills. For abscess swellings, clove sores and burst swellings, insert one pill the size of a hemp seed and apply once a day; the root comes out by itself. For bleeding from an injured clove sore, mix with lard and apply; if there is a hole, put it into the hole, and it heals. For leprous sores, wash with sour rice water, then apply the medicine mixed with lard. For leaking sores with holes, put one pill into the hole and apply it mixed with lard on top. For piles, smear it on a floss-wrapped stick and insert it into the hole, changing daily, until healed. For scrofula, mix with vinegar and apply; it heals. For tinea sores, rub with cloth until sweat comes, mix with vinegar and apply, changing once a day, until healed. For swellings of the chest, back, lower back and flanks, mix with vinegar and apply on the swelling, changing once a day, and also take two pills. For the various cold sores unhealed for years, mix with vinegar and apply. For malign thorn injuries, put one pill into the hole of the sore, and it heals at once. For viper bites, put a little into the bite; if the poison has entered the abdomen with vexation of the heart and breath about to expire, take three pills. For bee stings, apply a little and it heals. For women's illnesses where the placenta does not come down, take two pills. For children's fright epilepsy, take one pill the size of a grain of rice, smeared on the breast for the child to suck, adjusting for the child's size. For children's visiting hostility, mix one pill the size of a grain of rice with milk, smear it on the nipple and let the child suck; judge the amount by consideration. For scorpion stings, apply a little and it heals. For children with undigested milk and distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen, smear one pill the size of a grain of rice on the nipple and let the child suck; it heals at once.

## Notes

Qipo wanbing wan (the first formula of juan 8, "Treating miscellaneous illnesses"): said to treat the myriad illnesses—aggregation lumps, epilepsy, infixation, flying corpse, gu toxin, jaundice, malaria, water disease, great wind, wilting impediment, phlegm-rheum, accumulations, drum distension, diarrhoea, worms, postpartum stasis and infertility, children's dysentery, body odour, deafness and nasal congestion—with a long list of dosages and external uses for each condition.
- Thirty-one ingredients: drastic purgatives, water-expelling and highly toxic drugs (croton seed, kansui, euphorbia, genkwa, lepidium, blister beetle, centipede and others) together with bovine bezoar, musk, realgar, cinnabar, and warming and supplementing drugs. Vomiting or purging is the aim. From the *Qianjin fang* Qipo wanbing wan; Qipo is the Indian physician Jīvaka.
- Emendation: "sheng xijiao" (raw rhinoceros horn) reads "sheng xiejiao" in the base text; emended following the Siku quanshu edition.
