# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 014 五淋散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wulin san (Five Strangury Powder)

It treats all cases of insufficient kidney qi with heat in the bladder, blocked waterways, dribbling strangury that does not flow, passing little but getting up often, cramping pain of the navel and abdomen, accumulating with attacks at intervals and arising at once with fatigue, with urine like bean juice, or like sand and stones, or in cold strangury like grease, or in heat strangury with blood in the urine. (Another formula appears later.)

chi fuling (red poria; 6 liang)　danggui (Chinese angelica; rhizome head removed)　gancao (licorice; used raw; 5 liang each)　chi shaoyao (red peony root; rhizome head removed, chopped)　shanzhizi ren (gardenia kernel; 20 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, decoct down to eight-tenths and take it on an empty stomach before meals.

## Notes

Wulin san (from here the layer of Baoqing-era additions): for difficult, frequent and dribbling urination with cramping umbilical pain from insufficient kidney qi and bladder heat, and for the five strangury disorders (stone, greasy, blood strangury and others).
- Gardenia and red peony clear heat; red poria promotes urination; angelica and licorice are added. "Another formula appears later" probably refers to the other Wulin san later in juan 6.
