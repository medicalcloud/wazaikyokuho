# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 003 潤体丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Runti wan (Body-Moistening Pill)

It treats all kinds of wind with loss of control of the hands and feet, clouded and confused spirit and mind, halting and sluggish speech, deviation of the mouth and eyes, cramping of the sinews and vessels, vexing pain of the bones and joints, spinning head and dizziness, abstraction and restlessness, forgetfulness and palpitation, and congestion of phlegm and drool, as well as thickened, stubborn skin with numbness and loss of sensation.

fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia; rhizome head and forks removed, 1½ liang)　bai longnao (white borneol; ground separately)　ruxiang (frankincense; ground separately to the size of hemp seeds)　lingyangjiao (reiyokaku; antelope horn) powder (ground separately to powder)　fuzi (bushi; aconite lateral root; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　bai jiangcan (silkworm; lightly stir-fried)　binglang (areca)　roudoukou ren (nutmeg kernel)　chenxiang (aquilaria)　jilizi (shitsurishi; tribulus; lightly stir-fried)　dingxiang (clove)　manjingzi (vitex fruit; white skin removed)　niuhuang (goo; bezoar; ground separately to powder)　huoxiang leaves (agastache)　mahuang (mao; ephedra; nodes and roots removed)　raw rhinoceros horn powder (ground separately)　xionghuang (yuo; realgar; ground and water-ground)　shexiang (jako; musk; ground to powder)　muxiang (mokko; costus root)　chensha (cinnabar; ground and water-ground; 1 liang each)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed)　baifuzi (typhonium; blast-fried)　qianghuo (notopterygium; rhizome head removed)　yuancan'e (silkworm moth; lightly stir-fried)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; rhizome head removed)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　xiongqiong (ligusticum; 1½ liang each)　pearl powder (ground separately to powder)　duhuo (dokukatsu; angelica pubescens root; rhizome head removed; 3 *fen* each)　ganxie (dried scorpion; lightly stir-fried)　banxia (hange; pinellia; boiled thirty times in water, thinly sliced, dried over a fire and stir-fried with ginger juice)　chuan wutou (uzu) (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed, crushed and stir-fried yellow; 2 liang each)　baihuashe (long-nosed pit viper; soaked in wine and roasted, skin and bones removed, flesh taken)　tianma (gastrodia; sprouts removed; 3 liang each)　hupo (amber; ground separately to powder)　nifen (calomel; ground)　bai doukou ren (white cardamom; half a liang each)　gold leaf (60 leaves, for coating)

Make the above into a fine powder, add the separately ground medicines and mix evenly, knead with refined honey and form pills the size of a qianshi (euryale seed). Take one pill each time, chewed thoroughly, with warm wine; it may also be taken with schizonepeta tea. Increase to two pills. For tetanus from wounds with rigidity of the spine, convulsions of the hands, clenched jaws and epileptic fits, break three pills and dissolve them in hot soybean-steeped wine, pry the mouth open and pour it in; give again after a while. Sweating means recovery. For children's fright wind and epilepsies, dissolve half a pill each time in mint decoction. At any time.

## Notes

Runti wan: a large (38-ingredient) wind-dispelling, phlegm-transforming, orifice-opening formula for hemiplegia, speech disorders, cramping and dizziness after wind stroke.
- It combines orifice-opening substances (borneol, musk, bezoar, rhinoceros horn) with wind-dispelling animal drugs (pit viper, scorpion, silkworm) and warm drugs such as aconite.
- "The size of a jitou" refers to the euryale seed (qianshi).
- The Siku quanshu edition differs in the wording of processing and doses, calls the amber "southern foreign amber" and has additional entries for white poria and black aconite (see the variants).
