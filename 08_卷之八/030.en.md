# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之八 030 雲母膏 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yunmu gao (Mica Paste)

It treats all sores, swellings, wounds, fractures and similar illnesses.

shujiao (shokusho) (Sichuan pepper; seeds and closed fruits removed, lightly stir-fried to sweat out the oil)　baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica)　moyao (myrrh; ground)　chi shaoyao (shakuyaku; red peony root)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　danggui (toki) (Chinese angelica; half a liang each)　yanhua (salt flowers; ground; 14 liang)　qilinjie (dragon's blood; ground)　changpu (shobu; acorus)　baiji (bletilla)　xiongqiong (Sichuan lovage)　cao longdan (ryutan; gentian)　muxiang (mokko; costus root)　bailian (ampelopsis root)　fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia; rhizome head and forks removed)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, processed with ginger juice)　shexiang (jako; musk; ground)　jiegeng (kikyo; platycodon)　chaihu (saiko; bupleurum; rhizome head removed)　songzhi (matsuyani; pine resin)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked overnight in rice-washing water)　huangqin (ogon; scutellaria)　yehe (albizia; bark used)　ruxiang (frankincense)　fuzi (bushi; aconite; skin and navel removed)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed)　gao liangjiang (lesser galangal; half a liang each)　xiaoshi (saltpetre; ground to powder)　gancao (kanzo; licorice)　yunmu (unmo; mica; bright, white, thin pieces, ground to powder; 4 liang each)　sangbaipi (mulberry root bark)　shuiyin (suigin; mercury; when the paste has set and is as warm as the human body, put the mercury in a raw-silk bag and flick it with the fingers onto the paste in drops the size of a needle-head; this is called "nourishing the mother of the medicine")　baiye (hakuyo; arborvitae leaf; not from near a road)　huaiye (sophora leaf)　liuzhi (willow twigs; 2 liang each)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; 1 liang)　qingyou (clear oil; 40 liang)　huangdan (red lead; finely ground; 14 liang)　huangqi (ogi; astragalus; rhizome head removed; half a liang)

Except for the eight ingredients ground separately—mica, saltpetre, dragon's blood, myrrh, musk, frankincense, red lead and salt flowers—chop everything to the size of beans and soak the chopped medicines in the clear oil above in a porcelain vessel for seven days, sealed. Then decoct over a gentle fire, stirring ceaselessly, putting it on and taking it off the fire three times: each time it is put on, wait until it bubbles all over, then take it off; wait until the boiling subsides and put it on again—three times in this way—until the angelica dahurica, aconite and the like turn yellow, without letting them scorch black. Wring out the dregs through silk floss or new cloth, put it back into the pan and boil it down again over the fire. Then add the red lead and the eight separately ground medicines, stirring ceaselessly with a willow spatula until the paste sets; after a good while, when the color changes, boil down again over the fire, until a little dripped into water congeals and does not stick to the fingers. First heat a porcelain vessel; when it is hot pour the medicine into it, and when it has cooled to body warmth flick the mercury on top. Each time the paste is used, first scrape off the mercury. To treat an effusion of the back, first decoct 1 jin of old cattail in 3 sheng of water through fifty boils; when it is at body warmth, wash the sore with the cattail water, wipe dry and apply the medicine; [also] divide 1 liang into three doses taken with warm wine, and if pus has not yet formed it heals at once. Apply it externally. For breast abscess, apply externally. For scrofula and bone flat-abscess with toxin penetrating to the bone, divide 1 liang of the medicine into three doses taken with warm wine; in severe cases foul matter is purged out at once; also apply externally, and it heals. For intestinal abscess, divide half a liang of the medicine into five doses taken with licorice decoction; if pus has not yet formed it disperses at once; if there is already pus, the pus comes down with the medicine; afterwards take five pills the size of wutong seeds with wine every day, stopping when the pus stops. For wind eyes, apply to both temples (Taiyang points). For bites of the wall spider and other spiders, apply externally, leaving the opening of the sore uncovered. For effusions of the brain, of the beard and temples, of the eyebrows and ears, umbilical abscess, gum abscess and toothache, apply externally and wrap, and the pain stops at once. For arrow wounds with the arrowhead inside, apply externally and eat a little soft-boiled mung bean every day, and the arrowhead comes out by itself. For injuries by tigers and leopards, first wash with licorice decoction, then apply the paste, changing it daily; within three applications [it heals]. For dog and snake bites, take ten pills the size of wutong seeds with raw oil, and also apply externally. For difficult labor where delivery has not occurred after three days, take 1 *fen* with warm wine and the child is born at once. For blood dizziness (postpartum feebleness) near death, take ten pills with ginger juice and half a sheng of urine in warm wine, and even the seemingly dead return at once. For a dead fetus in the abdomen, take half a liang with elm-bark decoction and it is delivered at once. For a man's "original zang organ qi" (kidney mounting), take 1 *fen* with fennel in warm wine, one dose a day; within two doses it heals. For poisoning by toxic medicine, wash a sock in wine (one version reads "for summer heat toxin, take earth water"), warm it and take 1 *fen*, one dose a day; within four times foul matter is purged out and it heals. For tumors and warts, apply externally to disperse them. For all swellings and boils, apply externally and they heal at once. Whatever the suffering, it treats it, and as soon as the medicine arrives it heals. In all the above uses only sheep's blood is to be avoided; there is nothing else to avoid. If one keeps this medicine for self-protection, wrap it in waxed paper so that the wind does not dry it, and its strength will not diminish for thirty years.

## Notes

Yunmu gao (no layer marked): a plaster for all sores, swellings, wounds and fractures, with directions for external application or oral use (as pills) in effusions of the back, breast abscess, scrofula, bone abscess, intestinal abscess, wind eyes, insect bites, effusions of the brain, beard, eyebrows and ears, toothache, arrow wounds, tiger, leopard, dog and snake bites, difficult labor, postpartum feebleness, dead fetus, mounting, poisoning, tumors and more.
- Mainly mica, saltpetre, red lead and salt flowers, with frankincense, myrrh, dragon's blood, musk and nearly forty ingredients boiled into a paste in clear oil; mercury is flicked onto the surface for storage ("nourishing the mother of the medicine"). It contains mercury and red lead.
