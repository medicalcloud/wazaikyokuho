# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之八 051 琥珀膏 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Hupo gao (Amber Paste)

It treats scrofula of the neck and those arising in the armpits, at first like plums, swollen, bound, hard and stiff, gradually becoming like strung beads, neither dispersing nor bursting; or perforating with pus and ulceration, with unceasing discharge from the flesh, hard to heal over a long time and gradually becoming fistulas.

hupo (amber; 1 liang)　mutong (akebia)　guixin (cinnamon heart)　danggui (toki; Chinese angelica)　baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica)　fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia)　songzhi (matsuyani; pine resin)　zhusha (cinnabar; ground)　mubie (momordica seed; shells removed; half a liang each)　mayou (sesame oil; 2 jin)　dingxiang (clove)　muxiang (mokko; costus root; 3 *fen* each)

First pound and sift into a powder the five ingredients amber, clove, cinnamon heart, cinnabar and costus; chop the other medicines finely, soak them in the oil overnight, decoct in a pan over a gentle fire until the angelica dahurica is scorched yellow, and strain. Next add the powdered pine resin, strain off the dregs, let the oil settle until clear, put it back in the pan and boil down over a gentle fire, add 1 jin of red lead and stir ceaselessly with a willow spatula until black; when a drop in water forms a bead that does not spread and the hardness is right, add the amber and other powders, stir evenly and store in a porcelain vessel. Each time, according to the size, spread it evenly on paper over a fire and apply.

## Notes

Hupo gao (Wu Zhige's additions): a plaster for scrofula of the neck and armpits (hard plum-sized nodes forming chains, neither dispersing nor bursting, or bursting with unceasing discharge and becoming fistulas).
- A powder of amber, clove, cinnamon heart, cinnabar and costus added to a plaster of akebia, angelica, angelica dahurica, saposhnikovia, pine resin and momordica boiled in sesame oil with red lead (not in the ingredient list, but 1 jin is added in the preparation).
