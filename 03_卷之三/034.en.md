# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 034 如聖餅子 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Rusheng bingzi (Sage-Like Cakes)

It treats men and women with qi syncope, exuberance above and deficiency below, phlegm-fluid retention and wind-cold latent and lodged in the yang meridians, one-sided and general headache with pain reaching the vertex, vomiting and nausea, closed eyes and deafness. Taken regularly it clears the head and eyes, disperses wind, transforms phlegm and warms the stomach.

fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia)　tianma (gastrodia)　banxia (hange; pinellia; raw; half a liang each)　tiannanxing (arisaema; washed)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; skin and tips removed; 1 liang each)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; 2 liang each)

Make the above into a fine powder, knead with steamed bread soaked in hot water, form pills the size of a euryale seed, pinch them into small cakes and dry them in the sun. Take five cakes each time, chewed thoroughly together with three to five spikes of schizonepeta, with tea or wine as preferred; boiled water is also fine; at any time.

## Notes

Rusheng bingzi (from here the layer of Shaoxing-era additions; the formula referred to in juan 1, no. 043 and juan 2, no. 042): for one-sided and general headache, nausea and eye and ear symptoms in qi syncope with exuberance above and deficiency below, when phlegm-fluid retention and wind-cold lurk in the yang meridians.
- Arisaema, pinellia and aconite remove wind-phlegm and cold; lovage, saposhnikovia and gastrodia scatter wind from the head; dried ginger warms the stomach. Made into flat cakes that are chewed.
- The base text writes 川芎甘草 without a space, but these are two ingredients.
