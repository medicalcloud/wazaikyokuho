# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 029 消食丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Xiaoshi wan (Food-Dispersing Pill)

It treats deficiency of both spleen and stomach with inability to digest water and grain, glomus and oppression of the chest and diaphragm, and recurrent distension of the abdomen and flanks for years and months on end, with reduced eating, a liking for lying down, a bitter mouth without taste, and deficiency, emaciation and shortage of qi. It also treats cold in the chest with inability to get food down, stomach reflux with heartburn and vomiting of sudden turmoil; and is suitable after illness when one is newly deficient and cannot cope with grain qi, or when illness has weakened the qi so that eating has not returned to normal.

wumei (smoked plum; stones removed, dried over a fire)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried; 4 liang each)　xiaomainie (wheat sprouts; stir-fried yellow; 3 liang)　shenqu (medicated leaven; pounded to powder and stir-fried; 6 liang 2 *qian*)

Powder the above medicines and form pills the size of wutong seeds with refined honey. Take fifteen pills each time, increasing to twenty, with rice gruel, two doses a day, at any time.

## Notes

Xiaoshi wan: for indigestion, chest glomus, recurrent abdominal and flank distension, poor appetite, bitter mouth and emaciation from spleen-stomach deficiency, for stomach reflux and vomiting from cold in the chest, and for appetite that does not return after illness.
- Medicated leaven and sprouts digest food, dried ginger warms, and smoked plum regulates the stomach.
