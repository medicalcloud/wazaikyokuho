# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 083 木瓜丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Mugua wan (Mokkagan; Chaenomeles Pill)

It is suitable, whether long-standing or new, for weakness of the kidney meridian with [pathogen] descending to attack the lower back and knees, heaviness and little strength, swelling and itching of the thighs that break open into sores, dull pain in the soles and cramping of the sinews; or slack, weak lower back and knees, difficulty walking, rapid dyspnea on movement, a dark sallow complexion, constipation and difficult urination, and reduced eating.

shu gan dihuang (kanjio; prepared rehmannia; washed and dried over a fire)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; pulp removed)　wuyao (lindera; 4 liang each)　hei qianniu (black morning-glory seed; 3 liang, stir-fried)　shinanteng (photinia stem)　xingren (kyonin; apricot kernel; skin and tips removed)　danggui (toki) (Chinese angelica)　congrong (cistanche; soaked in wine and dried over a fire)　dried mugua (chaenomeles)　xuduan (zokudan; dipsacus)　niuxi (goshitsu; achyranthes; soaked in wine; 2 liang each)　chi shaoyao (shakuyaku; red peony root; 1 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with wine paste. Take thirty to fifty pills each time, swallowed on an empty stomach with chaenomeles decoction; warm wine is also fine.

## Notes

Mugua wan (bureau additions): a leg-qi formula for heaviness of the lower back and knees, swollen itchy thighs, sores, pain in the soles, difficulty walking and constipation from a weak kidney meridian.
- Chaenomeles, photinia, achyranthes and dipsacus remove damp and strengthen the legs; rehmannia, angelica and cistanche supplement; morning-glory seed, apricot kernel, tangerine peel and lindera move qi and free the bowels.
- The Siku quanshu edition gives 3 liang of red peony (see the variants).
