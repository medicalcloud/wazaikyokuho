# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 012 甘露飲 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Ganlu yin (Sweet Dew Beverage)

It may be taken by men, women and children with visiting heat in the stomach, receding gums and bad breath, swollen, festering gums with occasional discharge of pus and blood, heavy drooping eyelids that always want to close; or quick hunger with vexation but no desire for food; and red, swollen, painful eyes intolerant of cooling medicines, sores of the mouth and tongue, swollen, painful throat, and sores and rashes whether erupted or not yet erupted. It also treats all cases where the spleen and stomach have received damp and static heat is in the interior, or where drunkenness, overeating and sexual exhaustion lead damp and heat to contend and produce jaundice, with the body and face yellow, slight swelling of the limbs, chest fullness and shortness of breath, irregular stools, yellow, rough urine, or occasional fever.

pipaye (loquat leaf; hairs brushed off)　gan shu dihuang (prepared rehmannia; earth removed)　tianmendong (asparagus root; core removed, dried over a fire)　zhiqiao (bitter orange; pulp removed, stir-fried with bran)　shan yinchen (capillaris; stalks removed)　sheng gan dihuang (dried raw rehmannia)　maimendong (ophiopogon; core removed, dried over a fire)　shihu (dendrobium; rhizome head removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　huangqin (scutellaria)

Powder equal parts of the above. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, decoct down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm, after meals and at bedtime. For children divide one dose into two, adjusting further for age.

## Notes

Ganlu yin (Shaoxing-era additions): for gum disease (receding, swollen, bleeding or suppurating gums, bad breath), heavy eyelids, hunger without appetite, red swollen eyes, mouth sores, sore throat and eruptions from visiting heat in the stomach, and for jaundice from damp-heat of the spleen and stomach. Still used today for mouth ulcers and gingivitis from stomach yin deficiency with heat.
- Raw and prepared rehmannia, asparagus root, ophiopogon and dendrobium nourish yin; scutellaria, capillaris and loquat leaf clear heat; bitter orange and licorice are added.
