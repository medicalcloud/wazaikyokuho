# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 048 分気紫蘇飲 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Fenqi zisu yin (Qi-Separating Perilla Drink)

It treats disharmony of the spleen and stomach in men and women with choking obstruction of the chest and diaphragm, pain of the abdomen and flanks, hurried, urgent dyspnea, distension and oppression below the heart, no desire for food and unceasing vomiting counterflow.

wuweizi (gomishi; schisandra; stalks removed, washed)　sangbaipi (mulberry root bark; roasted, chopped)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed, washed clean)　jiegeng (kikyo; platycodon; chopped)　caoguo ren (tsaoko seed)　dafupi (areca husk)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; 3 jin each)

Cut these eight ingredients into a coarse powder, weigh 20 jin net, add 15 jin of dried perilla of selected tender twigs and leaves, pound it and mix all together evenly. For each dose take 4 *qian* with one large *zhan* of water, three coin-slices of ginger and a little salt, decoct together down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and [take it] on an empty stomach before meals. Taken regularly it harmonizes the stomach and improves eating.

## Notes

Fenqi zisu yin (Shaoxing-era additions): for chest obstruction, abdominal and flank pain, dyspnea, distension below the heart, poor appetite and vomiting from spleen-stomach disharmony.
- Much perilla, with mulberry bark, schisandra and platycodon to lower the lungs, areca husk, tangerine peel and tsaoko to move qi, and poria and licorice to regulate.
- The base text writes 大腹皮甘草 without a space, but these are two ingredients.
