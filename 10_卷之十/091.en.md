# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之十 091 蝦䗫丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Hama wan (Toad Pill)

It is suitable for the five gan and eight dysenteries in children with abdominal distension and yellow face, emaciated, haggard skin, intermittent chills and fever, no desire for milk or food, a liking for eating mud and earth, rubbing the nose and biting the nails, hair clumping like ears of grain, failure to grow flesh, many sores and tinea, irregular stools, urine like rice-washing water, regurgitation of milk and food, dysentery of varying colour, or, from drinking "crossed milk" (milk of a mother pregnant again), gradual yellowing and wasting turning into gan disease.

hama (toad)　shijunzi (quisqualis fruit; stir-fried)　zaojiao (gleditsia pod; burned; 2 liang each)　qingdai (indigo; 2½ liang)　longdan cao (gentian; "grass" removed; 4 liang)　xionghuang (realgar; ground and water-ground; 2 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder, mix in the ground medicines evenly, and form pills the size of millet grains with water paste. A one-year-old takes seven pills, a two-year-old ten, a three-year-old twenty, with milk or rice water, at any time.

## Notes

Hama wan (bureau additions): for the five gan and eight dysenteries in children (abdominal distension, sallowness, wasting, chills and fever, poor appetite, eating earth, nose-rubbing, nail-biting, clumped hair, sores and tinea, irregular stools, cloudy urine, regurgitation, variable diarrhoea), including gan from "crossed milk" (milk of a mother pregnant again).
- Toad, quisqualis, burned gleditsia and realgar kill worms; indigo and gentian clear gan heat.
- The note "grass removed" under gentian is as in the base text (perhaps an error for "rhizome head removed").
