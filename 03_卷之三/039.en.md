# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 039 沈香降気湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chenxiang jiangqi tang (Aquilaria Qi-Lowering Decoction)

It treats congestion and stagnation of yin and yang with qi failing to rise and descend, glomus and blockage of the chest and diaphragm, distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen, panting and shortness of breath, dry retching with vexing fullness, cough with phlegm and drool, no taste in the mouth, and a liking for lying down with reduced eating. It also treats lodged rheum of stomach impediment with belching smelling sour, propping binding below the flanks and a constant feeling of oppression; and coughing counterflow from cold in the middle, throughflux diarrhoea from spleen damp, empty rumbling of both flanks and griping pain below the navel. For leg qi patients with toxic qi rising, hard fullness of the heart and abdomen and swollen limbs, it is especially suitable. Taken regularly it opens the stomach, disperses phlegm, scatters congestion and gives an appetite.

xiangfu (cyperus; stir-fried, hairs removed; 400 liang)　chenxiang (aquilaria; 18½ liang)　suoshaner (amomum; 48 liang)　gancao (licorice; scorched; 120 liang)

Make the above into a fine powder. Take 1 *qian* each time with a little salt, stirred into boiling water. Against the fog and dew of early morning, take it on an empty stomach; it removes evil, malign qi and prevents miasmic epidemics.

## Notes

Chenxiang jiangqi tang (Shaoxing-era additions): for chest glomus, abdominal fullness, panting, cough and poor appetite from disordered rising and descending of qi, for lodged rheum, cough from middle cold, damp diarrhoea and pain below the navel, and for leg qi rising upward.
- A large amount of cyperus with aquilaria, amomum and licorice, stirred into hot water.
