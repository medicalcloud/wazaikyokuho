# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之七 017 蝉花無比散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chanhua wubi san (Incomparable Cicada Flower Powder)

It treats all of the following in adults and children, of many years' or recent standing: all wind eyes and qi eyes attacking and pouring in, with dim, dark eyes, wind-millet bumps on the lids, pain or itching, gradual formation of nebulae and membranes invading and covering the eye, and unclear vision; long-standing hemilateral or frontal head wind drawing into both eyes, which gradually become narrowed and small, with red festering extending around the orbits; and children's sores and rashes entering the eyes, with white membranes covering the eye, redness, roughness and dull pain. Taken regularly it dispels wind, clears nebulae and brightens the eyes.

shetui (snake slough; lightly roasted; 1 liang)　chantui (cicada slough; head, legs and wings removed; 2 liang)　qianghuo (notopterygium)　danggui (Chinese angelica; washed, dried over a fire)　shijueming (abalone shell; boiled with salt in east-flowing water for a full day and night, strained out, pounded and ground to powder)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage; 3 liang each)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; forks and branches removed)　fuling (poria; skin removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried; 4 liang each)　shaoyao (red peony root; 13 liang)　jili (tribulus; stir-fried, spines removed; half a jin)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked, skin removed, stir-fried; 12 liang)

Powder the above. Take 3 *qian* each time after meals mixed in rice-washing water; clear tea is also fine. Avoid eating wind-provoking and toxic things.

## Notes

Chanhua wubi san (from here the layer of bureau additions): for dim vision, bumps on the lids, pain, itching, nebulae and poor sight from all wind eyes and qi eyes, old or new; for hemilateral or frontal head wind drawing into the eyes; for festering around the orbits; and for white membranes, redness and pain when children's pox or rashes enter the eyes.
- Cicada slough, snake slough, abalone shell and tribulus clear nebulae; notopterygium, saposhnikovia and lovage disperse wind; angelica and red peony harmonize the blood; black atractylodes, poria and licorice are added.
