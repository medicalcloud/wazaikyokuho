# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 005 牛黄清心丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Niuhuang qingxin wan (Bezoar Heart-Clearing Pill)

It treats all kinds of wind with slackness and loss of control [of the limbs], halting and sluggish speech, palpitation of the heart and forgetfulness, abstraction that comes and goes, dizziness of the head and eyes as if covered, vexation and oppression in the chest, congestion of phlegm and drool, and a clouded, confused spirit. It also treats insufficiency of heart qi with an unsettled spirit and mind, fright and fear, grief, worry and sorrow, deficiency vexation with little sleep, and joy and anger at any time; or outbreaks of mania and withdrawal (kuang and dian) with a clouded, disordered spirit.

bai shaoyao (white peony root)　maimendong (ophiopogon; core removed)　huangqin (scutellaria)　danggui (Chinese angelica; sprouts removed)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; sprouts removed)　baizhu (white atractylodes; 1½ liang each)　chaihu (bupleurum)　jiegeng (platycodon)　xiongqiong (ligusticum)　bai fuling (white poria; skin removed)　xingren (apricot kernel; skin, tips and double kernels removed, stir-fried yellow with bran, ground separately; 1 liang 2½ *qian* each)　shenqu (medicated leaven; ground)　puhuang (typha pollen; stir-fried)　renshen (ginseng; rhizome head removed; 2½ liang each)　antelope horn powder　shexiang (musk; ground)　longnao (borneol; ground; 1 liang each)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　dadou huangjuan (soybean sprouts; crushed and stir-fried)　ejiao (donkey-hide gelatin; crushed and stir-fried; 1 liang 7½ *qian* each)　bailian (ampelopsis)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried; 7½ *qian* each)　niuhuang (bezoar; ground; 1 liang 2 *qian*)　rhinoceros horn powder (2 liang)　xionghuang (realgar; ground and water-ground; 8 *qian*)　dried shanyao (Chinese yam; 7 liang)　gancao (licorice; chopped and stir-fried; 5 liang)　gold leaf (1,200 leaves, of which 400 are used for coating)　dazao (jujubes; 100, steamed, skin and stones removed, ground to a paste)

Except for the jujubes, apricot kernels, gold leaf, the two horn powders, and the four items bezoar, musk, realgar and borneol, make [the rest] into a fine powder; add the remaining medicines and mix evenly, and form pills with refined honey and the jujube paste, ten pills to each liang, coated with gold leaf. Take one pill each time dissolved in warm water, after meals. For children's fright epilepsy, judge the amount and dissolve it in lukewarm bamboo-leaf decoction.

## Notes

Niuhuang qingxin wan: a heart-clearing, orifice-opening, phlegm-transforming formula for paralysis and speech disorders after wind-strike and for anxiety, insomnia, fright and manic agitation due to insufficient heart qi.
- Bezoar, rhinoceros horn, antelope horn, musk and borneol clear the heart and open the orifices; ginseng, atractylodes, Chinese yam, licorice and jujube supplement; angelica, peony and gelatin nourish the blood.
- This is the formula Yue Ke criticized in the *Tingshi* for its many ingredients mixing cold and hot (quoted in the Siku quanshu abstract).
- The Siku quanshu edition reads 緩瘲 for 緩縦 and 心忪 for 心怔 (see the variants).
