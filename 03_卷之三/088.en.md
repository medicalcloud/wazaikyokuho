# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 088 分心気飲 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Fenxinqi yin (Heart Qi-Separating Drink)

The patterns it treats are the same as for the earlier Fenxinqi yin.

mutong (akebia; nodes removed)　chi shaoyao (red peony root)　chi fuling (red poria)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　banxia (pinellia; washed seven times in hot water)　sangbaipi (mulberry root bark; lightly stir-fried)　dafupi (areca husk)　chenpi (aged tangerine peel; pulp removed)　qingpi (green tangerine peel; white removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　qianghuo (notopterygium; 1 liang each)　zisu (perilla; coarse stalks removed; 4 liang)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 3 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, three slices of ginger, two jujubes and five rush pith stems, decoct together down to seven-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm, at any time. Taken regularly it digests stagnant qi, makes yin and yang rise and descend, regulates and smooths the triple burner, harmonizes the spleen and improves eating.

## Notes

Fenxinqi yin (from here the layer of "newly added secret formulas from the bureaus"): the same indications as the earlier Fenxinqi yin (juan 3, no. 058), but a different formula based on perilla, with akebia, red peony, red poria, cinnamon, pinellia, mulberry bark, areca husk, the tangerine peels, notopterygium and licorice. The Fenxinqi yin generally used today is close to this one.
