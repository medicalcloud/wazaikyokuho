# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 002 小柴胡湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Xiao chaihu tang (Minor Bupleurum Decoction)

It treats cold damage and warm-heat diseases with fever and aversion to wind, stiffness of the neck and nape, fullness of the chest and pain in the flanks, retching, vexation and thirst, alternating chills and fever, yellowing of the whole body and face, difficult urination, and constipation with hard stools; or [disease] that has passed through the channels without resolving, or tidal fever that does not abate; also relapse from overexertion after recovery, with fever and pain; women damaged by wind, with headache and vexing heat; [women whose] menstruation has just stopped, with chills and fever like malaria occurring at set times; and wind damage after childbirth with headache and vexing heat. All of these should take it.

banxia (pinellia; washed seven times in hot water, dried over a fire, 2½ liang)　chaihu (bupleurum; rhizome head removed, half a jin)　renshen (ginseng)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　huangqin (scutellaria; 3 liang each)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take three large *qian* with one and a half *zhan* (cups) of water, five slices of shengjiang (fresh ginger) and one jujube (broken open), and decoct down to seven-tenths; remove the dregs and take it fairly hot, at any time. For children divide it into two doses, adjusting the amount to the child's size.

## Notes

Xiao chaihu tang: used centrally for shaoyang patterns of cold damage and warm-heat disease, and widely for jaundice, relapse after illness, heat entering the blood chamber (fever during menses) and postpartum colds.
- The ingredients are the same seven as Xiao chaihu tang in the *Shanghan lun* (bupleurum, scutellaria, pinellia, ginseng, licorice, fresh ginger, jujube); ginger and jujube are added at decoction. Making a coarse powder and decocting a dose of it (zhusan, "boiled powder") is typical of Song-period formularies.
- "Menstruation has just stopped, with chills and fever like malaria occurring at set times" follows the *Shanghan lun* clause on heat entering the blood chamber (corresponding to clause 144).
- The Siku quanshu edition reads 秘渋 (constipated and rough) for 秘硬 and "one slice" for "five slices" of fresh ginger (see the variants).
