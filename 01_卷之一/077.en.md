# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 077 大防風湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Da fangfeng tang (Major Saposhnikovia Decoction)

It dispels wind and smooths qi, quickens the blood and vessels, strengthens the sinews and bones, removes cold-damp and drives out cold qi. It also treats leg pain with paralysis and weakness after dysentery, with inability to walk, called "dysentery wind"; or both knees swollen, enlarged and painful, the thighs and shins withered and wasted to mere skin and bone, cramped and curled up in bed, unable to bend and stretch, called "crane's-knee wind". Taking it makes qi and blood flow freely, the flesh gradually grows back, and walking naturally returns to normal.

chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage; do not use lovage from Fuzhou)　fuzi (aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed; 1½ liang each)　shu gan dihuang (prepared rehmannia; washed)　baizhu (white atractylodes)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; rhizome head removed)　danggui (Chinese angelica; washed, rhizome head removed, soaked in wine, dried over a fire and stir-fried)　bai shaoyao (white peony root)　huangqi (astragalus)　duzhong (eucommia; coarse bark removed, stir-fried until the threads break; 2 liang each)　qianghuo (notopterygium; rhizome head removed)　renshen (ginseng; rhizome head removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　niuxi (achyranthes; rhizome head removed, soaked in wine, sliced and lightly stir-fried; 1 liang each)

Make the above into a coarse powder. For each dose take 5 *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, seven slices of ginger and one jujube, decoct together down to eight-tenths, remove the dregs and take it warm, on an empty stomach before meals.

## Notes

Da fangfeng tang (bureau additions): a formula that dispels wind-damp while supplementing qi and blood, for leg pain and paralysis after dysentery ("dysentery wind") and for "crane's-knee wind" (swollen knees with wasted thighs and shins); it is still used today, e.g. for rheumatoid arthritis.
- Qi- and blood-supplementing drugs close to Shiquan dabu tang without cinnamon and poria (ginseng, atractylodes, astragalus, licorice, rehmannia, angelica, white peony, lovage) are combined with aconite, saposhnikovia, notopterygium, eucommia and achyranthes.
