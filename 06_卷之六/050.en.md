# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 050 御米湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Yumi tang (Imperial Rice [Poppy] Decoction)

It governs all cases of long-standing dysentery, red or white, with gripping pain of the navel and abdomen, tenesmus with a sense of dragging downward, attacks and remissions at any time, without limit day and night, as well as unceasing bleeding from below and complete inability to eat.

houpo (magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, stir-fried, processed with ginger; 10 liang)　yingsuqiao (poppy husk; roasted with honey)　bai fuling (white poria; skin removed)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried; 5 liang each)　renshen (ginseng; rhizome head removed)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried; 2½ liang each)

Cut the above into pieces. For each dose take 3 *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water, three slices of fresh ginger, three large Huai jujubes and one mume fruit, decoct down to one *zhan*, remove the dregs and take it at a comfortable temperature on an empty stomach before meals. For the aged of seventy or eighty, two large *qian* per dose; for children one and a half *qian*, decocted as above and adjusted further for age.

## Notes

Yumi tang (from here the layer of Baoqing-era additions): for gripping umbilical pain, tenesmus and frequent stools day and night in long-standing red or white dysentery, for rectal bleeding, and for complete loss of appetite.
- Poppy husk ("imperial rice" is another name for the poppy) astringes the intestines; ginseng, poria and licorice supplement; magnolia bark and dried ginger warm and move qi; mume is added.
