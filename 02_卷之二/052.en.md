# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 052 川芎茶調散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Chuanxiong chatiao san (Senkyuchachosan; Sichuan Lovage Powder Taken with Tea)

It treats men and women with all kinds of wind attacking upward, with clouding and heaviness of the head and eyes, one-sided and general headache and nasal congestion with a heavy voice; wind damage with vigorous fever, vexing pain of the limbs, twitching of the flesh and heat in the diaphragm with exuberant phlegm; and women's blood wind attacking and pouring with pain at the temples. Whatever is contracted from wind qi, it treats it all.

bohe leaves (mint; not exposed to fire; 8 liang)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　jingjie (schizonepeta; stalks removed; 4 liang each)　xiangfuzi (cyperus; stir-fried; 8 liang; another version has asarum, rhizome head removed, 1 liang)　fangfeng (bofu; saposhnikovia; rhizome head removed; 1½ liang)　baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica)　qianghuo (notopterygium)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched; 2 liang each)

Make the above medicines into a fine powder. Take 2 *qian* each time after meals mixed in clear tea. Taken regularly it clears the head and eyes.

## Notes

Chuanxiong chatiao san (Wu Zhige's additions; the formula referred to in juan 1, no. 068): for one-sided and general headache and nasal congestion from wind attacking upward, for fever and body pain in colds, and for temple pain in women's blood wind. It is still used today for headache in colds and migraine.
- Mint, lovage, schizonepeta, saposhnikovia, angelica dahurica and notopterygium scatter wind from the head; taken with tea to clear heat above.
- The base text has cyperus, with a note that another version has asarum; the Chuanxiong chatiao san generally used today contains asarum.
- There is a stray "〕" at the end of the note in the base text.
