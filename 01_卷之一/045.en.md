# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之一 045 乳香没薬丸 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Ruxiang moyao wan (Frankincense and Myrrh Pill)

It treats all wind qi in men and women, frees the channels and network vessels and quickens the blood. It treats pain of the sinews and bones, numbness of the hands and feet, hemiplegia, dark wind with spinning head, one-sided and general head wind, minor wind-strike and acute wind, pain of the hands and feet, clenched jaws, and weakness of the four limbs. It is also suitable for wind toxin of the kidneys attacking the head and face above and pouring into the lower back and legs below with sores, and aching pain all over the body.

fuxiong (lovage from Fuzhou; 108 liang)　zhizhuhua (rhododendron flower; stir-fried)　mubie ren (momordica seed kernel)　baijiaoxiang (liquidambar resin; picked clean)　huoxiang (agastache; picked and stir-fried)　bai jiangcan (silkworm; washed and dried over a fire)　wulingzhi (flying-squirrel droppings; picked)　baizhi (angelica dahurica; picked)　danggui (Chinese angelica; 72 liang each)　dilong (earthworm; 144 liang)　heshouwu (fleeceflower root; 244 liang)　weilingxian (clematis root; washed; 222 liang)　caowutou (wild aconite; stir-fried; 648 liang)

Powder the above and form pills the size of wutong seeds with vinegar paste. Take five pills each time—more must not be taken—after meals, swallowed with mint tea; warm wine is also fine. Pregnant women must not take it.

## Notes

Ruxiang moyao wan (Shaoxing-era additions): for pain of the sinews and bones, numbness, hemiplegia and headache from wind qi, and for wind toxin of the kidneys.
- Despite the name, frankincense and myrrh do not appear in the list of ingredients in the base text. It contains a very large amount of wild aconite and other toxic drugs (rhododendron flower, momordica seed), hence the limit of five pills and the prohibition in pregnancy.
- The Siku quanshu edition differs in processing and doses (see the variants).
