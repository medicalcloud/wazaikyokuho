# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 010 嘉禾散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jiahe san (Fine Grain Powder)

(Also called Gushen san, Grain Spirit Powder.) It treats fullness in the middle with deficiency below, the five chokings and five diaphragm disorders, disharmony of the spleen and stomach, stuffiness and oppression of the chest and diaphragm, distension and fullness of the flanks and ribs, stabbing pain of the heart and abdomen, no desire for food, or much phlegm counterflow, a bitter mouth and sour tongue, chest fullness with shortness of breath, weary limbs and a withered yellow complexion. It is especially suitable when the middle jiao is deficient and blocked and cannot bear attack, when the visceral qi is deficient and cold and cannot accept drastic supplementation, when illness has weakened the qi so that eating does not return to normal, or for those born weak who cannot eat much. Taken regularly it nurtures the spirit and nourishes qi, harmonizes and supplements the spleen and stomach, and makes eating better.

pipaye (loquat leaf; hairs removed, smeared all over with ginger juice and roasted until fragrant and cooked)　yiyiren (yokuinin; coix seed; lightly stir-fried)　bai fuling (bukuryo; white poria; skin removed)　renshen (ninjin; ginseng; rhizome head removed)　suosharen (amomum; skin removed; 1 liang each)　dafuzi (areca seed; lightly stir-fried)　suifengzi (if lacking, toosendan fruit or chebule will do)　duzhong (tochu; eucommia; bark removed, smeared with ginger juice and wine mixed, roasted until fragrant, cooked and slightly scorched)　shihu (sekkoku; dendrobium; finely chopped, mixed with wine, lightly stir-fried)　huoxiang leaves (agastache)　muxiang (mokko; costus root)　chenxiang (aquilaria)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed; 3 *fen* each)　gunie (rice sprouts; lightly stir-fried)　binglang (areca; stir-fried)　dingxiang (clove)　wuweizi (gomishi; schisandra; lightly stir-fried)　bai doukou (white cardamom; lightly stir-fried, skin removed)　qingpi (green tangerine peel; pulp removed)　sangbaipi (mulberry root bark; lightly stir-fried; half a liang each)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes; stir-fried; 2 liang)　shenqu (medicated leaven; lightly stir-fried)　banxia (hange; pinellia; washed seven times in hot water; slice 1 *fen* of fresh ginger and pound it to a pulp with the pinellia, make cakes and roast them yellow; 1 *fen* each)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried; 1½ liang)

Pound and sieve the above into a powder. For each dose take 2 *qian* with one *zhan* of water, two slices of fresh ginger and three plump jujubes, decoct together down to seven-tenths and take it warm, at any time. It also treats cold damage in any season, regulating yin and yang so that no changes occur and recovery comes on a fixed day. For the five chokings, decoct it with one dried persimmon; effect shows after ten doses. For diaphragm qi with vomiting, emaciation and exhaustion, decoct it with three *cun* of Chinese chive bulb and five jujubes. Women may also take it.

## Notes

Jiahe san (Gushen san): for fullness above with deficiency below, the five chokings and five diaphragm disorders, and chest stuffiness, abdominal pain, poor appetite, acid reflux, lassitude and sallow complexion from spleen-stomach disharmony; especially for deficient blockage that can bear neither attack nor drastic supplementation, and for poor appetite after illness or from birth.
- A balanced 24-ingredient formula: ginseng, poria, atractylodes, licorice and coix supplement the spleen; costus, aquilaria, clove, agastache, amomum, cardamom, tangerine peels, areca and areca seed move qi; medicated leaven and rice sprouts digest food; eucommia, dendrobium and schisandra supplement below; loquat leaf, pinellia and mulberry bark send counterflow down.
- The Siku quanshu edition calls it Jiahe wan (see the variants).
