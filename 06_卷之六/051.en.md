# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之六 051 地楡散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Diyu san (Sanguisorba Powder)

It is suitable for deficient qi of the intestines and stomach with disharmony of cold and heat, unceasing diarrhoea, passing fresh blood, or matter like bean juice or pig's liver, or pus and blood mixed, more red than white, abdominal pain with tenesmus, very frequent stools and complete inability to eat. (Another formula appears later.)

shiliu pi (pomegranate rind)　lianpeng (lotus receptacle; stalk removed)　gancao (licorice; stir-fried)　yingsuqiao (poppy husk; pulp removed, smeared with honey and roasted; equal parts)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take two large *qian* with one and a half *zhan* of water and three slices of fresh ginger, decoct down to one *zhan* and take it at a comfortable temperature, at any time.

## Notes

Diyu san (Baoqing-era additions): for unceasing diarrhoea, fresh blood, stools like bean juice or pig's liver, pus and blood, abdominal pain, tenesmus, frequent stools and loss of appetite from deficient intestinal and stomach qi with disharmony of cold and heat.
- Pomegranate rind, lotus receptacle and poppy husk astringe; licorice is added. Although named after sanguisorba, the formula in the base text contains none. "Another formula appears later" probably refers to a later Diyu san.
