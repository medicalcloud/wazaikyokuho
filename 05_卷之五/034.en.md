# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 034 三仙丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Sanxian dan (Three Immortals Elixir)

(Also called Changshou wan, Longevity Pill.)

It treats deficiency cold of the kidney meridian with damaged, weak original qi, a declining spirit and timid strength, dim eyes and deafness. Taken regularly it supplements and fills the lower meridians, warms and nourishes the spleen and stomach, strengthens qi and searches out wind, preserves the complexion and quickens the blood, increases the strength of the sinews and blackens the beard and moustache.

chuan wutou (uzu; Sichuan aconite; 1 liang, raw, skin removed, chopped into dice-sized pieces, stir-fried yellow with half a liang of salt, salt removed)　huixiang (fennel; weigh 3 liang net, stir-fried until fragrant throughout)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; 2 liang, soaked overnight in rice-washing water, skin scraped off, chopped, stir-fried yellow with a handful of scallion whites, scallion removed)

Make the above into a fine powder and form pills the size of wutong seeds with flour paste boiled in wine. Take fifty to seventy pills each time on an empty stomach with warm wine or salt water as preferred.

## Notes

Sanxian dan (Changshou wan; Chunyou-era additions): for a weakened spirit, lack of strength, dim vision and deafness from deficiency cold of the kidney meridian and damaged original qi.
- Three ingredients ("three immortals"): Sichuan aconite stir-fried with salt, fennel, and black atractylodes stir-fried with scallion white.
