# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之八 062 五香連翹湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Wuxiang lianqiao tang (Five Aromatics and Forsythia Decoction)

It treats all malign nodes, scrofula, abscesses and flat-abscesses, malign swellings and similar illnesses. (From the *Sanyin fang*.)

chenxiang (aquilaria; not exposed to fire)　ruxiang (frankincense; not exposed to fire, ground)　gancao (licorice; raw)　boshang qing muxiang (imported aristolochia root; not exposed to fire; 1 *fen* each)　lianqiao (forsythia; stalks removed)　shegan (belamcanda)　shengma (cimicifuga)　sangjisheng (mulberry mistletoe; if unavailable, substitute cimicifuga)　duhuo (pubescent angelica; what pharmacies sell nowadays is only old peucedanum or local angelica, unfit for use; using notopterygium instead is excellent)　mutong (akebia; nodes removed; 3 *fen* each)　dingxiang (clove; not exposed to fire; half a liang)　dahuang (rhubarb; steamed; 3 liang)　shexiang (musk; genuine, ground separately; 1½ *qian*)

Cut the above into pieces. For each dose take four large *qian* with two *zhan* of water, boil down to a little over one *zhan*, remove the dregs, take eight-tenths of the clear liquid and drink it hot on an empty stomach. If after half a day or more there is no purging, take another dose, until foul matter is purged. Taking it before new flesh has grown does no harm; it breaks the qi of heat toxin. The original formula includes bamboo sap and refined mirabilite, but these have been omitted lest the inflexible be unable to judge their use; the wise should add or subtract as they see fit.

There are very many formulas called Wuxiang lianqiao tang; that of the *Sanyin fang* should be taken as standard. The *Li shi fang* version is also preserved here.

*Li shi fang* version: frankincense, licorice, costus, aquilaria, forsythia, belamcanda, cimicifuga, akebia, mulberry mistletoe and pubescent angelica, 3 *fen* each, and half a liang of clove; for constipation add 3 *fen* of rhubarb. Li does not use rhubarb because it may be unsuitable for the weak and elderly, so it is added or omitted as the occasion requires.

Another formula: aristolochia root 3 *fen*, mulberry mistletoe 2 *fen*, aquilaria, akebia, raw astragalus and rhubarb (soaked in wine and roasted in embers) 1 liang each, musk 2 *qian*, frankincense, agastache, Sichuan cimicifuga and forsythia half a liang each, clove 3 *fen*. This formula is the same as those of the *Sanyin fang* and the *Li shi fang*, merely adding clove and agastache.

## Notes

Wuxiang lianqiao tang (bureau additions; the last formula of juan 8): for all malign nodes, scrofula, abscesses and malign swellings. Given as the *Sanyin fang* formula, with the original bamboo sap and mirabilite omitted; the *Li shi fang* version (rhubarb added as needed) and another variant (adding clove and agastache) are also recorded.
- Five aromatics (aquilaria, frankincense, aristolochia root, clove, musk) move qi; forsythia, belamcanda and cimicifuga clear heat toxin; pubescent angelica, mistletoe and akebia are added; rhubarb purges. A note says the pubescent angelica sold at the time was inferior, so notopterygium should be used instead.
