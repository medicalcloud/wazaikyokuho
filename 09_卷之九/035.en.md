# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之九 035 大聖散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Dasheng san (Great Sage Powder)

It treats women with a deficient, cold sea of blood and long childlessness; postpartum failed blood surging into the heart; wind-strike with clenched jaw; and a child dead in the abdomen—pry the mouth open and pour in the medicine, and delivery soon follows without harm. It treats miscarriage with attacking, stabbing pain in the abdomen; transverse and breech births; retained placenta; blood dizziness, blood aggregations, blood stagnation and blood flooding; blood entering the limbs; all ailments of the blood viscus; the various kinds of wind qi; cold damage with vomiting, cough and alternating chills and fever; sores over the whole body; headache and nausea; irregular menses; red and white vaginal discharge; malign qi in the breasts; a deficient, cold womb with repeated miscarriage; unsettled flooding becoming illness; and amenorrhoea in unmarried girls. All should take it. Taken regularly it warms the womb, harmonizes blood and qi, gladdens the complexion, drives away wind-cold and dispels the myriad illnesses. It also treats men's five taxations and seven damages, deficiency damage and similar illnesses.

zelan (lycopus; leaves)　shigao (gypsum; ground; 2 liang each)　juanbai (selaginella; roots removed)　bai fuling (white poria; skin removed)　fangfeng (saposhnikovia; rhizome head removed)　houpo (magnolia bark; coarse bark removed, roasted with ginger juice)　xixin (asarum; shoots removed)　baiziren (arborvitae seed; lightly stir-fried)　jiegeng (platycodon)　wuzhuyu (evodia; washed seven times in hot water, dried over a fire, stir-fried; 1 liang each)　wuweizi (schisandra; picked clean)　renshen (ginseng)　gaoben (Chinese lovage; shoots removed)　ganjiang (dried ginger; blast-fried)　chuanjiao (Sichuan pepper; seeds and closed fruits removed, lightly stir-fried to sweat out the oil)　baizhi (angelica dahurica)　baizhu (white atractylodes)　huangqi (astragalus; shoots removed)　chuanwu (Sichuan aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　danshen (salvia root; 3 *fen* each)　wuyi (elm seed paste; lightly stir-fried until red)　gancao (licorice; mix-fried)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　shaoyao (peony root)　danggui (Chinese angelica; 1 liang 3 *fen* each)　baiwei (cynanchum root)　ejiao (donkey-hide gelatin; broken up, stir-fried dry; half a liang each)　rougui (cinnamon bark; 1 liang 1 *fen*)　sheng gan dihuang (dried raw rehmannia; 1½ liang)

Make the above into a fine powder. Take 2 *qian* each time on an empty stomach and at bedtime mixed in hot wine. In acute illness, take it at any time, three times a day.

## Notes

Dasheng san: for infertility from a deficient, cold sea of blood; postpartum failed blood surging into the heart; wind-strike with clenched jaw; dead foetus; abdominal pain after miscarriage; transverse and breech birth; retained placenta; the various blood disorders (dizziness, aggregations, stagnation, flooding); wind qi; vomiting, cough and chills in cold damage; sores; headache; irregular menses; discharge; breast disorders; habitual miscarriage; flooding; and amenorrhoea in girls—also for men's five taxations and seven damages.
- A large formula of twenty-nine ingredients: lycopus, selaginella, salvia and cynanchum move blood; ginseng, astragalus, atractylodes, poria, angelica, lovage, peony, raw rehmannia and donkey-hide gelatin supplement; Sichuan aconite, dried ginger, Sichuan pepper, evodia and cinnamon warm; saposhnikovia, asarum, Chinese lovage and angelica dahurica dispel wind.
