# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之二 047 人参軽骨散 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Renshen qinggu san (Ginseng Bone-Lightening Powder)

It resolves and frees cold damage in any season: headache and vigorous fever, stiffness of the neck and back, vexing pain of the joints, aversion to cold and wind, weary limbs, irregular stools, red, rough urine, vomiting counterflow and vexing thirst; or wind damage and contraction of cold with headache, fever, nasal congestion, a heavy voice and cough with phlegm and drool; and miasmic qi of mountain mists and seasonal pestilence with tidal fever coming and going. It also treats the five taxations and seven damages with qi stagnation of the middle duct, stuffiness and oppression of the heart and abdomen, lodged phlegm with vomiting counterflow, and cold qi rushing and surging, attacking and pouring with stabbing pain. It also treats women's griping pain of blood and qi and irregular menses. It is suitable for all of these.

beimu (baimo; fritillaria; core removed)　bai fuling (bukuryo; white poria; dried over a fire)　banxia (hange; pinellia; boiled; 1 liang each)　zhiqiao (bitter orange; pulp removed, stir-fried; 2½ liang)　cangzhu (black atractylodes; soaked overnight; 6 liang)　renshen (ginseng)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes; dried over a fire)　baizhi (byakushi; angelica dahurica; not exposed to fire)　chenpi (chinpi; aged tangerine peel; white pith removed)　qinjiao (large-leaf gentian)　chi shaoyao (shakuyaku; red peony root; 2 liang each)　chuanxiong (Sichuan lovage)　danggui (toki; Chinese angelica; rhizome head removed, dried over a fire)　rougui (cinnamon bark; coarse bark removed)　ganjiang (kankyo; dried ginger; blast-fried; 1½ liang each)　chaihu (saiko; bupleurum; rhizome head removed)　mahuang (mao; ephedra; roots and nodes removed; 3 liang each)　jiegeng (kikyo; platycodon; rhizome head removed)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; scorched)　houpo (koboku; magnolia bark; 4 liang each, soaked in ginger juice)

Make the above medicines into a fine powder. For each dose take 3 *qian* with one *zhan* of water and three slices of fresh ginger, decoct together down to seven-tenths and take it fairly hot at a comfortable temperature. For weariness of the body add one smoked plum; for cough add two jujubes and decoct together. At any time.

## Notes

Renshen qinggu san (Wu Zhige's additions): for the various symptoms of seasonal cold damage (colds), miasma and epidemics, for qi stagnation, phlegm and cold-qi abdominal pain with the five taxations and seven damages, and for women's blood-qi pain and irregular menses.
- Its composition is close to Wuji san (Goshakusan) (juan 2, no. 008) with ginseng, white atractylodes, fritillaria, gentian and bupleurum added.
