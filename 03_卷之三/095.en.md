# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之三 095 四君子湯 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Si junzi tang (Shikunshito; Four Gentlemen Decoction)

It treats deficiency of the ying-nutrient and wei-defense qi, weakness of the zang-fu organs, distension and fullness of the heart and abdomen, complete lack of desire to eat, borborygmus with diarrhea, and retching and vomiting. It is very suitable to take.

renshen (ninjin; ginseng; rhizome head removed)　gancao (kanzo; licorice; mix-fried)　fuling (bukuryo; poria; skin removed)　baizhu (byakujutsu; white atractylodes; equal parts of each)

Make the above into a fine powder. For each dose take two *qian* with one *zhan* (cup) of water and decoct down to seven-tenths; take it at a comfortable warmth, at any time. It may also be taken with a little salt, stirred into plain hot water. Taken regularly, it warms and harmonizes the spleen and stomach, improves eating and drinking, and wards off pathogenic cold and miasmic mists.

## Notes

Si junzi tang: the basic qi-tonifying formula for spleen-stomach qi deficiency with poor appetite, diarrhea and vomiting.
- Ginseng, licorice, poria and white atractylodes in equal parts. It became the foundation of many later formulas, such as Liu junzi tang (adding tangerine peel and pinellia).
- In this book it belongs to the layer "secret formulas newly added from the bureaus' experience" (a later addition) in the chapter "treating all qi disorders" of juan 3.
- "Stirred into plain hot water" (白湯点) means dissolving the powder in hot water and drinking it.
