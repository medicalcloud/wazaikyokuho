# 太平惠民和劑局方 卷之五 032 接気丹 (English translation)

## Translation of the text

Jieqi dan (Qi-Connecting Elixir)

It treats deficient, exhausted true origin with yin evil alone exuberant and sudden expiry of yang qi, or great vomiting and great diarrhoea, or collapse from long-standing dysentery. For the rest it treats the same conditions as Heixi dan, but this medicine is especially good.

chenxiang (aquilaria; 1 liang)　liuhuang (sulphur; fused into "sand" [with the black lead] as for Heixi dan, cooled and finely ground)　heixi (black lead; weighed after removing the dross; 2 liang each)　niuxi (achyranthes; soaked in wine)　baizhu (white atractylodes; dried over a fire)　congrong (cistanche; soaked in wine; half a liang each)　dingxiang (clove; 3 *qian*)　chuanlianzi (toosendan fruit; stones removed, flesh used)　muxiang (costus root)　huixiang (fennel; stir-fried)　roudoukou (nutmeg; roasted)　poguzhi (psoralea; stir-fried)　guixin (cinnamon heart; coarse bark removed)　fuzi (aconite; blast-fried, skin and navel removed)　huluba (fenugreek; stir-fried)　yangqishi (actinolite; calcined; 1 liang each)

Pound all the above medicines together with 4 liang of the [lead-sulphur] sand into a fine powder, mix evenly, and form pills the size of wutong seeds with a paste of glutinous rice flour boiled in wine. Swallow fifty pills on an empty stomach with warm wine or salt water.

## Notes

Jieqi dan (from here the layer of Chunyou-era additions): for sudden collapse of yang with exhausted true origin and exuberant yin evil, great vomiting and diarrhoea, and collapse from chronic dysentery; its indications are said to be the same as Heixi dan (in the section on chronic cold of juan 5).
- Black lead fused with sulphur rescues yang; aconite, cinnamon heart, fenugreek, psoralea, actinolite and cistanche warm the kidneys; aquilaria, costus, clove, fennel, nutmeg and toosendan move qi. It contains lead and is highly toxic.
